Inflation-Adjusted Savings Formulas

Formulas Used
Nominal Future Value = Current Savings × (1 + Annual Return)^Years

Real Future Value = Nominal Future Value ÷ (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Real Return Rate = ((1 + Nominal Return) / (1 + Inflation Rate)) − 1

Purchasing Power Maintained % = Real Future Value ÷ Current Savings × 100

The nominal future value shows raw account growth. Dividing by the inflation factor converts this to today's purchasing power — the real future value. The real return rate (Fisher equation) gives the true annual growth in buying power. Purchasing power maintained percentage directly answers: "Am I getting richer or poorer in real terms?"

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current savings: The total balance you are starting with today.
  2. Set your annual return rate: High-yield savings accounts earn 4–5%; index funds historically average 7–10%; conservative bonds around 3–5%.
  3. Enter the inflation rate: Use 3% for the US historical average, or your own estimate.
  4. Set the investment period: How many years you plan to let your savings grow.
  5. Click "Calculate Real Returns": Compare the four outputs to understand the true growth of your savings in real purchasing power terms.

Example Scenario

You have $25,000 in savings. You are considering a high-yield savings account at 4.5% versus investing in an index fund at 7%. Inflation is 3%. Over 20 years:

  • High-yield savings (4.5%): Nominal FV = $60,199 | Real FV = $33,338 | Real return = 1.46%/yr | Power maintained: 133%
  • Index fund (7%): Nominal FV = $96,742 | Real FV = $53,574 | Real return = 3.88%/yr | Power maintained: 214%
  • Simple savings (0.5%): Nominal FV = $27,628 | Real FV = $15,303 | Real return = −2.43%/yr | Power maintained: 61% — a real loss!

Only returns above the inflation rate actually grow your purchasing power — and the gap compounds dramatically over 20+ years.

Frequently Asked Questions

The real return rate is your investment return after subtracting inflation. The precise formula is: Real Rate = ((1 + Nominal Rate) / (1 + Inflation Rate)) − 1. If inflation exceeds your return, your real return is negative — meaning your savings are losing purchasing power.

The nominal future value is the raw dollar amount your savings will grow to. The real future value adjusts that figure for inflation, showing what those future dollars will actually be worth in today's purchasing power. The difference represents the erosion caused by inflation.

Purchasing power maintained % is the real future value divided by your current savings. A value above 100% means your savings grew faster than inflation. Below 100% means inflation outpaced your returns and you are poorer in real terms.

Yes — if your savings account earns less than the inflation rate, your money is losing purchasing power in real terms even as the balance grows. This is why keeping large cash savings in low-interest accounts can be financially harmful over long periods, and why investing is recommended for long-term goals.
