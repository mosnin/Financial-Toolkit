Lean FIRE Calculator

Lean FIRE is the path to financial independence through a deliberately frugal retirement lifestyle — lower expenses mean a smaller FI number and a faster timeline to leaving work behind. This calculator computes your Lean FIRE number (25x your lean monthly expenses), the time to reach it, your annual lean retirement income, and how the lean path compares to achieving standard FIRE at a typical spending level.

How Lean FIRE Is Calculated

Formula
Lean Annual Expenses = Lean Monthly Expenses × 12
Lean FIRE Number = Lean Annual Expenses × 25 (4% SWR)

Time to Lean FIRE: solve for n (months) where
PV × (1+r)^n + PMT × [(1+r)^n − 1] / r ≥ Lean FIRE Number

Years Saved = Time to Standard FIRE − Time to Lean FIRE

The Lean FIRE number is simply 25x your annual lean expenses. Because lean expenses are lower than standard retirement spending, the target is smaller — and the timeline to reach it shorter. The comparison with standard FIRE shows how many years of work a frugal lifestyle can eliminate from your path to independence.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter lean monthly expenses: The minimum you could live on comfortably — essentials only, no extras.
  2. Add standard expenses (optional): Your current or preferred higher spending for comparison.
  3. Enter current savings and monthly contributions: All investment accounts combined.
  4. Set your return rate: 7% nominal is standard for a diversified portfolio.
  5. Review the comparison: See exactly how many years the frugal path saves versus standard FIRE.

Example Scenario

Riley has $60,000 saved and contributes $2,500/month. Lean monthly expenses would be $2,800/month ($33,600/year), giving a Lean FIRE number of $840,000. Standard lifestyle would cost $4,500/month ($54,000/year), needing $1,350,000 for standard FIRE. At 7% returns, Riley reaches Lean FIRE in about 14 years 2 months, versus 20 years 5 months for standard FIRE — saving over 6 years by embracing the lean lifestyle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Lean FIRE is retiring early on a minimal budget — typically under $40,000/year. It requires a smaller portfolio (lower expenses × 25) and is achievable faster than standard or Fat FIRE. The trade-off is a frugal, intentional lifestyle with little financial cushion.

Your Lean FIRE number is 25 times your annual lean expenses. At $30,000/year in lean expenses, you need $750,000. At $40,000/year, you need $1,000,000. The lower your expenses, the smaller and more achievable the target.

Lean FIRE has little margin for unexpected costs — a major medical event, home repair, or unexpected inflation can stress a tight budget. Many Lean FIRE practitioners maintain some part-time income flexibility or hold a small cash buffer to manage these risks.

Lean FIRE needs a smaller portfolio and is reached faster. Standard FIRE targets typical middle-class spending ($50,000–$80,000/year). Fat FIRE targets luxury spending ($100,000+/year). Each requires progressively larger portfolios and longer timelines.
