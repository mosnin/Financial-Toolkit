How Renovation Costs Are Estimated

Renovation costs are estimated using industry-standard cost-per-square-foot ranges that vary by project type and quality level:

Cost Estimation Formula
Estimated Cost = Square Footage x Cost per Sq Ft (based on project type and quality)

The calculator applies low and high cost-per-square-foot multipliers for each combination of project type and quality level, giving you a realistic range rather than a single number.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select the project type - choose the kind of renovation you are planning.
  2. Enter the square footage - the area of the space being renovated.
  3. Choose a quality level - basic for budget-friendly finishes, mid-range for standard upgrades, or high-end for premium materials and craftsmanship.
  4. Click "Calculate Cost" to see your estimated cost range.

Example Scenario

Suppose you want to remodel a 200-square-foot kitchen at a mid-range quality level. The calculator estimates a cost range of approximately $24,000 to $30,000, with an average cost of about $135 per square foot. This includes materials, labor, and standard fixtures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Home renovation costs typically range from $15-$60 per square foot for basic updates, $60-$150 for mid-range remodels, and $150-$400+ for high-end renovations. Costs vary significantly by project type, materials, and location.

Kitchen and bathroom remodels typically offer the best return on investment, followed by adding usable square footage, updating flooring, and improving curb appeal. Minor kitchen remodels can recoup 70-80% of costs at resale.

Timeline varies by scope: minor updates take 1-4 weeks, mid-range remodels 1-3 months, and major renovations 3-9 months or more. Permitting, material lead times, and contractor availability all affect the schedule.

Yes, it is recommended to add a 10-20% contingency buffer to your renovation budget. Unexpected issues like hidden water damage, outdated wiring, or structural problems are common, especially in older homes.

Renovating is usually cheaper than building new, with costs averaging 20-50% less. However, if more than 50% of the structure needs work, building new may be more cost-effective in the long run.
