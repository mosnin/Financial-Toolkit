Inflation-Adjusted Retirement Calculator

Most retirement calculators use today's dollars — but the retirement income you need will be much larger in future dollars due to inflation. This calculator takes your desired retirement lifestyle in today's dollars, adjusts it for inflation over your working years, calculates the inflation-adjusted income you will actually need at retirement, and shows the portfolio size required to sustain that income along with the shortfall if you plan without inflation in mind.

Inflation-Adjusted Retirement Formula

Formulas Used
Inflation-Adjusted Income = Desired Income × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years to Retirement

Required Portfolio = Inflation-Adjusted Income ÷ Portfolio Return Rate

Nominal Portfolio (no inflation adj.) = Desired Income ÷ Portfolio Return Rate

Shortfall = Required Portfolio − Nominal Portfolio

The adjusted income formula shows what your desired lifestyle will actually cost in future dollars. Dividing by the portfolio return rate (a simplified perpetuity formula) gives the portfolio size needed to sustain that income indefinitely. The shortfall is the additional portfolio you need beyond a naive non-inflation-adjusted plan.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter desired retirement income: In today's dollars — the annual income you want to live on in retirement at today's prices.
  2. Set years to retirement: How many years until you plan to retire.
  3. Enter expected inflation rate: 3% is the US long-run average; use 2% for optimistic planning or 4% for conservative.
  4. Set portfolio return rate: Your expected annual return from your retirement portfolio during retirement (not the accumulation phase).
  5. Review the results: The shortfall figure is especially important — it shows how much more you need compared to a plan that ignores inflation.

Example Scenario

You want $70,000/year in retirement (today's dollars), you are 30 years from retirement, inflation is 3%, and your portfolio earns 6% in retirement:

  • Inflation-adjusted income needed: $70,000 × (1.03)^30 = $169,960/year
  • Required portfolio (inflation-adjusted): $169,960 / 0.06 = $2,832,667
  • Nominal portfolio (no inflation adj.): $70,000 / 0.06 = $1,166,667
  • Shortfall from ignoring inflation: $1,666,000 — over $1.6 million gap!

This massive shortfall shows why inflation planning is not optional for retirement — it can be the difference between a comfortable retirement and running out of money.

Frequently Asked Questions

Inflation means that the same retirement income will buy less every year. If you need $60,000/year today and inflation averages 3%, you will need $80,635/year in 10 years and $108,183/year in 20 years to maintain the same lifestyle. Planning without inflation leads to severely underfunded retirement.

The 4% rule states that you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio per year and have a very high probability of your money lasting 30 years. To find the required portfolio, divide your desired annual income by 0.04 (or multiply by 25). This calculator uses your custom portfolio return rate for more precision.

The shortfall is the difference between the portfolio needed to fund inflation-adjusted retirement income versus the portfolio needed to fund today's nominal retirement income. This gap can be hundreds of thousands — or even millions — of dollars over a long retirement horizon.

A common assumption is 5–7% for a balanced portfolio of stocks and bonds during retirement. Conservative planners use 4–5%; more equity-heavy portfolios might use 6–8%. Using a realistic rate that accounts for a conservative retirement allocation gives the most accurate target.
