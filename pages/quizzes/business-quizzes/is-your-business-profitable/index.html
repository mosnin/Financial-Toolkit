Is Your Business Profitable?

Profitability is more than just having revenue — it means your business keeps more than it spends. This 7-question quiz examines your gross margin, net margin trend, cost of goods awareness, and owner compensation habits to give you an honest picture of your business's financial health.

Frequently Asked Questions

A net profit margin of 10% or higher is generally considered healthy for most small businesses. Margins vary widely by industry — retail averages 2–5%, while service businesses can achieve 15–30%. The key is tracking whether your margin is improving over time.

Gross profit is revenue minus the direct cost of goods sold (COGS) — what it costs to produce your product or deliver your service. Net profit subtracts all operating expenses, taxes, and interest from gross profit. A business can have strong gross margins but poor net margins if overhead is too high.

For sole proprietors and partnerships, owner draws are not technically expenses — they come from profit. However, to assess true profitability, you should assign yourself a market-rate salary as an expense. If the business only "profits" because you're not paying yourself, it's not genuinely profitable.
