ROI Formulas

ROI (Return on Investment)
ROI = ((Final Value - Initial Investment) / Initial Investment) x 100
Annualized ROI
Annualized ROI = ((Final Value / Initial Investment)^(1/Years) - 1) x 100
Net Profit
Net Profit = Final Value - Initial Investment

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your initial investment -- the total amount you invested at the start.
  2. Enter the final value -- the current value or the amount you received when you sold.
  3. Enter the time period -- how many years you held the investment (can include decimals, e.g., 2.5 years).
  4. Click "Calculate ROI" to see your total ROI, annualized ROI, and net profit.

Example Scenario

You invested $10,000 in a stock portfolio that grew to $15,000 over 3 years.

  • Net Profit: $15,000 - $10,000 = $5,000
  • ROI: ($5,000 / $10,000) x 100 = 50%
  • Annualized ROI: (($15,000 / $10,000)^(1/3) - 1) x 100 = 14.47%

While the total return was 50%, the annualized rate of 14.47% lets you compare this to other investments of different durations on an apples-to-apples basis.

Frequently Asked Questions

ROI (Return on Investment) measures the gain or loss generated on an investment relative to its cost. It is expressed as a percentage: ROI = (Net Profit / Initial Investment) x 100.

Annualized ROI adjusts the return to reflect a per-year rate, making it easier to compare investments held for different time periods. It uses the formula: ((1 + ROI)^(1/years) - 1) x 100.

A good ROI depends on the type of investment and risk involved. The S&P 500 has historically returned about 10% annually. Real estate typically yields 8-12%. Any ROI above the risk-free rate and inflation is generally considered acceptable.
