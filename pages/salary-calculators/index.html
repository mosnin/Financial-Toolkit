Frequently Asked Questions

Take-home pay is your gross salary minus federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and any pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, and HSA contributions.

Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then multiply by 52 (weeks per year). For example, $25/hour at 40 hours/week equals $52,000 per year ($25 x 40 x 52).

The average annual raise is 3-5%. A promotion typically comes with a 10-20% increase. Cost-of-living adjustments usually range from 2-4%. Any raise above inflation (currently around 3%) represents a real increase in purchasing power.

Start with the equivalent full-time salary, add 25-35% for self-employment taxes, benefits, and overhead, then divide by billable hours (typically 1,000-1,500 per year). A $75,000 salary equivalent translates to roughly $65-$100/hour as a freelancer.

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must receive 1.5 times their regular hourly rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Some states and employers offer double-time for hours beyond a certain threshold or for holiday work.
