Pricing Formulas

Selling Price (from margin)
Selling Price = Total Cost / (1 - Desired Margin / 100)
Markup Percentage
Markup % = ((Selling Price - Total Cost) / Total Cost) x 100
Profit Per Unit
Profit = Selling Price - Total Cost

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the cost of goods per unit (materials, labor, manufacturing).
  2. Set your desired profit margin as a percentage of the selling price.
  3. Optionally add overhead allocation per unit to cover indirect costs.
  4. Optionally enter a competitor price to see how your pricing compares.
  5. Click Calculate Price to see the recommended selling price, markup, and profit.

Example Scenario

A candle maker has these costs per unit:

  • Cost of Goods: $8.00 (wax, wick, fragrance, jar)
  • Overhead: $2.00 (portion of rent, utilities, packaging)
  • Desired Margin: 60%

Calculation:

  • Total Cost: $8.00 + $2.00 = $10.00
  • Selling Price: $10.00 / (1 - 0.60) = $25.00
  • Profit Per Unit: $25.00 - $10.00 = $15.00
  • Markup: ($25.00 - $10.00) / $10.00 x 100 = 150%

Frequently Asked Questions

Margin is the percentage of the selling price that is profit (profit / price x 100). Markup is the percentage added to cost (profit / cost x 100). A 50% markup results in a 33.3% margin.

Divide your total cost by (1 - desired margin as a decimal). For example, if your cost is $60 and you want a 40% margin: $60 / (1 - 0.40) = $100 selling price.

Overhead allocation distributes indirect business costs (rent, utilities, admin) to each product unit. It ensures your pricing covers not just direct costs but also the cost of running your business.

Use competitor pricing as a reference point, not a ceiling. If your calculated price is higher, identify what value justifies the premium. If lower, you may have room to increase margins.

Profit margins vary widely by industry. Retail typically sees 2-5%, software 70-90%, consulting 15-25%, and manufacturing 5-15%. Research your specific industry benchmarks.
