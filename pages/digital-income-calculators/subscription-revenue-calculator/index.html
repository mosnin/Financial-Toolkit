Subscription Revenue Formulas

MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
MRR = Number of Subscribers x Monthly Price
ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)
ARR = MRR x 12
Churn Impact
Monthly Churn Loss = MRR x (Churn Rate / 100)
Net New MRR
Net New MRR = (New Subscribers Gained x Price) - (Churned Subscribers x Price)
Month N Subscribers (Projection)
Subscribers(N) = Subscribers(N-1) x (1 + Growth Rate - Churn Rate)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current number of subscribers (active paying members).
  2. Set your monthly subscription price per subscriber.
  3. Enter your monthly churn rate — the percentage who cancel each month. Check your payment platform analytics for this figure.
  4. Set your monthly growth rate — how fast you are adding new subscribers as a percentage of your current base.
  5. Click Calculate Revenue to see MRR, ARR, churn impact, net new MRR, and a 12-month projection table.

Example Scenario

A newsletter creator has 800 paid subscribers at $15/month, 4% monthly churn, and 7% monthly new subscriber growth.

  • Current MRR: 800 x $15 = $12,000
  • ARR: $12,000 x 12 = $144,000
  • Monthly Churn Loss: $12,000 x 4% = $480
  • New Subscriber MRR: 800 x 7% x $15 = $840 new MRR
  • Net New MRR: $840 - $480 = $360/month
  • Month 12 Projected MRR: approximately $15,420

Frequently Asked Questions

MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue) is calculated by multiplying the number of active subscribers by the monthly subscription price. It represents predictable monthly income and is the most fundamental metric for subscription businesses.

For consumer subscriptions, monthly churn below 5% is considered acceptable, while below 2% is strong. For B2B SaaS, monthly churn below 1% is ideal. High churn above 8% monthly is a serious warning sign requiring immediate attention.

Net New MRR is the net change in MRR after accounting for both new subscriber revenue gained and churned subscriber revenue lost. A positive Net New MRR means the business is growing; negative means it is contracting.

Monthly growth compounds by applying the growth rate each month to the previous month's subscriber count minus churned subscribers. Even small monthly growth rates compound significantly — 5% monthly growth yields roughly 80% annual growth.
