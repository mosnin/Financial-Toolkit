Frequently Asked Questions

Saving $1,000 per month at a 7% average annual return takes approximately 30 years to reach $1 million. At $2,000 per month it drops to roughly 22 years, and at $3,000 per month about 18 years. Starting earlier makes a profound difference because of compounding. Use our Millionaire Timeline Calculator to enter your exact savings rate and see your personalized timeline.

The conventional guideline is to save at least 15–20% of gross income for retirement. Aggressive wealth builders in the FIRE community target 40–70% savings rates to reach financial independence in 10–15 years. Even increasing your savings rate from 10% to 15% can shave years off your wealth-building timeline due to compound growth.

Compound growth means earning returns on your returns, not just on your original principal. Over long periods this creates an exponential curve where wealth growth accelerates over time. $10,000 growing at 8% per year becomes $21,589 in 10 years, $46,610 in 20 years, and $100,627 in 30 years — all without adding another dollar. This is why starting early is the single most powerful wealth-building strategy.

Net worth equals total assets minus total liabilities. Assets include savings accounts, investments, retirement accounts, real estate equity, and personal property. Liabilities include mortgage balances, student loans, car loans, and credit card debt. Tracking net worth monthly provides a more accurate measure of financial progress than income or spending alone.

Yes, significantly. Research consistently shows that asset allocation explains the majority of long-term portfolio return differences. A 100% stock portfolio has historically returned about 10% annually versus roughly 6% for a 60/40 stock-bond mix. Over 30 years on a $500,000 portfolio, that difference compounds to over $3 million.
