Paycheck Calculation Formula

Your net pay is determined by subtracting all tax withholdings from your gross pay:

Net Pay Formula
Net Pay = Gross Pay - Federal Tax - State Tax - FICA

Where Federal Tax is based on progressive brackets after allowances, State Tax is applied at your state's rate, and FICA is 7.65% (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare).

How to Use This Paycheck Calculator

  1. Enter your gross pay - the total amount you earn per pay period before any deductions.
  2. Select your pay frequency - how often you receive a paycheck (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly).
  3. Choose your filing status - single, married filing jointly, or head of household.
  4. Enter your allowances - the number of federal withholding allowances you claim.
  5. Enter your state tax rate - your state income tax rate (enter 0 for no-income-tax states).
  6. Click "Calculate Paycheck" to see your net pay and full deduction breakdown.

Example Scenario

Suppose you earn $5,000 gross pay biweekly, file as single with 1 allowance, and live in a state with a 5% income tax rate. Your estimated federal tax would be approximately $654, state tax $250, and FICA $382.50. Your net take-home pay would be approximately $3,713.50 per paycheck.

Frequently Asked Questions

Federal income tax is calculated using progressive tax brackets based on your filing status and taxable income after deductions and allowances. Each portion of your income is taxed at the corresponding bracket rate.

FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It includes Social Security tax (6.2% up to the wage base limit) and Medicare tax (1.45% with no limit). The combined FICA rate is 7.65% for most employees.

No. Nine states have no state income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Residents of these states keep more of their gross pay.

Each allowance reduces your taxable income by a set amount, which lowers the federal tax withheld from your paycheck. More allowances mean less tax withheld and higher take-home pay, but you may owe taxes at filing time.

Gross pay is your total earnings before any deductions. Net pay (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after federal tax, state tax, FICA, and any other deductions are subtracted from your gross pay.
