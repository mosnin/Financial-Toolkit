How the Comparison Works

1099 Net Income
Net SE Income = Contract Rate − Business Expenses
SE Tax = Net SE Income × 0.9235 × 15.3%
Deductible SE Tax = SE Tax ÷ 2
AGI = Net SE Income − Deductible SE Tax
Federal Income Tax = Based on AGI and filing status brackets
1099 Net = Contract Rate − SE Tax − Federal Tax
W2 Total Compensation Value
W2 Total = Salary + Benefits Value
FICA (employee share) = Salary × 7.65%
W2 Net = Salary − FICA − Federal Income Tax + Benefits Value
Break-Even 1099 Rate
The minimum 1099 contract rate needed to equal W2 total compensation after all taxes.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual 1099 contract rate — the gross amount you'd earn as a contractor.
  2. Enter any business expenses you could deduct (home office, equipment, subscriptions).
  3. Enter the comparable W2 salary you're evaluating.
  4. Enter the benefits value — health insurance, 401(k) match, paid time off, etc.
  5. Select your filing status and click Compare Income.

Example Scenario

A software developer is weighing a $130,000 1099 contract against a $100,000 W2 offer with $18,000 in benefits (health, 401k match, PTO value).

  • 1099 SE Tax: $130,000 × 0.9235 × 15.3% ≈ $18,373
  • 1099 Net (approx. after all taxes): ~$88,500
  • W2 Total Comp: $100,000 + $18,000 = $118,000; Net after FICA + income tax: ~$91,000
  • Break-Even 1099 Rate: approximately $138,000
  • Recommendation: The W2 offer is slightly better unless the contractor can exceed the break-even rate.
