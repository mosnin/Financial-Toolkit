How the Downsizing Savings Formula Works

Formulas
Monthly Savings = (Current Housing + Utilities + Maintenance) − (New Housing + Utilities + Maintenance)
Annual Savings = Monthly Savings × 12
Cumulative Savings (N years) = Monthly Savings × 12 × N
Net Savings = Cumulative Savings − Moving Cost
Break-even Months = Moving Cost / Monthly Savings

The calculator compares total all-in monthly housing costs (housing payment, utilities, and maintenance) before and after downsizing. Moving costs are subtracted from cumulative savings to show the true net financial position. The break-even point tells you exactly when downsizing starts paying off.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter current costs: Include your current mortgage or rent, average monthly utilities, and any maintenance costs or HOA fees.
  2. Enter new home costs: Research a realistic downsized home and estimate the new monthly costs across all three categories.
  3. Add moving expenses: Include hiring movers, storage, deposits, and any renovation costs at the new property.
  4. Set your time horizon: The longer you stay, the more the savings accumulate, making downsizing increasingly attractive.

Example Scenario

Chris and Sam own a 4-bedroom home and are considering downsizing to a 2-bedroom condo:

  • Current costs: Mortgage $2,200 + utilities $320 + maintenance $250 = $2,770/month
  • Downsized costs: Mortgage $1,400 + utilities $180 + HOA $120 = $1,700/month
  • Monthly savings: $1,070
  • Moving costs: $6,000 (movers + deposits)
  • Break-even: 5.6 months
  • 5-year net savings: $1,070 × 60 − $6,000 = $58,200

Frequently Asked Questions

When downsizing, consider the difference in monthly housing costs (mortgage or rent), utility savings (smaller space means lower heating and cooling), maintenance cost reductions, and one-time moving costs.
