Efficiency Score Formula

Scoring Methodology
Base Score from Energy Cost/Sq Ft: <$1.00/sqft = 10 pts | $1-$1.50 = 8 pts | $1.50-$2 = 6 pts | $2-$2.50 = 4 pts | >$2.50 = 2 pts Insulation Adjustment: Poor −2 | Average 0 | Good +1 | Excellent +2 HVAC Age Adjustment: <5yr +1 | 5-10yr 0 | 10-15yr −0.5 | >15yr −1 Window Adjustment: Single −1 | Double 0 | Triple +1 Total Score = Clamped between 1 and 10 Potential Savings = Annual Bill × Improvement Potential %

The efficiency score is a relative estimate based on entered inputs. Professional energy audits (available through utilities and RESNET-certified auditors) provide more accurate assessments.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual energy bill -- add your electric and gas bills for the full year.
  2. Enter home size -- the heated and cooled square footage of your home.
  3. Select insulation level -- choose the option that best describes your home's insulation.
  4. Enter HVAC age -- the age of your primary heating and cooling system in years.
  5. Select window type -- the type of windows in most of your home.
  6. Click "Calculate Efficiency Score" to see your score and upgrade savings estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions

A home energy efficiency score estimates how efficiently your home uses energy on a scale of 1-10 (10 being most efficient). It considers factors like insulation quality, HVAC age, window efficiency, and home size relative to energy use.

Air sealing and adding insulation typically offer the best ROI, with payback periods of 2-5 years and lifetime savings of 10-20% on heating and cooling costs. HVAC upgrades can save 20-30% but have higher upfront costs.

For central air conditioners, look for a SEER2 rating of 15 or higher. For gas furnaces, look for 95%+ AFUE. For heat pumps, look for HSPF2 of 7.5+. High-efficiency models qualify for federal tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act.

Yes. The Inflation Reduction Act provides federal tax credits of up to 30% for qualified energy efficiency improvements including insulation, windows, doors, and heat pumps, capped at $1,200-$2,000 per year depending on the category. Many states and utilities also offer additional rebate programs.
