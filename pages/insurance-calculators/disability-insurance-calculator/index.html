Disability Insurance Formula

Disability insurance needs are calculated by identifying the gap between your expenses and existing coverage:

Coverage Gap Formula
Coverage Gap = Monthly Expenses - Existing Disability Coverage
Recommended Benefit
Recommended Benefit = max(Coverage Gap, 65% of Gross Income - Existing Coverage)

Most disability policies replace 60-70% of gross income. The recommended benefit is the larger of your expense-based gap or the standard 65% income replacement minus existing coverage.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly gross income - your total monthly earnings before taxes.
  2. Enter existing disability coverage - any employer or personal disability benefits.
  3. Enter essential monthly expenses - housing, food, utilities, insurance, debt payments.
  4. Click "Calculate Coverage" to see your coverage gap and recommended benefit.

Example Scenario

An individual earning $6,000 per month with $1,500 in existing employer disability coverage and $4,500 in monthly expenses has a coverage gap of $3,000. The recommended additional disability benefit would be $3,000 per month to fully cover essential expenses during a disability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most experts recommend disability coverage that replaces 60-70% of your gross income. The coverage gap is the difference between your monthly expenses and any existing disability benefits.

Short-term disability covers a few weeks to six months. Long-term disability kicks in after short-term ends and can last years or until retirement age. Long-term disability is generally more important.

Many employers offer group disability insurance covering 50-60% of base salary. Check with your HR department. Employer coverage may not be enough and typically ends when you leave.

More than 1 in 4 of today's 20-year-olds will become disabled before reaching retirement age, according to the Social Security Administration. Disability is far more common than most people realize.
