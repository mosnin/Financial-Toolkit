Social Security Benefit Formula

The SSA uses a three-step formula to calculate your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) at full retirement age:

PIA Bend Point Formula (2024)
PIA = 90% of first $1,174 AIME + 32% of AIME $1,174-$7,078 + 15% of AIME over $7,078

Your AIME (Average Indexed Monthly Earnings) is calculated from your highest 35 years of earnings. Benefits are then adjusted based on claiming age: reduced for early claiming (before FRA) and increased 8% per year for delayed claiming (after FRA up to 70).

Example Scenario

A person born in 1965 with average annual earnings of $65,000 would have a PIA of approximately $2,200 per month at full retirement age (67). Claiming at 62 would reduce this to roughly $1,540 per month, while waiting until 70 would increase it to about $2,728 per month. Over a lifetime to age 85, the total benefits received at each claiming age differ significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Benefits are based on your highest 35 years of earnings, adjusted for inflation. The SSA calculates your Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME) and applies a formula with bend points to determine your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA), which is your benefit at full retirement age.

Claiming at 62 reduces your benefit by about 30% compared to full retirement age (67 for those born after 1960). Waiting until 70 increases your benefit by 24% above your full retirement age amount through delayed retirement credits of 8% per year.

Full retirement age (FRA) is when you can receive your full Social Security benefit. For those born in 1960 or later, FRA is 67. For those born between 1943 and 1959, it ranges from 66 to 66 and 10 months.

It depends on your health, financial needs, and life expectancy. Claiming early gives you income sooner but at a permanently reduced rate. If you expect to live past your late 70s, delaying benefits typically provides more total lifetime income.

Up to 85% of your Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your combined income. If your combined income exceeds $25,000 for individuals or $32,000 for couples, some portion of benefits becomes taxable.
