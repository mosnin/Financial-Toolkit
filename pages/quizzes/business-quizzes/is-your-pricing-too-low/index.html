Is Your Pricing Too Low?

Underpricing is one of the most costly and common mistakes small business owners make. It erodes margins, attracts the wrong clients, and signals low value. This 7-question quiz analyses your current pricing strategy against key financial and market indicators to tell you exactly where you stand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most service businesses should target a gross margin of 50–70% and a net margin of 15–30%. Product businesses vary more widely. If your gross margin is below 30%, you likely have insufficient room to cover operating expenses and still profit. Use this as a signal to review your pricing or reduce COGS.

Research competitor pricing through their websites, sales calls, or platforms like Clutch, Upwork, or industry associations. If your close rate is very high (above 80%), that's often a sign your prices are too low — clients expect some negotiation or comparison shopping. Very high demand with very thin margins is the classic underpricing signal.

The most effective approach is to raise prices for new clients first, then grandfather existing clients in with advance notice. Lead with the value you deliver, not just the number. A 10–20% increase typically results in less than 10% client loss — meaning your revenue actually increases even if a few clients leave.
