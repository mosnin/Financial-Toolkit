Rent Projection Formula

Formulas Used
Projected Monthly Rent (Year N) = Current Rent × (1 + Rent Inflation Rate)^N

Annual Rent (Year N) = Projected Monthly Rent × 12

Total Rent Paid = Σ (Annual Rent for Year 1 through Year N)

Monthly Increase = Projected Rent − Current Rent

Each year's rent is compounded on the previous year, meaning increases accelerate in dollar terms over time. The total rent paid sums every year's annual cost — a figure that often surprises renters who have not calculated it explicitly. Understanding this total helps renters make informed buy versus rent decisions.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current monthly rent: The rent you pay right now.
  2. Set the local rent inflation rate: Check your city's historical rent increases — national average is 4–5%; hot markets can be 6–10%.
  3. Choose the projection period: Up to 30 years. 10 years is a useful planning horizon for most renters.
  4. Optionally enter a home price: If you are considering buying, enter a representative home price to see a 5-year cost comparison versus renting.
  5. Review the milestones table: See rent at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, annual costs, and running total.

Example Scenario

You currently pay $1,800/month in rent. Local rents are rising at 5% per year. Over 10 years:

  • Year 1: $1,890/month ($22,680/yr)
  • Year 3: $2,083/month ($24,996/yr)
  • Year 5: $2,297/month ($27,565/yr)
  • Year 10: $2,932/month ($35,185/yr)
  • Total rent paid over 10 years: ~$289,000 — nearly $300K with no equity built

Seeing the 10-year cumulative rent total often motivates renters to explore homeownership or to negotiate better lease terms with their landlord.

Frequently Asked Questions

Historically, US rent has increased about 3–5% per year on average, though this varies greatly by city and time period. Major metropolitan areas like New York, San Francisco, and Austin have seen 6–10% annual increases in hot markets. The national average over the past decade is approximately 4–5% annually.

Projected Rent = Current Monthly Rent × (1 + Annual Rent Increase Rate)^Years. For example, $1,500/month rent with 5% annual increases becomes $1,914/month in 5 years and $2,443/month in 10 years — a 63% increase over a decade.

Buying can lock in a fixed mortgage payment and protect against rent inflation, but it also comes with costs like maintenance, property taxes, and opportunity cost of a down payment. The decision depends on your local market, how long you plan to stay, your financial situation, and current mortgage rates versus rent levels.

Total rent paid is the sum of all monthly rent payments over the period, where each year's rent is higher than the previous due to the annual increase rate. It is calculated by summing each year's annual rent (monthly × 12) from year 1 through the end of the period.
