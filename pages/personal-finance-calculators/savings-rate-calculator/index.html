Savings Rate Calculator

Your savings rate — the percentage of income you save every month — is the single most powerful variable in your financial future. This calculator adds up savings across all account types (401k, IRA, taxable, and other), calculates your total savings rate, compares it to key benchmarks, and projects what your savings could grow to in 10, 20, and 30 years using compound interest.

How Savings Rate Is Calculated

Formula
Total Monthly Savings = 401k + IRA + Taxable + Other
Savings Rate = (Total Monthly Savings / Gross Monthly Income) × 100
Projected Value = PMT × [((1 + r)^n − 1) / r] × (1 + r)

The projection uses future value of annuity formula with monthly compounding. It shows the potential value of your current savings rate assuming consistent contributions and a constant rate of return. In reality, returns vary year-to-year — the projection is a long-run estimate based on historical average returns.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly gross income: Your total earnings before taxes and deductions.
  2. Add monthly savings by account: Include all intentional savings — 401k, IRA, brokerage, and cash savings.
  3. Set expected annual return: 7% is a common assumption based on historical market averages; use 5-6% to be conservative.
  4. Calculate: See your savings rate, benchmark comparison, and 30-year projection.
  5. Identify gaps: If your rate is below 15%, look for ways to increase contributions by 1-2% at a time.

Example Scenario

Dana earns $7,000/month gross and saves: 401k $700, IRA $583, Taxable $300, HYSA $200. Total savings: $1,783/month. Savings rate: 25.5% — above the 20% recommendation. At 7% annual return, Dana's $1,783/month grows to approximately $296,000 in 10 years, $953,000 in 20 years, and $2.27 million in 30 years. Increasing to 30% ($2,100/month) would project to $2.67M in 30 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Financial advisors recommend 15-20% of gross income. The 50/30/20 rule targets 20%. FIRE adherents aim for 30-50%+. Starting at 10% and increasing gradually is far better than saving nothing.

Many planners include the employer match since it is part of total compensation being saved. Others track personal contributions only. Either approach works — be consistent so you can compare over time.

All intentional savings: 401k, IRA, brokerage investments, HSA, HYSA deposits, and extra debt principal payments. Minimum debt payments do not count since they are servicing existing debt, not building new wealth.

At 10% savings rate: ~40+ years to retirement. At 20%: ~35 years. At 30%: ~28 years. At 50%: ~17 years. Higher rates build wealth faster AND mean you need less to retire since you are used to living on less.
