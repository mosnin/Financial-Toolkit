How Payroll Taxes Are Calculated

Step 1: Annualize wages
Annual Wages = Gross Per Period × Pay Periods Per Year
Step 2: Federal withholding (annualized method)
Taxable Annual = Annual Wages − Pre-Tax Deductions − Standard Withholding Allowance
Apply annual brackets, divide by pay periods
Step 3: FICA taxes
Social Security = min(Gross, SS Wage Base/Periods) × 6.2%
Medicare = Gross × 1.45%
Step 4: Net Pay
Net = Gross − Federal Withholding − State Withholding − SS − Medicare − Pre-Tax Deductions

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your gross wages per pay period (before any deductions).
  2. Select your pay frequency.
  3. Select your W4 filing status.
  4. Enter any pre-tax deductions per period (401k, health insurance, HSA).
  5. Enter your state tax rate as a percentage (use 0 for no state income tax).
  6. Click Calculate Net Pay.

Example Scenario

A bi-weekly employee earns $3,500 per paycheck (single, no W4 adjustments), $200 pre-tax 401k, 5% state tax:

  • Annual Gross: $3,500 × 26 = $91,000
  • Federal Withholding per check: ~$462 (based on annualized 22% bracket)
  • Social Security: $3,500 × 6.2% = $217
  • Medicare: $3,500 × 1.45% = $50.75
  • State: ($3,500 − $200) × 5% = $165
  • Net Pay: $3,500 − $462 − $217 − $50.75 − $165 − $200 = ~$2,405

Frequently Asked Questions

The IRS percentage method annualizes each paycheck, subtracts the standard withholding allowance, applies the progressive tax brackets, then divides back by pay periods. The 2020+ W4 uses income amounts rather than allowances.

For 2026, the Social Security taxable wage base is approximately $174,900. Wages above this limit are not subject to the 6.2% Social Security tax, though Medicare's 1.45% applies to all wages.

Common pay frequencies are weekly (52 paychecks/year), bi-weekly (26/year), semi-monthly (24/year), and monthly (12/year). Bi-weekly and semi-monthly are most common in US employment.

Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal and state income tax withholding because they lower your taxable wages. However, they do not reduce Social Security or Medicare (FICA) taxes, which apply to gross wages before the 401(k) deduction.
