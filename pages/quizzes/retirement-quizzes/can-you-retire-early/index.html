Can You Retire Early?

Financial independence and early retirement (FIRE) is achievable — but only if the numbers add up. This 9-question quiz assesses your savings rate, portfolio size relative to your expenses, passive income streams, healthcare strategy, debt-free status, and withdrawal rate knowledge to tell you how close you really are to leaving work behind on your own terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

The 4% rule (from the Trinity Study) states that withdrawing 4% of your portfolio in year one, then adjusting for inflation, has historically sustained a portfolio for 30 years with a high success rate. For early retirees with 40+ year horizons, many FIRE planners use 3–3.5% instead to increase safety margins.

Your FI number is the total portfolio value needed so that investment returns can sustain your living expenses indefinitely. The standard formula is: Annual Expenses × 25 (which equals a 4% withdrawal rate). If you spend $50,000/year, your FI number is $1,250,000. Reducing annual expenses has a double benefit — it lowers the FI number and frees up more money to save.

Healthcare is the biggest wild card for early retirees in the US. Common strategies include: ACA marketplace plans (often subsidised at low income levels), health-sharing ministries, COBRA continuation coverage for up to 18 months, a spouse's employer plan, or part-time work with health benefits. Budget $500–$1,500+/month per person if you don't have a subsidised option.
