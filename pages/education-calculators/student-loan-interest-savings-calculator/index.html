Interest Savings Formula

The savings are calculated by comparing the total interest paid under the standard repayment schedule versus the accelerated schedule with extra payments:

Interest Savings
Interest Saved = Total Interest (Standard) - Total Interest (With Extra Payments)

The standard monthly payment is calculated using the amortization formula. Extra payments reduce the principal faster, which reduces the interest accrued each month and shortens the loan term.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current loan balance - the remaining principal on your student loan.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate - found on your loan statement or servicer website.
  3. Enter the original loan term - the total repayment period in years.
  4. Enter your extra monthly payment - any additional amount beyond the minimum payment.
  5. Click "Calculate Savings" to see how much interest and time you will save.

Example Scenario

With a $35,000 student loan at 5.5% interest over 10 years, your standard monthly payment is about $380. Adding an extra $100 per month would save you approximately $3,800 in interest and pay off the loan about 2 years and 7 months early.

Frequently Asked Questions

The savings depend on your loan balance, interest rate, and extra payment amount. Even an extra $50-100 per month can save thousands of dollars in interest and shave years off your repayment timeline.

If your loan interest rate is higher than expected investment returns (after taxes), paying off loans first typically makes more sense. For low-rate federal loans, investing may provide better returns. Consider your risk tolerance and financial goals.

Extra payments should go toward principal, which reduces the balance that accrues interest. Contact your loan servicer to ensure extra payments are applied to principal rather than advancing your due date.

Federal student loans have no prepayment penalties. Most private student loans also do not charge prepayment penalties, but check your loan agreement to be sure.
