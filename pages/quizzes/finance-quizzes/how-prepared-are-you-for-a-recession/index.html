How Prepared Are You for a Recession?

Recessions are a normal part of the economic cycle — but their impact on your personal finances depends entirely on your preparation. This 8-question quiz evaluates your emergency fund, job security, debt exposure, income diversification, and investment resilience to give you an honest recession-readiness rating and a specific action plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

In the lead-up to a potential recession, focus on building cash reserves (emergency fund to 6+ months) before aggressively paying down debt. Cash is the most valuable asset during a recession because it provides optionality — you can cover expenses, buy discounted assets, or weather a job loss without borrowing.

Timing the market consistently is not possible — even professional fund managers fail to do it reliably. If your emergency fund is fully funded and you have no imminent cash needs, staying invested through downturns and continuing to invest during them (buying at lower prices) is typically the optimal strategy for long-term investors.

Healthcare, government, utilities, education, and essential retail tend to be most resilient. Finance, technology, construction, and discretionary retail are typically more cyclical and vulnerable to layoffs. Your personal job security also depends on your employer's financial health, industry exposure, and your replaceability in your specific role.

During economic uncertainty, increasing your emergency fund to 6–12 months of expenses is prudent. Cash beyond this threshold can be gradually invested as markets decline, taking advantage of lower prices. Holding excessive cash long-term has its own risk: inflation erodes purchasing power over time.
