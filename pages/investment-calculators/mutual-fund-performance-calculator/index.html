Mutual Fund Performance Formula

Total return accounts for both NAV appreciation and dividend income:

Total Return Formula
Total Return = ((Current NAV − Purchase NAV + Dividends per Share) / Purchase NAV) × 100

CAGR normalizes the total return to an annualized rate:

CAGR Formula
CAGR = ((Current Value + Dividends) / Initial Investment)^(1 / Years) − 1

Effective dividend yield expresses income relative to the initial investment:

Effective Yield Formula
Effective Yield = Total Dividends Received / Initial Investment × 100

Example Scenario

Suppose you invested $10,000 in a mutual fund at a NAV of $25.00, purchasing 400 shares. The current NAV is $38.50, giving a current portfolio value of $15,400. You have also received $850 in total dividends over 5 years. The total return would be approximately 62.5%, the CAGR around 10.25% per year, and the effective dividend yield 8.5% on your initial investment over the period.

Frequently Asked Questions

NAV stands for Net Asset Value. It is the per-share value of a mutual fund, calculated by dividing the total value of the fund's assets minus liabilities by the number of shares outstanding. Mutual funds are bought and sold at the NAV price.

Total return includes both capital appreciation (the increase in NAV) and any income distributions (dividends). The formula is: Total Return = ((Current NAV - Purchase NAV + Dividends per Share) / Purchase NAV) × 100.

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) converts your total return into an equivalent annualized rate, making it easy to compare funds held for different periods. It accounts for the compounding effect and normalizes performance to a per-year basis.

Effective yield for a mutual fund represents the income return from dividends expressed as a percentage of your initial investment. It shows how much of your return came from distributions versus capital appreciation.
