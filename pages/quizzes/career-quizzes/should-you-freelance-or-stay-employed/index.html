Should You Freelance or Stay Employed?

Freelancing offers freedom and income potential — but it also demands financial discipline, self-marketing, and tolerance for uncertainty. Staying employed provides stability and benefits, but limits your ceiling. This 9-question quiz evaluates your readiness across the financial, personal, and professional dimensions that matter most before making the leap.

Frequently Asked Questions

As a freelancer, you pay self-employment tax (15.3% on top of income tax), fund your own benefits (health insurance can cost $400–$800/month), and manage overhead. Most financial advisors recommend charging 30–50% above your equivalent hourly salary to net the same take-home. Our freelance rate calculator helps you find the right number.

Income variability is the top risk. Client delays, slow-pay invoices, project cancellations, and dry spells can create feast-or-famine cycles. Most financial experts recommend freelancers maintain 6 months of expenses in an emergency fund before going full-time independent. Without a financial cushion, one bad month can derail your whole operation.

For most people, yes. Freelancing on the side while employed lets you build a client pipeline, test your pricing, accumulate savings, and validate demand — all without sacrificing income security. When your side income consistently covers at least 50–75% of your expenses for 3+ months, you have a much stronger foundation for going full-time.
