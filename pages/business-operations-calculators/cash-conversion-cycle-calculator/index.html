Cash Conversion Cycle Formula

The CCC formula combines three key operational metrics to reveal the overall cash efficiency of your business.

Cash Conversion Cycle
CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO
Days Inventory Outstanding
DIO = (Average Inventory / COGS) x 365
Days Sales Outstanding
DSO = (Accounts Receivable / Revenue) x 365
Days Payable Outstanding
DPO = (Accounts Payable / COGS) x 365

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Find your DIO -- divide average inventory by COGS and multiply by 365, or use your accounting software's report.
  2. Find your DSO -- divide accounts receivable by total revenue and multiply by 365.
  3. Find your DPO -- divide accounts payable by COGS and multiply by 365.
  4. Click "Calculate CCC" to see your cycle length and get actionable improvement suggestions.

Example Scenario

A manufacturing business has the following metrics: DIO of 45 days, DSO of 30 days, and DPO of 20 days.

  • CCC: 45 + 30 - 20 = 55 days

This means the company ties up cash for 55 days between paying for inventory and collecting from customers. By reducing DSO to 20 days and increasing DPO to 40 days, the CCC would drop to 25 days -- freeing up significant working capital.

Frequently Asked Questions

The cash conversion cycle (CCC) measures how many days it takes a business to convert its investments in inventory and other resources into cash flows from sales. A lower CCC means faster cash conversion.

A negative or near-zero CCC is ideal, meaning you collect cash before paying suppliers. Amazon and many retailers achieve negative CCCs. For most businesses, a CCC under 30 days is considered efficient.

To reduce CCC: decrease DIO by improving inventory management, reduce DSO by tightening credit terms and collections, and increase DPO by negotiating longer payment terms with suppliers.

DPO measures how many days a company takes to pay its suppliers. A higher DPO can improve cash flow since you hold onto cash longer, but extremely high DPO may strain supplier relationships.
