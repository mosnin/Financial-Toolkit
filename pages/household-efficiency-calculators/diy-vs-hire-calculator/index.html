DIY vs Hire Formula

Cost Comparison
Time Cost = DIY Hours × Hourly Value Risk Buffer % by complexity: Low: 5% of materials | Medium: 15% | High: 30% Risk Buffer = Materials × Risk % True DIY Cost = Materials + Time Cost + Risk Buffer Net Savings = Pro Quote - True DIY Cost If Net Savings > 0: DIY wins financially If Net Savings < 0: Hiring out is the smarter financial choice

Note: this calculator measures financial savings only. Other factors like quality, warranty, permits, and your personal enjoyment of the work are also valid considerations.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the professional quote -- get a real quote if possible; otherwise use a typical cost from sites like HomeAdvisor or Angi.
  2. Enter your material cost -- price out the materials you would need at a hardware store.
  3. Estimate your DIY hours -- be honest and add a 25% buffer for learning time on unfamiliar projects.
  4. Enter your hourly time value -- your after-tax wage is a good baseline.
  5. Select mistake risk level -- higher for projects that involve plumbing, electrical, or structural work.
  6. Click "Compare Costs" for a clear financial verdict.

Example Scenario

Interior Painting -- 3 Rooms

Pro quote: $1,200 | Materials: $280 | Hours: 12 | Hourly value: $40 | Risk: Low

Time cost: 12 × $40 = $480 | Risk buffer: $280 × 5% = $14

True DIY cost: $280 + $480 + $14 = $774

Net savings: $1,200 - $774 = $426 -- DIY wins

Frequently Asked Questions

DIY saves money when the material cost is significantly lower than the professional quote, your time value is lower than the labor charge, the skill requirement matches your abilities, and the risk of a costly mistake is low. Simple projects like painting, basic landscaping, and minor repairs are often good DIY candidates.

Time value is how much your time is worth per hour -- typically your after-tax hourly wage, or the hourly rate of work you could be doing instead. If you earn $40/hour and a DIY project takes 8 hours, your time cost is $320. If the labor portion of the pro quote is $280, hiring out makes financial sense.

Yes. For complex projects like electrical, plumbing, or roofing, a mistake can cost far more to fix than the original pro quote. Add a risk factor (typically 10-30% of materials cost) for any project where errors could damage your home or require a professional to redo the work.

Projects that typically should not be DIY include: main electrical panel work, gas line repairs, structural changes, roofing on high-pitch roofs, foundation work, HVAC refrigerant handling, and any work that requires a permit and inspection. Errors in these areas can be dangerous or void your home insurance.
