How Feasibility Is Calculated

Formula
Years to Retire = Target Age − Current Age
Projected Portfolio = PV × (1+r)^n + PMT × [((1+r)^n − 1) / r]
FI Number = Annual Expenses ÷ 0.04
Gap/Surplus = Projected Portfolio − FI Number

Feasible if Projected Portfolio ≥ FI Number

The future value formula compounds your current savings and monthly contributions at your expected annual return over the years remaining until your target retirement age. The result is compared to your FI number (annual expenses divided by 4% SWR) to determine feasibility. A positive gap means you're on track; a negative gap means adjustments are needed.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current and target ages: The gap between them determines your accumulation window.
  2. Add current savings and monthly contributions: Include all investment accounts — 401k, IRA, taxable accounts.
  3. Set your expected return: 7% nominal is a common assumption for a diversified stock portfolio.
  4. Enter retirement annual expenses: Estimate what you'll spend per year in retirement in today's dollars.
  5. Read the verdict: Green means feasible, yellow means close, red means a gap — with exact dollar amounts shown.

Example Scenario

Jordan is 33 years old with $85,000 saved and contributes $2,500/month. Targeting retirement at age 50 (17 years away) with $65,000/year in retirement expenses, Jordan's FI number is $1,625,000. At a 7% return, the projected portfolio at age 50 is approximately $1,422,000 — a gap of $203,000. The verdict: not quite feasible at the current savings rate. Increasing contributions by $400/month would close the gap and make age-50 retirement achievable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Early retirement is feasible when your projected portfolio at your target age meets or exceeds your FI number — the amount needed to fund 25+ years of expenses at a 4% withdrawal rate. This calculator makes that comparison directly.

7% nominal is a widely used assumption based on historical US stock market returns. For more conservative planning, use 6%. Always run a range of scenarios — return assumptions have a large effect on long-term projections.

A gap means your savings won't fully cover your FI number by your target age. You can close it by increasing monthly contributions, retiring one or two years later, reducing planned expenses, or accepting part-time work income in early retirement (Barista FIRE approach).

In retirement, you withdraw from your portfolio each year. Using the 4% rule, you can take 4% of the initial portfolio value annually (adjusted for inflation). This calculator shows the annual income your projected portfolio supports at retirement age.
