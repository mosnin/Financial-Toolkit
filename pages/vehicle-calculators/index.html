Frequently Asked Questions

Financial experts recommend spending no more than 10-15% of your gross monthly income on car payments. Your total vehicle expenses (payment, insurance, fuel, maintenance) should not exceed 20% of your take-home pay.

Leasing offers lower monthly payments and the ability to drive a new car every few years. Buying costs more monthly but builds equity and is cheaper long-term if you keep the car for 5+ years. Lease if you want lower payments and new features; buy if you want to minimize long-term costs.

New cars lose about 20-30% of their value in the first year and roughly 50-60% over five years. Luxury vehicles tend to depreciate faster, while trucks and certain brands like Toyota and Honda hold value better.

EVs typically have higher upfront costs but lower operating costs. Electricity is cheaper than gas per mile, EVs need less maintenance (no oil changes, fewer brake replacements), and federal tax credits of up to $7,500 can offset the purchase price. Total cost of ownership often favors EVs over 5+ years.

Good auto loan rates depend on your credit score and the current rate environment. With excellent credit (750+), expect rates of 4-6%. Good credit (700-749) typically sees 6-8%. Fair credit (650-699) may see 8-12%. Always compare rates from multiple lenders including banks, credit unions, and dealer financing.
