Operating Margin Formula

Operating margin is derived from the operating section of the income statement, before non-operating items.

Operating Income (EBIT)
Operating Income = Revenue - COGS - Operating Expenses
Operating Margin
Operating Margin = (Operating Income / Revenue) x 100
Gross Margin (for reference)
Gross Margin = ((Revenue - COGS) / Revenue) x 100

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your revenue -- total sales income for the period.
  2. Enter COGS -- all direct costs tied to producing goods or delivering services.
  3. Enter operating expenses -- indirect costs like payroll, rent, utilities, marketing, and depreciation (exclude interest and taxes).
  4. Select your industry to receive a benchmark comparison of your operating margin.
  5. Click "Calculate Operating Margin" to see your results.

Example Scenario

A professional services firm has $400,000 revenue, $160,000 COGS, and $120,000 in operating expenses.

  • Gross Profit: $400,000 - $160,000 = $240,000 (60% gross margin)
  • Operating Income: $240,000 - $120,000 = $120,000
  • Operating Margin: ($120,000 / $400,000) x 100 = 30%

A 30% operating margin is excellent for professional services, well above the 15-25% industry average. This indicates strong cost discipline and efficient operations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Operating margin measures how much profit a business makes from its core operations for each dollar of revenue, before interest and taxes. It is calculated as Operating Income / Revenue x 100.

Operating margin benchmarks vary by industry. Technology and software companies often achieve 20-35%, retail 3-8%, manufacturing 8-15%, and professional services 15-25%. Higher is generally better, but context matters.

Operating margin (EBIT margin) excludes interest and taxes, showing the profitability of core business operations. Net profit margin includes all expenses including interest and taxes, giving the true bottom-line figure.

To improve operating margin: reduce COGS through better procurement, cut unnecessary operating expenses, increase revenue through pricing or volume, or shift toward higher-margin products and services.
