Break-Even Formula

Total Monthly Need (gross)
Need = (Personal Expenses + Business Expenses + Desired Salary) / (1 - Tax Rate)
Minimum Hourly Rate
Min Rate = Minimum Monthly Revenue / (Billable Hrs/Week × 4.33 weeks)
Days Per Month to Break Even
Days = Minimum Monthly Revenue / (Hourly Rate × 6 billable hrs/day)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly personal expenses — rent, groceries, utilities, subscriptions, debt payments, and all other living costs.
  2. Add monthly business expenses — software, accounting, marketing, professional insurance, and other operational costs.
  3. Enter your desired monthly take-home pay above and beyond expenses.
  4. Input your estimated combined tax rate (self-employment + income tax).
  5. Click Calculate Break-Even to see your revenue floor and rate scenarios.

Example Scenario

A freelancer has $3,500 in personal expenses, $800 in business expenses, wants $5,000 take-home, and estimates a 30% tax rate.

  • Total Monthly Need (net): $3,500 + $800 + $5,000 = $9,300
  • Minimum Revenue: $9,300 / (1 - 0.30) = $13,286/month
  • Min Rate at 25 hrs/wk: $13,286 / (25 × 4.33) = $122.76/hr
  • Days to Break Even at $100/hr: $13,286 / (100 × 6) = 22.1 days

Frequently Asked Questions

Your freelance break-even is the minimum monthly revenue needed to cover all personal and business expenses plus your desired salary after taxes. Below this number, your business is operating at a loss.

Taxes must be paid from revenue, so your break-even revenue must be grossed up. If you need $6,000 net and pay 30% taxes, you need $6,000 / 0.70 = $8,571 in revenue just to break even.

Most freelancers achieve 50-70% billable utilization of their working hours. If you work 8 hours a day, expect 4-6 billable hours. Use conservative estimates to avoid falling short of break-even.

Divide your minimum monthly revenue by your daily revenue capacity (hourly rate times 6 billable hours per day). This gives the minimum working days needed and shows how much buffer you have in a typical month.
