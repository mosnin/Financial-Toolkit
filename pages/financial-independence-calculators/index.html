Financial Independence Calculators

Financial independence — the point at which your investment portfolio generates enough income to cover your living expenses indefinitely — is an achievable goal with the right plan and consistent action. Our financial independence calculators help you determine your personal FI number, choose the FIRE path that fits your lifestyle (lean, barista, coast, or traditional), and model the exact timeline to reach your target based on your savings rate and expected returns. Whether you plan to retire at 35 or simply want the freedom of knowing you could quit your job, these tools translate the FIRE movement's core principles into concrete, personalized projections. Frequently Asked Questions

The 4% rule, derived from the Trinity Study, states that you can withdraw 4% of your initial portfolio value each year (adjusted for inflation) with a high probability of not running out of money over a 30-year retirement. It implies a financial independence number of 25 times your annual expenses. For longer retirements common in early retirement, many planners recommend a more conservative 3–3.5% rate.

Lean FIRE involves retiring on a very frugal budget, typically under $40,000 per year, requiring a smaller portfolio. Barista FIRE means retiring from a high-stress career but working part-time (enough to cover basic expenses) so your portfolio can continue growing untouched. Coast FIRE is the point where your existing portfolio, left to grow without contributions, will reach your full FI number by traditional retirement age.

Savings rate is the single most powerful lever in a FIRE plan because it simultaneously increases the amount you invest and decreases your required FI number (since you need less income to maintain a lower-spending lifestyle). At a 10% savings rate, FIRE takes about 43 years. At 50%, roughly 17 years. At 75%, approximately 7 years. Use our FIRE Timeline Calculator to model your exact scenario.

A market crash at the start of retirement is the primary risk to FIRE plans, a concept known as sequence of returns risk. Strategies to mitigate this include maintaining 1–2 years of cash reserves, using a flexible spending approach (spending less in down markets), considering part-time work during downturns, and maintaining a slightly lower withdrawal rate like 3–3.5%. Our Safe Withdrawal Rate Calculator lets you model these scenarios.

Your FI number is the total portfolio value needed to cover your living expenses indefinitely through investment returns. The most common formula is annual expenses multiplied by 25 (based on a 4% withdrawal rate). For example, if you spend $50,000 per year, your FI number is $1.25 million. Use our Financial Independence Number Calculator to personalize this figure with your actual expenses and chosen withdrawal rate.
