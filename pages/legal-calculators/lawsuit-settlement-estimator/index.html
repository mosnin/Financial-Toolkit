Settlement Estimation Methodology

Settlement estimates are derived from multiple factors:

Settlement Estimate Formula
Base Value = Total Damages x Case Type Multiplier
Adjusted Value = Base Value x Liability Factor x Jurisdiction Factor
Range = Adjusted Value x 0.6 (low) to Adjusted Value x 1.2 (high)

The case type multiplier accounts for non-economic damages typical to each case type. The liability factor adjusts based on how clearly the defendant is at fault. The jurisdiction factor reflects local settlement trends. The range accounts for negotiation variables.

How to Use This Estimator

  1. Select your case type - different case types have different settlement patterns and multipliers.
  2. Enter total documented damages - include all quantifiable losses (medical bills, lost wages, property damage, etc.).
  3. Assess liability strength - how clearly is the defendant at fault? Consider available evidence.
  4. Select jurisdiction type - consider whether your area tends to favor plaintiffs or defendants.
  5. Click "Estimate Settlement" to see the estimated settlement range and expected timeline.

Example Scenario

A personal injury case with $50,000 in documented damages, strong liability (clear evidence of defendant fault), and a neutral jurisdiction. The case type multiplier of 2x brings the base value to $100,000. With a strong liability factor of 0.85 and a neutral jurisdiction factor of 1.0, the adjusted value is $85,000. The estimated settlement range would be approximately $51,000 to $102,000, with an expected timeline of 6-18 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Lawsuit settlements are estimated based on several factors: the total documented damages, the strength of liability, the jurisdiction, the type of case, and the willingness of both parties to settle. Settlements typically range from 40-80% of the total damages depending on these factors.

Approximately 95% of civil lawsuits settle before going to trial. Settlements are preferred because they reduce risk, legal costs, and time for both parties.

Liability strength is one of the most important factors. If the defendant is clearly at fault, settlements tend to be higher. If liability is disputed or shared, settlements are discounted proportionally.

The timeline varies greatly. Simple cases may settle in 3-6 months. Moderate cases typically take 6-18 months. Complex litigation can take 2-5 years depending on discovery, expert witnesses, and court schedules.
