Mileage Reimbursement Formula

The IRS standard mileage reimbursement is calculated as:

Reimbursement Amount
Reimbursement = Miles Driven x IRS Standard Rate Per Mile

For the actual expense comparison, divide your total vehicle costs by miles driven to get your actual cost per mile, then compare to the IRS rate.

How to Use This Mileage Reimbursement Calculator

  1. Enter the miles driven - the total business miles for the period.
  2. Confirm the IRS rate - the current standard mileage rate (default is $0.67 for 2024).
  3. Enter your actual costs (optional) - your total vehicle expenses including gas, insurance, maintenance, and depreciation for the same period.
  4. Click "Calculate Reimbursement" to see your reimbursement amount and comparison to actual costs.

Example Scenario

Suppose you drove 5,000 business miles this year. At the IRS rate of $0.67/mile, your reimbursement would be $3,350. If your actual vehicle costs for the period were $3,000, the standard mileage rate gives you $350 more. In this case, the standard rate is the better method.

Frequently Asked Questions

The IRS standard mileage rate for business use of a vehicle is $0.67 per mile for 2024. This rate is updated annually and covers the cost of gas, depreciation, insurance, maintenance, and other vehicle expenses.

Mileage reimbursement is calculated by multiplying the number of business miles driven by the IRS standard mileage rate. For example, 500 miles at $0.67/mile equals $335 in reimbursement.

Compare both methods. The standard mileage rate is simpler but may not cover all your costs if you drive an expensive or inefficient vehicle. Actual expenses require tracking every cost but may yield a higher deduction for some drivers.

Mileage reimbursement at or below the IRS standard rate is generally not taxable income. However, any reimbursement above the IRS rate is considered taxable income and must be reported.

Business miles include driving from one work location to another, driving to meet clients, and travel to temporary work locations. Commuting from home to your regular office does not qualify.
