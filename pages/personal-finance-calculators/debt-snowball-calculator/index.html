Snowball Payoff Order & Milestones

How the Debt Snowball Works

Strategy
1. List all debts from SMALLEST balance to LARGEST.
2. Make minimum payments on all debts every month.
3. Put all extra money toward the SMALLEST balance debt.
4. When paid off, roll that full payment to the next smallest.
5. Repeat — your snowball grows with each debt eliminated.

The power of the snowball is psychological. Quick wins from paying off small debts release dopamine and reinforce the habit of debt repayment. Research shows that people who achieve early payoff milestones are significantly more likely to complete their full debt payoff plan.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter each debt: Name, balance, annual interest rate, and minimum monthly payment for up to five debts.
  2. Add an extra payment: Any additional amount above your total minimum payments speeds up the snowball.
  3. Calculate: The calculator orders debts smallest-to-largest and simulates the month-by-month payoff.
  4. Review milestones: Each row shows when that specific debt will be fully paid off — your celebration dates.
  5. Plan ahead: Use the debt-free date to set financial goals for life after debt.

Example Scenario

Morgan has four debts: a medical bill ($800 at 0%), a store card ($1,200 at 24.99%), a personal loan ($5,000 at 12%), and a car loan ($11,000 at 7.5%). Minimum payments total $380/month, and Morgan adds $200 extra. The snowball order is: medical bill first (paid off in 4 months), then the store card (month 10), then the personal loan (month 26), and finally the car loan (month 44). Each payoff date becomes a real milestone to celebrate.

Frequently Asked Questions

The debt snowball method pays off debts from smallest balance to largest. Minimum payments go to all debts while extra money targets the smallest. When eliminated, that payment rolls to the next — creating a growing snowball of payments.

Mathematically the avalanche saves more interest. But the snowball's quick wins boost motivation, and research shows it often leads to higher payoff completion rates. The best method is the one you will stick with.

Even $50–$100 extra per month accelerates the snowball significantly. Look for spending cuts or side income opportunities to grow your extra payment over time — every dollar helps.

Milestones are the dates each individual debt is fully paid off. Since the snowball targets small balances first, you often hit your first milestone within a few months — providing a powerful psychological reward to keep going.
