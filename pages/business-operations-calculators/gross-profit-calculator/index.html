Gross Profit Formulas

These core formulas form the basis of income statement analysis.

Gross Profit
Gross Profit = Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
Gross Margin %
Gross Margin = (Gross Profit / Revenue) x 100
Gross Profit per Unit
Gross Profit per Unit = Gross Profit / Units Sold
COGS Ratio
COGS Ratio = (COGS / Revenue) x 100

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your revenue -- the total sales income for the period you are analyzing.
  2. Enter your COGS -- direct costs including raw materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead.
  3. Optionally enter units sold to see gross profit broken down per unit, useful for pricing analysis.
  4. Click "Calculate Gross Profit" to see your gross profit, gross margin, and per-unit profitability.

Example Scenario

A consumer goods company generates $250,000 in revenue, has $150,000 in COGS, and sold 1,000 units.

  • Gross Profit: $250,000 - $150,000 = $100,000
  • Gross Margin: ($100,000 / $250,000) x 100 = 40%
  • COGS Ratio: ($150,000 / $250,000) x 100 = 60%
  • Gross Profit per Unit: $100,000 / 1,000 = $100 per unit

A 40% gross margin leaves $100,000 to cover operating expenses and generate net profit. Improving COGS by 5% would add $12,500 directly to gross profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Gross profit is the revenue remaining after subtracting the cost of goods sold (COGS). It represents how much money is available to cover operating expenses and generate net profit.

Gross margin is gross profit expressed as a percentage of revenue. It shows what proportion of each dollar of revenue is kept as gross profit after covering direct production costs.

Good gross margins vary widely by industry. Software companies typically achieve 70-90%, retail 20-40%, manufacturing 25-50%, and grocery 20-25%. Compare your margin to industry peers for proper benchmarking.

Gross profit is revenue minus COGS only. Net profit subtracts all additional expenses including operating costs, interest, and taxes. Gross profit is always higher than net profit.
