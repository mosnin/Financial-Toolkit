Frequently Asked Questions

Lenders prefer a DTI below 36%, with no more than 28% going toward housing costs. A DTI above 43% may make it difficult to qualify for a mortgage. Below 20% is considered excellent.

The avalanche method (paying highest-interest debt first) saves the most money on interest. The snowball method (paying smallest balance first) provides psychological wins that help you stay motivated. Choose the approach that you will stick with.

Credit utilization (the percentage of your available credit you are using) accounts for about 30% of your credit score. Keeping utilization below 30% is recommended, and below 10% is ideal for the best credit score impact.

Debt consolidation can be beneficial if you qualify for a lower interest rate than your current debts and it simplifies your payments. However, it only helps if you avoid accumulating new debt on the freed-up credit lines.

Paying only minimum payments on credit card debt can take 15-30+ years and cost many times the original balance in interest. Even small additional payments can dramatically reduce the time and cost of repayment.
