How Dental Cost Calculations Work

This calculator applies your insurance coverage percentage to the estimated procedure cost:

Insurance Pays
Insurance Amount = Procedure Cost × (Coverage % / 100)
Your Out-of-Pocket
Out-of-Pocket = Procedure Cost - Insurance Amount

Note that most dental plans also have an annual deductible (typically $50-$150) that must be met before coverage kicks in, and an annual maximum benefit ($1,000-$2,000) after which you pay 100%. This calculator provides a simplified estimate without those factors.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your procedure from the dropdown — common procedures have national average costs pre-filled.
  2. Adjust the cost if you have a specific quote from your dentist or live in a high/low cost area.
  3. Select your insurance type — or enter a custom coverage percentage from your plan's Explanation of Benefits (EOB).
  4. Click Calculate to see your estimated out-of-pocket cost and what insurance covers.

Example Scenario

A patient needs a porcelain crown. The dentist's fee is $1,300. Their insurance plan covers major procedures at 50% after a $100 deductible (already met). Insurance pays $650, leaving the patient responsible for $650 out-of-pocket. However, if they have already used $800 of their $1,500 annual maximum, only $700 in benefits remain — reducing the insurance payout to $650 in this case, which still works out, but illustrates why tracking your annual maximum usage matters.

Most dental insurance plans follow a 100-80-50 structure: 100% for preventive care, 80% for basic procedures (fillings, extractions), and 50% for major procedures (crowns, root canals). Annual maximums typically range from $1,000 to $2,000.
