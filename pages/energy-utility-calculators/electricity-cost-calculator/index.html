Electricity Cost Formula

Cost Calculation
Monthly Cost = Monthly kWh × Rate per kWh Daily Cost = Monthly Cost / 30.44 (avg days per month) Annual Cost = Monthly Cost × 12 Billing Period Cost = Monthly Cost × Billing Period Months

Your electricity rate per kWh is found on your utility bill. It may include a flat energy charge plus additional fees. Some utilities have tiered rates that increase once you exceed a usage threshold.

The billing period option helps estimate what your bill will look like based on how frequently your utility bills you.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Find your monthly kWh usage -- this appears on your electricity bill.
  2. Enter your rate per kWh -- look for the energy charge on your bill, typically $0.10 to $0.30/kWh.
  3. Select your billing period -- choose how often you receive a bill.
  4. Click "Calculate Electricity Cost" to see costs broken down by day, month, and year.

Example Scenario

Average US Household: 900 kWh at $0.16/kWh

Monthly usage: 900 kWh | Rate: $0.16/kWh | Billing: Monthly

Monthly Cost = 900 × $0.16 = $144.00

Daily Cost = $144.00 / 30.44 = $4.73/day

Annual Cost = $144.00 × 12 = $1,728/year

This is close to the national average electricity spend. If you live in a higher-rate state like California at $0.28/kWh, the same usage would cost $3,024/year.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average US residential electricity rate is approximately $0.16 per kWh as of 2025, though rates vary significantly by state -- from around $0.10/kWh in Louisiana to over $0.30/kWh in Hawaii and California.

The average US home uses about 899 kWh per month. This varies widely based on home size, climate, number of occupants, and appliances. Homes in the South tend to use more due to air conditioning.

Key strategies include: switching to LED lighting, using a programmable thermostat, upgrading to Energy Star appliances, improving insulation, using power strips to eliminate phantom loads, and running appliances during off-peak hours if your utility offers time-of-use pricing.

The biggest electricity consumers in most homes are HVAC systems (heating and cooling), water heaters, electric dryers, refrigerators, and electric ovens. Smart thermostats and efficient HVAC systems often offer the largest savings opportunities.
