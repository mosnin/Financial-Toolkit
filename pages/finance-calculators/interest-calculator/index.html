Simple Interest Formula

Simple interest is calculated using one of the most straightforward formulas in finance:

Simple Interest Formula
I = P x r x t

Where I is the interest earned, P is the principal (initial amount), r is the annual interest rate (as a decimal), and t is the time in years. The total amount is simply A = P + I.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the principal - the starting amount of money.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate - the percentage rate per year.
  3. Enter the time period - the number of years (decimals allowed, e.g., 2.5 for two and a half years).
  4. Click "Calculate Interest" to view the interest earned and total amount.

Example Scenario

If you invest $10,000 at a 5% annual simple interest rate for 3 years, you would earn $1,500 in interest (10,000 x 0.05 x 3 = 1,500). Your total amount at the end of 3 years would be $11,500.

Frequently Asked Questions

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original principal amount. Unlike compound interest, it does not include interest on previously earned interest. The formula is I = P x r x t, where P is principal, r is the annual rate as a decimal, and t is time in years.

Simple interest is calculated only on the initial principal, so the interest amount stays the same each period. Compound interest is calculated on both the principal and accumulated interest, causing exponential growth over time. For long periods, compound interest yields significantly more.

Simple interest is commonly used for short-term loans, car loans, some personal loans, and certificates of deposit. It is also used in U.S. Treasury bills and some bonds.

If you are a borrower, simple interest means you pay interest only on the original amount borrowed, which is generally favorable. If you are an investor, simple interest earns less than compound interest over long periods because it does not benefit from interest-on-interest growth.
