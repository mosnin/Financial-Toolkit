GPA Projection Formula

The required GPA for remaining courses is derived from the weighted average formula:

Required GPA Formula
Required GPA = (Target GPA x Total Credits - Current GPA x Completed Credits) / Remaining Credits

Where Total Credits = Completed Credits + Remaining Credits. If the result exceeds 4.0, the target is not achievable with the given remaining credits.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current cumulative GPA - found on your transcript or student portal.
  2. Enter credits completed - total credit hours you have already earned.
  3. Enter your target GPA - the cumulative GPA you want to achieve.
  4. Enter remaining credits - how many credit hours you have left before graduation.
  5. Click "Calculate Required GPA" to see the GPA needed in your remaining courses.

Example Scenario

A student with a 3.2 GPA after 60 credits wants to graduate with a 3.5 GPA and has 60 credits remaining. The required GPA for remaining courses is: (3.5 x 120 - 3.2 x 60) / 60 = (420 - 192) / 60 = 3.80. The student needs a 3.80 GPA in remaining courses, which is challenging but achievable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cumulative GPA is calculated by dividing the total quality points (GPA x credit hours for each course) by the total credit hours attempted. For example, if you have a 3.2 GPA over 60 credits, your total quality points are 192.

The more credits you have already completed, the harder it is to change your GPA significantly. Early in your college career, a single strong semester can make a big difference. Later on, GPA changes more slowly because existing credits carry more weight.

Most graduate programs prefer a GPA of 3.0 or higher, with competitive programs often expecting 3.5+. However, requirements vary by program, and other factors like test scores, research experience, and recommendations also matter.

A 4.0 in remaining courses means earning an A in every class. If the calculator shows you need above a 4.0, your target GPA is mathematically impossible with the remaining credits. Consider adjusting your target or taking additional courses.
