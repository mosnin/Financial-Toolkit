Comparison Formula

To compare contractor and employee compensation fairly:

Comparison Formulas
1099 Net = Contractor Rate - SE Tax (15.3%) - Expenses W-2 Total = Salary + Benefits Value

The 1099 rate must cover self-employment tax (15.3%), health insurance, retirement, and other benefits that W-2 employees receive from their employer.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the 1099 contractor rate - the total annual pay as a contractor.
  2. Enter the W-2 salary - the annual salary offered as an employee.
  3. Enter benefits value - estimated annual value of employee benefits.
  4. Enter contractor expenses - costs you would incur as a contractor for insurance, equipment, etc.
  5. Click "Compare Options" to see which option comes out ahead.

Example Scenario

A $120,000 contractor rate vs a $90,000 W-2 salary with $15,000 in benefits: The contractor pays $18,360 in SE tax and $10,000 in expenses, netting $91,640. The W-2 total comp is $105,000. In this case, the W-2 position is worth $13,360 more annually.

Frequently Asked Questions

Contractors typically need to charge 25-40% more than an equivalent W-2 salary to cover self-employment tax, health insurance, retirement, and benefits.

W-2 employees have taxes withheld and receive benefits. 1099 contractors pay their own taxes including full SE tax, buy their own benefits, but may deduct business expenses.

Factor in health insurance, 401(k) match, PTO, paid holidays, life insurance, disability insurance, employer FICA (7.65%), and other perks.

It depends on the rates and your situation. Contractors enjoy flexibility and deductions but pay more in taxes. Employees get stability and benefits. Compare total compensation to decide.
