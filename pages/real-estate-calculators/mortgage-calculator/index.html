How the Formula Works

The mortgage payment formula calculates the fixed monthly payment needed to fully amortize a loan over a set period. The standard formula is:

Monthly Payment Formula
M = P [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]

Where M is the monthly payment, P is the principal (loan amount), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of payments (years multiplied by 12). Each payment covers both interest on the remaining balance and a portion of the principal.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the total home price you are considering.
  2. Set your down payment as a percentage of the home price. The default is 20%, which avoids private mortgage insurance.
  3. Enter the annual interest rate offered by your lender or a current average rate.
  4. Select your preferred loan term. A 30-year term has lower monthly payments, while a 15-year term saves significantly on interest.
  5. Click "Calculate Payment" to see your estimated monthly payment, total interest, and total cost.

Example Scenario

Suppose you are buying a home for $400,000 with a 20% down payment ($80,000), leaving a loan amount of $320,000. At a 6.5% interest rate on a 30-year term, your monthly payment would be approximately $2,023. Over the life of the loan, you would pay about $408,274 in total interest, making the total cost of the home around $808,274 including your down payment.

If you chose a 15-year term instead, your monthly payment would jump to about $2,787, but your total interest drops to roughly $181,703 -- saving you over $226,000 in interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Your monthly mortgage payment is calculated using the formula M = P[r(1+r)^n]/[(1+r)^n-1], where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the total number of payments. This formula accounts for both principal repayment and interest charges spread evenly over the life of the loan.

A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but significantly lower total interest costs. A 30-year mortgage offers lower monthly payments, making it more affordable month-to-month, but you pay substantially more in interest over the life of the loan. For example, on a $300,000 loan at 6.5%, a 15-year term costs about $130,000 in interest while a 30-year term costs roughly $383,000.

This calculator focuses on the principal and interest portion of your mortgage payment. Your actual monthly housing cost will also include property taxes, homeowners insurance, and possibly private mortgage insurance (PMI) if your down payment is less than 20%. Use our Property Tax Calculator and Down Payment Calculator for those estimates.

The traditional recommendation is 20% to avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI). However, many loan programs allow as little as 3-5% down. A larger down payment means a smaller loan, lower monthly payments, and less total interest paid. Use our Down Payment Calculator to plan your savings strategy.

Use the rate quoted by your lender or check current average rates from sources like Freddie Mac. Rates vary based on your credit score, loan type, down payment, and market conditions. Even a small difference in rate can significantly affect your total cost over the life of the loan.
