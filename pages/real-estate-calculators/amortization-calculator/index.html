Amortization Formula

Monthly Payment Formula
M = P [ r(1+r)^n ] / [ (1+r)^n - 1 ]

Where M is the monthly payment, P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12), and n is the total number of payments (years x 12).

Each monthly payment is split between interest (calculated on the remaining balance) and principal repayment. Early in the loan, most of the payment goes toward interest. Over time, the principal portion grows as the balance shrinks.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your loan amount -- the total amount you are borrowing.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate -- the yearly rate charged by your lender.
  3. Enter the loan term in years -- typical terms are 15 or 30 years for mortgages.
  4. Click "Generate Schedule" to see your monthly payment and full amortization table.
  5. Review the year-by-year breakdown to understand how your payments are allocated between principal and interest.

Example Scenario

$300,000 Mortgage at 6.5% for 30 Years

Suppose you take out a $300,000 mortgage at a 6.5% annual interest rate for a 30-year term. Your monthly payment would be approximately $1,896.20.

Over the life of the loan, you would pay a total of approximately $382,633 in interest, making the total cost of the loan about $682,633. In the first year alone, roughly $19,362 of your payments goes to interest, while only $3,392 goes toward the principal.

If you switched to a 15-year term instead, the monthly payment rises to about $2,613, but total interest drops to roughly $170,353 -- a savings of over $212,000.
