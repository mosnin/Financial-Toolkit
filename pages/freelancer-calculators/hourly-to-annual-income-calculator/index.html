Knowing your hourly rate doesn't tell the full story — your true annual income depends on how many weeks you work, how much of your time is actually billable, and how you handle vacation. This calculator converts your freelance hourly rate to a realistic annual income projection, including a stability range that reflects real-world variability.

Hourly to Annual Income Formula

Working Weeks
Working Weeks = Weeks Worked Per Year - Vacation Weeks
Gross Annual Revenue
Revenue = Hourly Rate × Billable Hrs/Week × Working Weeks
Effective Annual Income (adjusted for non-billable time)
Effective = Revenue × (1 - Non-Billable Ratio) — reflects true time investment
Income Stability Range
Low = Revenue × 0.75 | High = Revenue × 1.10 (capacity ceiling)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your hourly rate and target billable hours per week.
  2. Set the total weeks you plan to work per year, and separately your vacation weeks.
  3. Enter your non-billable time ratio — the percentage of your total working hours not generating direct revenue.
  4. Click Calculate Annual Income to see projected revenue, effective income, and your income stability range.

Example Scenario

A freelance UX designer charges $100/hr, targets 30 billable hours/week, works 48 weeks/year, takes 4 vacation weeks, and has a 30% non-billable ratio.

  • Working Weeks: 48 - 4 = 44 weeks
  • Gross Revenue: $100 × 30 × 44 = $132,000
  • Effective Annual Income (adjusted): $132,000 (revenue is as-billed; non-billable just measures time cost)
  • Monthly Average: $132,000 / 12 = $11,000
  • Income Stability Range: $99,000 – $145,200

Frequently Asked Questions

Multiply your hourly rate by actual billable hours per week, then by working weeks per year (total minus vacation). Subtract non-billable time to understand the true time cost of generating that revenue.

The non-billable ratio is the percentage of total working hours spent on tasks that don't generate direct revenue — admin, business development, networking, and professional development. Most freelancers have a 25-40% non-billable ratio.

Most freelancers target 46-50 working weeks per year after accounting for holidays, vacation, sick days, and professional development. Using 48 weeks is a conservative, realistic planning assumption.

The stability range represents the realistic low-to-high income spread. Your low end reflects slow months and client gaps (roughly 75% of projected), while the high end represents peak utilization during strong months.
