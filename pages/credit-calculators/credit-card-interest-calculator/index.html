Credit Card Interest Formula

Monthly interest on a credit card is calculated as:

Monthly Interest Charge
Monthly Interest = Balance x (APR / 12)

For example, a $5,000 balance at 22.99% APR generates $95.79 in monthly interest ($5,000 x 0.2299 / 12). If you pay $200, only $104.21 goes toward reducing the balance.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current balance - the total amount owed on your credit card.
  2. Enter your APR - found on your card statement or account details.
  3. Enter your monthly payment - the amount you plan to pay each month.
  4. Click "Calculate" to see your interest breakdown and payoff timeline.

Example Scenario

You have a $4,000 credit card balance at 19.99% APR and pay $150 per month. In the first month, $66.63 goes to interest and $83.37 goes to principal. At this rate, you would pay off the balance in about 32 months, paying a total of $788 in interest on top of the original $4,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

Credit card interest is typically calculated using the average daily balance method. Your APR is divided by 365 to get a daily rate, which is applied to your average daily balance. The monthly charge is the sum of these daily charges.
