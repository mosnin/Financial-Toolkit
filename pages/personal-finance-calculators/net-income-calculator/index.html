How Net Income Is Calculated

Formula
Taxable Income = Gross Salary − Pre-Tax Deductions
Federal Tax = Taxable Income × Federal Rate
State Tax = Taxable Income × State Rate
FICA = Gross Salary × FICA Rate
Net Annual = Gross − Federal Tax − State Tax − FICA − Pre-Tax Deductions
Effective Tax Rate = (Total Taxes / Gross Salary) × 100

Note that pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance) reduce your taxable income for federal and state purposes, but FICA taxes are typically calculated on gross wages before most pre-tax deductions (though 401k contributions are generally exempt from income tax but still subject to FICA). This calculator applies a simplified model for estimation purposes.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter gross annual salary: The total compensation before any deductions.
  2. Set tax rates: Use your marginal federal bracket rate, your state's rate, and 7.65% for FICA (standard employee rate).
  3. Add pre-tax deductions: Annual 401k contribution and health insurance premium reduce taxable income.
  4. Calculate: See your net monthly and annual take-home, plus a full breakdown of where your money goes.
  5. Use results for budgeting: Your net monthly income is the correct figure to use in expense planning.

Example Scenario

Jordan earns $80,000/year gross and contributes $8,000 to a 401k and pays $3,000/year for health insurance. Taxable income: $69,000. At 22% federal, 5% state, and 7.65% FICA on the full gross: Federal tax = $15,180, State tax = $3,450, FICA = $6,120. Total taxes: $24,750. Net annual: $80,000 - $24,750 - $11,000 (deductions) = $44,250. Net monthly take-home: $3,688. Effective tax rate: 30.9%.

Frequently Asked Questions

Gross income is total earnings before any deductions. Net income is what you take home after federal and state taxes, FICA, and pre-tax deductions like 401k and health insurance are subtracted.

FICA = Social Security (6.2%) + Medicare (1.45%) = 7.65% for employees. Your employer matches this amount. Self-employed individuals pay the full 15.3% as self-employment tax.

Pre-tax deductions reduce your taxable income before income taxes are calculated. Contributing $6,000 to a 401k lowers your taxable income by $6,000 — saving you the tax you would have paid on that amount based on your marginal rate.

Your effective tax rate is total taxes paid divided by gross income — the true average percentage of your earnings that goes to taxes. It is always lower than your marginal (bracket) rate since lower income is taxed at lower rates.
