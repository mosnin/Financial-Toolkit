Frequently Asked Questions

Markup is the percentage added to your cost to arrive at the selling price, while profit margin is the percentage of the selling price that is profit. For example, a product that costs $50 and sells for $100 has a 100% markup but a 50% profit margin. They describe the same relationship from different perspectives.

The break-even point is where total revenue equals total costs. Divide your fixed costs by (selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit) to get the number of units you need to sell. Our Break-Even Calculator handles this automatically and shows you a visual chart of the crossover point.

A healthy LTV to CAC ratio is typically 3:1 or higher, meaning each customer generates at least three times what it costs to acquire them. A ratio below 1:1 means you are losing money on each customer. Use our LTV and CAC calculators together to track this critical metric.

Most advisors recommend 12 to 18 months of runway after each funding round. This gives you enough time to hit milestones and raise the next round without making desperate decisions. Use our Burn Rate and Cash Runway calculators to monitor your position and plan ahead.

Start by calculating your target annual income, then add business expenses, taxes, and benefits you need to self-fund. Divide by your realistic billable hours per year (typically 1,000 to 1,500) to get your minimum hourly rate. Our Freelance Rate Calculator walks you through this step by step.
