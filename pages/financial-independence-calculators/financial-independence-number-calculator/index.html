Financial Independence Number Calculator

Your FI number is the specific dollar amount of invested assets that, once reached, lets your portfolio fund your lifestyle indefinitely — making work truly optional. This calculator derives your personal FI number from your annual retirement expenses and preferred safe withdrawal rate, with an optional inflation buffer. It also shows your required monthly passive income at FI and compares results across the most common SWR benchmarks.

How the FI Number Is Calculated

Core Formula
FI Number = Annual Expenses ÷ Safe Withdrawal Rate
Example: $60,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,500,000

With Inflation Buffer: FI Number × (1 + Buffer%)
Example: $1,500,000 × 1.10 = $1,650,000

Monthly Passive Income = FI Number × SWR ÷ 12

The safe withdrawal rate determines the multiple of annual expenses you must accumulate. A 4% SWR corresponds to the classic "25x rule." A 3.5% SWR implies ~28.6x your expenses, and a 3% SWR implies ~33.3x. Early retirees with a 40–50 year horizon often prefer the more conservative 3–3.5% targets to reduce sequence-of-returns risk.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual retirement expenses: Sum all expected yearly costs — housing, food, healthcare, travel, and leisure. Use today's dollars.
  2. Set your safe withdrawal rate: The default 4% is the classic benchmark. Consider 3–3.5% if retiring before 50.
  3. Add an inflation buffer (optional): A 10–20% buffer adds a safety margin for expense growth and market uncertainty.
  4. Review the SWR comparison table: See exactly how your FI number changes at each withdrawal rate scenario.

Example Scenario

Alex plans to spend $72,000 per year in retirement. Using the standard 4% SWR, Alex's FI number is $1,800,000 (25x expenses). At a more conservative 3.5% SWR, the target rises to $2,057,143 — about $257,143 beyond the basic 25x. With a 10% inflation buffer applied to the 4% SWR baseline, Alex's buffered FI target becomes $1,980,000. Monthly passive income at FI would be $6,000/month at the 4% rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Your FI number equals your annual expenses divided by your safe withdrawal rate. At 4% SWR this is 25x your annual expenses. At 3.5% it's about 28.6x, and at 3% it's about 33.3x.

The 4% rule targets a 30-year retirement. If you're retiring early (before 55), a 3–3.5% SWR is safer for a 40–50+ year horizon. A lower SWR gives a larger portfolio buffer against prolonged market downturns.

An inflation buffer adds a percentage on top of your base FI number as a safety margin. A 10% buffer means targeting 110% of your baseline FI number, providing cushion for rising healthcare costs, unexpected expenses, or a changing lifestyle in retirement.

The 25x rule is the 4% SWR baseline. "Beyond 25x" shows the additional amount you'd need at lower, more conservative withdrawal rates. For example, targeting 3% SWR means saving 33.3x expenses — 8.3x more than the basic 25x benchmark.
