Early Retirement Formula

The calculator simulates year-by-year portfolio drawdown with inflation-adjusted expenses:

Annual Simulation
Balance(n+1) = Balance(n) x (1 + return) - Expenses x (1 + inflation)^n

Each year, your portfolio grows by the expected return rate while you withdraw expenses that increase with inflation. The simulation continues until the balance reaches zero or you reach age 90.

How to Use This Early Retirement Calculator

  1. Enter your current age - the age at which you want to retire.
  2. Enter your current savings - total invested assets available for retirement.
  3. Enter annual expenses - your expected yearly spending in retirement.
  4. Enter expected annual return - your projected portfolio growth rate.
  5. Enter expected inflation - the annual rate at which prices increase.
  6. Click "Check Feasibility" to see if early retirement is viable for you.

Example Scenario

A 40-year-old with $800,000 in savings, $50,000 in annual expenses, 7% expected returns, and 3% inflation would see their real return at about 4%. With a 4% real withdrawal rate, the portfolio could sustain roughly 30+ years of withdrawals, lasting well past age 70 and potentially to 90 depending on market performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

A common rule is 25 times your annual expenses (the 4% rule). If you spend $50,000 per year, you need $1.25 million. For earlier retirement, you may want 30-33 times expenses to account for a longer retirement period and inflation.

FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) is a movement focused on extreme saving and investing to achieve financial independence decades before traditional retirement age. FIRE adherents typically save 50-70% of income and aim to retire in their 30s or 40s.

Inflation erodes purchasing power over time. At 3% inflation, $50,000 today will have the buying power of about $33,500 in 15 years. Early retirees face more years of inflation, making it crucial to factor into planning.

Key risks include sequence of returns risk, healthcare costs before Medicare at 65, longer exposure to inflation, and underestimating longevity. Building a buffer and having flexible spending helps mitigate these risks.

Options include Roth IRA contribution withdrawals (tax and penalty free), the Rule of 55 for 401(k) plans, 72(t) SEPP payments, and taxable brokerage accounts. Planning your account types is essential for early retirement.
