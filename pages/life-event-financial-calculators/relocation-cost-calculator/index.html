Relocation Cost Calculator

Moving to a new city involves far more than a moving truck — it means adjusting to a new cost of living, potentially a new salary, and a period of financial disruption during the transition. This calculator gives you the complete financial picture: total one-time relocation costs, how your monthly budget changes, and the true 3-year financial impact of your move.

Enter your current city's cost of living index versus your destination to see whether a pay raise actually puts you ahead, or whether a lower-cost city makes up for a smaller salary.

Methodology & Formulas

Cost of Living Adjustment
COL Ratio = Destination COL Index / Current COL Index
COL-Adjusted Effective Salary
Effective Salary = New Salary / COL Ratio
Monthly Cost Change
Monthly Change = (Monthly Expenses × COL Ratio) − Monthly Expenses
3-Year Net Impact
3-Year Impact = (Monthly Real Income Gain × 36) − Total Relocation Cost

Monthly expenses are estimated at 85% of current monthly net salary for calculation purposes. The COL ratio adjusts your estimated expenses to reflect the destination city's relative cost level.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Find COL index values — research your current and destination cities using cost of living databases. The US average is 100.
  2. Enter both salaries — if your salary is unchanged (remote work), enter the same value for both.
  3. Enter one-time moving costs — moving company, deposit, setup, and travel costs.
  4. Enter any job gap months — if you will be unemployed during the move, this costs you in lost income.
  5. Review the 3-year net impact — this is the most important number to evaluate whether the move makes financial sense.

Example Scenario

Jamie is moving from New York City (COL 187) to Nashville, TN (COL 94) for a job paying $90,000 versus their current NYC salary of $110,000. The COL ratio is 94/187 = 0.50, meaning Nashville costs roughly half as much. Jamie's COL-adjusted effective salary in Nashville is $90,000 / 0.50 = $180,000 equivalent — actually a massive raise in real terms. One-time move costs are $8,300. Monthly expenses drop by approximately $2,800. Break-even is just 3 months, and over 3 years Jamie is approximately $100,000 better off financially despite the nominal pay cut.
