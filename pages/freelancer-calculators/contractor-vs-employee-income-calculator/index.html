This calculator computes your true annual net income in both scenarios and tells you the break-even contractor rate — the minimum you must charge to come out ahead of a given salary.

Comparison Formula

Contractor Annual Net
Net = (Hourly Rate × Billable Hours - Business Expenses) × (1 - Tax Rate)
Employee Annual Net
Net = (Salary × (1 - Employee Tax Rate)) + Benefits Value
Break-Even Contractor Rate
Rate = Employee Total Need / (Billable Hours × (1 - Tax Rate))
Employee Total Need (with security premium)
Need = (Salary + Benefits) × (1 + Security Premium %)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your contractor hourly rate and estimated annual billable hours.
  2. Add annual business expenses and estimate your combined SE + income tax rate.
  3. Enter the employee salary you're comparing against and the full benefits value (health, 401k match, PTO).
  4. Set a job security premium — the additional compensation a contractor should earn to justify income risk.
  5. Click Compare Income to see true net income for both scenarios and your break-even rate.

Example Scenario

A contractor charges $85/hr for 1,800 billable hours with $8,000 in expenses and 35% taxes. The employee comparison is a $120,000 salary with $18,000 in benefits at 28% tax rate, plus 15% security premium.

  • Contractor Gross: $85 × 1,800 = $153,000
  • Contractor Net: ($153,000 - $8,000) × 0.65 = $94,250
  • Employee Net: $120,000 × 0.72 + $18,000 = $104,400
  • Employee Total Need (15% premium): $138,000 × 1.15 = $158,700
  • Break-Even Rate: $158,700 / (1,800 × 0.65) = $135.64/hr
  • Verdict: Contractor earns less — needs to raise rate or find more hours

Frequently Asked Questions

A contractor typically needs to earn 30-50% more than an equivalent salary to account for self-employment tax, benefits costs, and business expenses. Use this calculator to find your exact break-even rate.

Count employer health insurance ($7,000-$15,000/year), 401k match (3-6% of salary), paid time off (6-8% of salary equivalent), equipment, training budgets, and liability coverage.

Contractors pay both employee and employer FICA taxes — 15.3% on 92.35% of net income. Employees only pay the employee half (7.65%). This means a contractor earning $100,000 pays roughly $14,130 more in Social Security and Medicare than an employee.

A job security premium compensates for income gaps between contracts and the cost of finding new work. Most advisors recommend contractors factor in an additional 10-20% premium to account for this risk.
