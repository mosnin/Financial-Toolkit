Zero-Based Budget Calculator

A zero-based budget assigns every dollar of your income a specific job — whether that's rent, groceries, savings, or debt repayment — so that income minus all allocations equals exactly zero. This method gives you complete intentionality over your money and eliminates untracked spending. Use this calculator to build your monthly zero-based budget and instantly see your balance.

How Zero-Based Budgeting Works

Core Formula
Monthly Income − (Fixed Expenses + Variable Expenses + Savings) = $0

Savings Rate = (Emergency Fund + Retirement + Investments) / Total Income × 100
Budget Balance = Income − Total Allocated (target: exactly $0)

Every dollar must have a destination. If your balance is positive, you have unassigned money — allocate it to savings or debt payoff. If it's negative, you're overspending and must reduce category allocations until you reach zero.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your net income: Use your take-home pay after taxes, not gross income.
  2. Fill in every fixed expense: Include rent, insurance, and loan payments that don't change month to month.
  3. Estimate variable expenses: Use last month's bank statements for accurate figures on groceries, utilities, and dining.
  4. Assign savings: Treat savings as a non-negotiable expense category, not a leftover.
  5. Aim for zero: Adjust categories until the balance reads $0. Any surplus should go directly to savings or debt.

Example Zero-Based Budget

Alex earns $5,000/month take-home. Here is a balanced zero-based budget:

  • Housing: $1,400 (28%)
  • Groceries + Utilities: $550 (11%)
  • Transportation: $300 (6%)
  • Insurance + Subscriptions: $380 (7.6%)
  • Dining + Entertainment: $370 (7.4%)
  • Debt Payments: $500 (10%)
  • Savings + Retirement: $500 (10%)
  • Other: $1,000 (20%)

Total allocated: $5,000. Balance: $0. Every dollar has a purpose.

Frequently Asked Questions

A zero-based budget assigns every dollar of your monthly income to a specific category so that income minus all allocations equals zero. This does not mean spending everything — savings and investments are also assigned categories, ensuring no dollar is left unaccounted for.

Unlike the 50/30/20 rule or envelope method, zero-based budgeting starts from zero each month and forces you to justify every dollar you allocate. This makes it more intentional and precise, though it requires more upfront effort to set up correctly each month.

If you have leftover income (positive balance), allocate that surplus to savings, an emergency fund, or extra debt payments. If expenses exceed income (negative balance), reduce spending in discretionary categories like dining out, entertainment, or subscriptions until the budget balances.

Zero-based budgets should be rebuilt each month because income and expenses change. Variable costs like utilities, groceries, and entertainment fluctuate, so a fresh budget each month gives you the most accurate picture and keeps you in full control of your finances.
