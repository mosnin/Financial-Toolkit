Carbon Footprint Formula

Emissions Factors Used
Electricity: 0.386 kg CO2/kWh (US average grid mix) Natural Gas: 5.3 kg CO2/therm Driving: 0.404 kg CO2/mile (avg US passenger vehicle) Flights: ~0.255 metric tons CO2 per round trip (avg 4-hr flight) Diet: Vegan 1.5t | Vegetarian 1.7t | Low-Meat 2.5t | Average 3.3t | Meat-Heavy 4.5t/year Annual CO2 (tons) = Sum of all category emissions / 1,000 Offset Cost = Annual CO2 tons × $20

Emissions factors are based on EPA and IPCC guidelines. The electricity factor reflects the US average grid; your actual factor may differ based on your state's energy mix.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly electricity usage -- find this on your electric bill in kWh.
  2. Enter monthly natural gas usage -- in therms, found on your gas bill.
  3. Enter annual miles driven -- check your odometer or estimate from your commute.
  4. Enter flights per year -- count round trips of average length.
  5. Select your diet type -- choose the option that best matches your eating habits.
  6. Click "Calculate Footprint" to see your total emissions and category breakdown.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average American produces approximately 16 metric tons of CO2 equivalent per year. The global average is about 4 metric tons. Scientists suggest we need to reach below 2 tons per person by 2050 to limit warming to 1.5°C.

A carbon offset is a reduction in CO2 emissions made elsewhere to compensate for emissions you produce. Examples include investing in renewable energy projects, reforestation, or methane capture. Carbon offsets are typically priced at $10-$50 per metric ton of CO2.

For most Americans, the biggest carbon contributors are transportation (especially driving and flying), home energy use (heating, cooling, electricity), and food production (especially beef and dairy). Reducing car miles, cutting flights, and shifting toward a plant-based diet can significantly reduce your footprint.

This calculator uses EPA and IPCC emissions factors and provides a reasonable estimate for personal carbon awareness. Actual emissions vary by electricity grid mix, vehicle efficiency, flight class, and regional factors. It is designed for educational awareness rather than certified carbon accounting.
