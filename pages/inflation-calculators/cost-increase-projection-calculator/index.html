Cost Increase Projection Calculator

Whether it's rent, healthcare, tuition, or groceries, most costs increase by some percentage every year. This calculator projects any current cost forward at an annual increase rate, generates a detailed table for 1, 3, 5, 10, and 20 years, calculates the total extra you will spend over the entire period, and translates that into a monthly budget impact figure so you can plan effectively.

Cost Projection Formula

Formulas Used
Projected Cost (Year N) = Current Cost × (1 + Annual Increase Rate)^N

Total Extra Spend = Σ(Year 1 to N: Projected Cost − Current Cost)

Monthly Budget Impact = Total Extra Spend ÷ (Years × 12)

Total % Increase = ((Final Cost − Current Cost) / Current Cost) × 100

The compound growth formula shows how costs escalate non-linearly over time. Total extra spend sums up all the additional dollars paid above today's flat cost across every year. The monthly budget impact figure is particularly useful for planning how much to set aside each month to absorb future cost increases.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the current annual cost: For monthly costs (like rent or insurance), multiply by 12 to get the annual figure.
  2. Set the annual increase rate: Use historical data for your cost category — see the FAQ for typical rates by category.
  3. Set the projection period: Up to 50 years. Try 20 years to see the long-term picture.
  4. Review the milestone table: Quickly see costs at 1, 3, 5, 10, and 20 years.
  5. Use the full table: Year-by-year data helps with detailed budgeting and retirement planning.

Example Scenario

Your current health insurance premium is $4,800 per year ($400/month). Healthcare costs increase at 6% per year. Over 20 years:

  • Cost at year 1: $5,088 ($424/mo)
  • Cost at year 5: $6,422 ($535/mo)
  • Cost at year 10: $8,598 ($717/mo)
  • Cost at year 20: $15,395 ($1,283/mo)
  • Total extra spend over 20 years: Approximately $82,000 more than if costs stayed flat
  • Monthly budget impact: ~$342/month average extra over the 20-year period

Without planning for this increase, a major portion of your retirement budget will be consumed by healthcare cost inflation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Use the compound growth formula: Future Cost = Current Cost × (1 + Annual Increase Rate)^Years. This accounts for compounding — each year's increase is applied to the previous year's higher price, not the original price.

Total extra spend is the cumulative additional amount you will pay over a period because of annual cost increases, compared to if the cost had stayed flat. It is calculated by summing each year's cost and subtracting what you would have paid at the original flat cost.

General inflation: 2–4%. Rent: 3–6%. Healthcare: 5–8%. College tuition: 5–7%. Groceries: 2–5%. Utilities: 3–5%. Car insurance: 4–7%. Use the actual historical rate for the specific cost category you are projecting for the most accurate results.

Monthly budget impact translates the cumulative extra spend into a monthly average, making it easier to plan. For instance, knowing that healthcare inflation will cost you an extra $150/month on average over 10 years helps you decide how much extra to set aside each month now.
