Business Tax Formulas by Entity Type

Sole Proprietorship / Single-Member LLC
Net Profit = Revenue − Expenses
SE Tax = Net Profit × 0.9235 × 15.3%
AGI = Net Profit − (SE Tax / 2) − QBI Deduction (up to 20%)
Income Tax = Applied to AGI at personal brackets
S-Corporation
Net Profit = Revenue − Expenses
Payroll Tax on Salary = Salary × 15.3% (employer + employee share)
Remaining Distribution = Net Profit − Salary (no SE tax)
Owner pays income tax on both salary and distribution
C-Corporation
Corporate Taxable Income = Revenue − Expenses
Corporate Tax = Taxable Income × 21%
(Dividends paid to owners taxed again at personal rate — double taxation)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your business structure from the dropdown.
  2. Enter your gross annual revenue.
  3. Enter all deductible business expenses (COGS, rent, payroll, etc.).
  4. For S-Corps, enter the owner's reasonable salary.
  5. Select the owner's filing status for pass-through income tax calculation.
  6. Click Calculate Tax Liability.

Example Scenario

A freelance consultant with $200,000 revenue and $50,000 in deductible expenses, filing as sole proprietor (single):

  • Net Profit: $200,000 − $50,000 = $150,000
  • SE Tax: $150,000 × 0.9235 × 15.3% ≈ $21,215
  • Deductible SE: $21,215 / 2 = $10,608
  • QBI Deduction (20%): ~$27,878
  • AGI: $150,000 − $10,608 − $27,878 = $111,514
  • Federal Income Tax (after std deduction): ~$17,400
  • Total Tax: $21,215 + $17,400 = ~$38,615
  • Effective Rate: ~19.3% of net profit

Frequently Asked Questions

C-Corporations are taxed at a flat 21% federal corporate income tax rate on all taxable income, regardless of income level, under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act.

By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship (pass-through), and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership. The owner pays income tax and self-employment tax on business profits on their personal return.

The Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction allows eligible self-employed and pass-through business owners to deduct up to 20% of qualified business income, subject to income limits and phase-outs.

An S-Corp election can save on self-employment taxes once net income exceeds roughly $40,000–$50,000 per year, because only a 'reasonable salary' is subject to payroll taxes — remaining profit is distributed tax-free from SE tax.
