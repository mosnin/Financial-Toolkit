Inflation Calculators

Inflation silently erodes purchasing power, savings, and retirement security over time, making it one of the most important forces in personal financial planning. Our inflation calculators help you understand exactly how rising prices affect your salary, savings goals, retirement nest egg, rent, tuition, and everyday costs like groceries and healthcare. By modeling inflation-adjusted figures rather than nominal ones, you can set more realistic targets and avoid the trap of feeling financially secure today while falling behind in real terms. Use these tools to stress-test your financial plan against a range of inflation scenarios and build a strategy that holds up over the long run.

Frequently Asked Questions

The US inflation rate has averaged approximately 3.3% per year since 1914, though the Federal Reserve targets a 2% long-run rate. Inflation spiked significantly in the early 1980s and again in 2021–2023. For long-range financial planning, using a 2.5–3% assumption provides a conservative buffer above the Fed's target.

At 3% annual inflation, $1 million today will have the purchasing power of only about $412,000 in 30 years. This means retirement savers must aim for a nominal portfolio value significantly higher than they think they need. Our Inflation-Adjusted Retirement Calculator shows your required savings target in today's dollars so you can set a meaningful goal.

Healthcare inflation consistently runs 1–2 percentage points above general CPI due to factors including technological innovation costs, aging demographics, administrative complexity, and limited market competition. Over a 20-year retirement, retirees who budget only for general inflation often find healthcare expenses significantly exceed their projections.

To keep pace with inflation, your annual raises must at minimum match the CPI increase each year. If inflation runs at 4% and you receive a 2% raise, your real purchasing power has declined by roughly 2%. Use our Inflation-Adjusted Salary Calculator to enter your pay history and see whether your income has grown in real terms over time.

College tuition has historically inflated at roughly 4–6% per year, significantly outpacing general inflation. At a 5% annual increase, a degree that costs $50,000 per year today would cost about $81,000 per year in 10 years and $132,000 per year in 20 years. Our Inflation-Adjusted Tuition Calculator helps families start saving with an accurate future cost target.
