Car Affordability Formula

This calculator estimates your maximum car price using your desired payment, a standard interest rate, and loan term:

Maximum Financed Amount
Max Financed = Payment x [(1 + r)^n - 1] / [r(1 + r)^n]
Maximum Car Price
Max Price = Max Financed Amount + Down Payment

Where r is the assumed monthly interest rate (6.5% APR / 12) and n is 60 months. The recommended budget uses a conservative 10% of income rule.

How to Use This Car Affordability Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly income - your take-home pay after taxes.
  2. Enter existing debts - total monthly payments for other debts (credit cards, student loans, etc.).
  3. Enter your desired payment - the monthly car payment you feel comfortable with.
  4. Enter your down payment - how much cash you can put down.
  5. Click "Calculate Affordability" to see your maximum price and budget recommendation.

Example Scenario

With a monthly income of $5,000, existing debts of $500, a desired payment of $400, and a $5,000 down payment, assuming 6.5% APR for 60 months, you could afford a car priced up to about $25,600. The conservative recommendation (10% of income = $500/month payment) would allow a car up to roughly $30,300.

Frequently Asked Questions

A common rule of thumb is to spend no more than 10-15% of your monthly take-home pay on car payments. Your total vehicle expenses (payment, insurance, fuel, maintenance) should stay under 20% of your income.

The 20/4/10 rule suggests putting at least 20% down, financing for no more than 4 years, and keeping total transportation costs under 10% of gross income. This conservative approach helps avoid being upside-down on a car loan.

A larger down payment means you need to finance less, allowing you to either buy a more expensive vehicle within the same monthly budget or keep your monthly payments lower. It also reduces total interest paid and helps avoid negative equity.
