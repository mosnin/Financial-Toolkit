Solar Savings Formula

Solar ROI Calculations
Annual kWh Production = System Size (kW) × 1,400 kWh/kW/yr (US avg) Annual Savings = MIN(Annual kWh × Rate, Annual Electric Bill) Federal Tax Credit = Installation Cost × 30% Net System Cost = Installation Cost − Tax Credit − State Incentives Payback Period = Net System Cost / Annual Savings 25-Year Net Savings = (Annual Savings × 25) − Net System Cost CO2 Offset = Annual kWh × 0.386 kg CO2/kWh / 1,000 (tons)

Annual production uses 1,400 kWh/kW as a national average. Actual production varies from ~1,000 kWh/kW in the Pacific Northwest to ~1,700+ kWh/kW in the Southwest. A solar installer can provide a site-specific production estimate using satellite data.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly electric bill -- your average monthly electricity cost.
  2. Enter system size in kW -- a typical home system is 6-10 kW; your installer will size this for your usage.
  3. Enter total installation cost -- get quotes from local installers for the most accurate figure.
  4. Enter state/local incentives -- check dsireusa.org for incentives in your state.
  5. Enter your electricity rate -- found on your utility bill in $/kWh.
  6. Click "Calculate Solar Savings" to see payback period, annual savings, and 25-year ROI.

Example Scenario

8 kW System for $150/Month Electric Bill

Monthly bill: $150 | System: 8 kW | Install cost: $22,000 | State incentive: $1,000 | Rate: $0.16/kWh

Annual Production = 8 kW × 1,400 = 11,200 kWh

Annual Savings = MIN(11,200 × $0.16, $1,800) = $1,792/year

Federal Tax Credit = $22,000 × 30% = $6,600

Net Cost = $22,000 − $6,600 − $1,000 = $14,400

Payback = $14,400 / $1,792 = 8.0 years

25-Year Net Savings = ($1,792 × 25) − $14,400 = $30,400

Frequently Asked Questions

The average residential solar system costs $15,000 to $30,000 before incentives. A typical 6-8 kW system costs about $18,000-$24,000 before the 30% federal tax credit, bringing the net cost to $12,600-$16,800.

The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) for residential solar is 30% of the total system cost through 2032, then steps down to 26% in 2033 and 22% in 2034. There is no cap on system size for residential installations.

Most solar panels come with 25-year performance warranties and typically last 30+ years. Panel efficiency degrades slightly over time -- typically 0.5-0.7% per year. After 25 years, panels generally still produce 80-85% of their original rated output.

Yes, solar panels still generate electricity on cloudy days, though at reduced output -- typically 10-25% of full capacity. Overcast regions still benefit from solar, though systems in sunny states produce significantly more power annually than those in cloudy regions.
