Retirement Budget Formula

The calculator sums all monthly expense categories and applies inflation compounding to show future costs:

Annual Year 1 Cost
Annual Cost = Sum of all monthly expenses x 12
Inflation-Adjusted Future Cost
Annual Cost at Year N = Annual Cost x (1 + inflation rate)^N
Total 20-Year Cost
Sum of annual costs for years 1 through 20, each inflation-adjusted

How to Use This Lifestyle Cost Calculator

  1. Enter estimated monthly amounts for each spending category in retirement.
  2. Be honest about travel and healthcare — these are commonly underestimated categories.
  3. Set your inflation rate - 3% is a common assumption for general expenses; healthcare inflates faster at 5%+.
  4. Click "Calculate Retirement Budget" to see your total monthly budget, annual cost, and 20-year projections.
  5. Use the results to feed into the Nest Egg Calculator or Income Replacement Calculator.

Example Scenario

A couple retiring in an average-cost city budgets $1,800/month for housing, $700 for food, $600 for healthcare, $400 for transportation, $600 for travel, $400 for entertainment, and $300 for other — totaling $4,800/month or $57,600/year. At 3% inflation, that same lifestyle costs $77,700/year by year 10 and $103,800/year by year 20. Total 20-year inflation-adjusted spending exceeds $1.57 million, guiding their required nest egg calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, households age 65 and older spend an average of about $4,800 per month ($57,600 annually). Actual spending varies from under $2,500/month for frugal retirees in low-cost areas to $10,000+/month for active retirees in high-cost cities.

For most retirees: housing (30-35%), healthcare (15-20%), food (12-15%), transportation (10-12%), and entertainment/travel (8-12%). Housing and healthcare tend to be the most volatile and often-underestimated categories.

At 3% annual inflation, a $5,000/month budget in year one becomes $9,031/month by year 20. Your retirement portfolio must grow over time to keep pace, making inflation planning one of the most important aspects of retirement budgeting.

Spending in retirement often follows a 'smile' curve: higher in early active years (travel, hobbies), lower in the middle years, and higher again later due to healthcare. A flat budget is simpler but may not reflect your personal spending trajectory.
