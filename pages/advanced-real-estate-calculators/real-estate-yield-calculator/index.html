Real Estate Yield Formula

Yield Calculations
Gross Yield = (Annual Rental Income / Property Price) × 100 Net Operating Income (NOI) = Annual Rental Income − Annual Expenses Net Yield = (NOI / Property Price) × 100 Appreciation Yield = Annual Appreciation Rate % Total Return = Net Yield + Appreciation Yield

Gross yield gives a quick top-line view of income performance without deducting costs. It is useful for initial screening but overstates actual returns.

Net yield is a more honest measure, reflecting the income left after operating costs. It excludes financing costs (mortgage), making it comparable across properties regardless of how they are financed.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual rental income -- the total rent collected per year.
  2. Enter property price -- the purchase price or current market value.
  3. Enter annual expenses -- all operating costs excluding mortgage payments.
  4. Enter annual appreciation % -- expected average annual property value growth.
  5. Click "Calculate Yield" to see gross yield, net yield, and total return with benchmark comparisons.

Example Scenario

$400,000 Property Generating $24,000 Annual Rent

A $400,000 rental property generates $24,000/year in rent with $8,000 in annual expenses and 3.5% expected appreciation.

Gross Yield = $24,000 / $400,000 = 6.00%

NOI = $24,000 − $8,000 = $16,000

Net Yield = $16,000 / $400,000 = 4.00%

Total Return = 4.00% + 3.50% = 7.50%

This total return of 7.5% compares favorably to 10-year Treasuries (~4.3%) and approaches historical S&P 500 averages when leverage is applied.

Frequently Asked Questions

Gross yield is the annual rental income as a percentage of the property price, with no deductions. Net yield deducts all annual operating expenses from the rental income before calculating the percentage. Net yield is a more accurate measure of actual returns.

A net yield of 4-6% is generally considered acceptable for residential rental properties in established markets. Yields above 7-8% may indicate higher risk, a less desirable area, or an undervalued property. Prime city center properties often yield 2-4% net.

Total return combines both the income yield (net yield) and the capital appreciation yield. A property with a 4% net yield and 3% annual appreciation delivers a 7% total return, which is competitive with many equity investments.

The S&P 500 has historically returned approximately 10% annually. Real estate total returns including appreciation typically range from 7-12%, though with more control, tax advantages, and the ability to use leverage. Direct comparison requires accounting for leverage, taxes, and liquidity differences.
