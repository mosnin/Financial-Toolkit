Debt-to-Wealth Ratio Calculator

Your debt-to-wealth ratio is one of the clearest signals of financial health. It compares everything you owe to everything you own — and when that ratio drops below 1.0, you have crossed into positive net worth territory. This calculator shows your current ratio, visualizes it on a progress bar, and projects a month-by-month timeline to the moment your net worth turns positive, factoring in both debt paydown and asset growth.

Debt-to-Wealth Ratio Formula

The debt-to-wealth ratio divides total liabilities by total assets to produce a simple decimal that signals where you stand relative to financial independence:

Debt-to-Wealth Ratio
Ratio = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets
Net Worth
Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Where r is the annual asset return rate and m is months elapsed. Debts decrease by the monthly paydown amount each period.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter total assets — sum all savings, investments, property values, and other owned assets at current market value.
  2. Enter total liabilities — all outstanding balances: mortgage, auto loans, student loans, credit cards, and any other debts.
  3. Enter monthly debt paydown — the extra amount you apply to reducing debt principal each month beyond minimum interest.
  4. Enter monthly savings — how much you add to savings or investments each month.
  5. Optionally enter annual asset return rate — expected growth rate on your existing asset base.
  6. Click "Calculate Ratio" to see your ratio, net worth status, and a year-by-year progress table.

Example Scenario

Suppose you have $80,000 in assets (savings, 401k, car) and $95,000 in liabilities (student loans, car loan, credit cards). Your debt-to-wealth ratio is 1.19 — meaning you owe 19% more than you own, a negative net worth of -$15,000. If you pay down $600 in debt and add $400 to savings each month with a 6% annual return on assets, this calculator projects you will cross into positive net worth in approximately 14 months and reach a 0.50 ratio within five years.

Frequently Asked Questions

A debt-to-wealth ratio below 1.0 means your assets exceed your liabilities, which is the foundation of positive net worth. A ratio of 0.5 or lower is generally considered healthy, meaning debts are no more than half of total assets. A ratio above 1.0 indicates negative net worth — your debts exceed what you own.

Divide your total liabilities (all debts, loans, and obligations) by your total assets (everything you own at current market value). For example, $60,000 in debt divided by $100,000 in assets equals a ratio of 0.60. A ratio below 1.0 means positive net worth; above 1.0 means negative net worth.

Include all assets at current market value: checking and savings accounts, investment and retirement accounts (401k, IRA, brokerage), real estate equity, vehicle values, and other valuable property. Use realistic current market values, not purchase prices.

The timeline depends on three factors: how much you reduce debt each month, how much your assets grow through savings and investment returns, and the gap between your current net worth and zero. This calculator models both scenarios to give you a projected month and year when you will cross into positive net worth territory.
