Divorce Cost Calculator

Understand the full financial picture of divorce before, during, and after the process. This calculator helps you estimate how joint assets and debts will be divided, how your monthly income and expenses will change, and how long it will take to reach financial stability on your own.

Unlike general legal cost estimators, this tool focuses on long-term financial planning — giving you a realistic view of your post-divorce budget and recovery timeline so you can make informed decisions during settlement negotiations.

Methodology & Formulas

This calculator uses income-based proportional allocation combined with standard equitable distribution principles:

Net Marital Estate
Net Estate = Total Joint Assets − Total Joint Debts
Income-Weighted Settlement Share
Your Share % = 50% ± (Income Disparity Adjustment up to 10%)
Monthly Income After Divorce
Post-Divorce Income = (Your Annual Income / 12) + Child Support + Alimony
Recovery Timeline
Recovery Months = Total Transition Costs / Monthly Surplus (Income − Solo Expenses)

Solo monthly expenses are estimated at 65% of the former combined household expenses, reflecting the reality that individual living costs are higher per person but lower overall than shared costs.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter joint assets — include home equity, joint bank accounts, investments, and retirement accounts accumulated during the marriage.
  2. Enter joint debts — mortgage balance, auto loans, credit card debt, and any other marital liabilities.
  3. Enter both incomes — use gross annual figures. This affects the income-weighted settlement allocation.
  4. Enter child support and alimony estimates — use positive numbers if you receive them, negative if you pay.
  5. Enter transition costs — legal fees plus housing setup costs to establish your post-divorce home.
  6. Review your results — use the monthly budget adjustment figure to understand how you need to restructure spending.

Example Scenario

Alex and Jordan are divorcing. They have $350,000 in joint assets (home equity + retirement) and $120,000 in joint debts, leaving a net estate of $230,000. Alex earns $65,000/year and Jordan earns $95,000/year. Alex is estimated to receive child support of $800/month. Legal fees are $8,000 and housing transition costs are $5,000. Their combined household expenses are $6,000/month.

The calculator estimates Alex's settlement share at roughly $110,000–$120,000 (with income-weighted adjustment), a monthly income change of approximately +$300 after child support, solo monthly expenses of $3,900, and a financial recovery timeline of approximately 14 months to recoup transition costs. Alex would need to reduce solo spending by about $250/month compared to the shared household contribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most states follow equitable distribution, meaning marital assets are divided fairly but not necessarily 50/50. Some states are community property states where a 50/50 split is the default. The actual split depends on income disparity, custody arrangements, length of marriage, and each spouse's financial contributions.

Financial recovery timelines vary widely. For someone losing significant income or assets, recovery can take 3–7 years. Key factors include your post-divorce income, whether you receive or pay alimony, child support obligations, and how quickly you can adjust your living expenses to your new income level.

Beyond legal fees, hidden costs include setting up a new household (furniture, deposits, utilities), tax filing status changes that reduce deductions, loss of employer benefits from a spouse's plan, reduced retirement savings from splitting assets, and the emotional toll that can affect work productivity.

A Certified Divorce Financial Analyst (CDFA) or financial planner can help you understand the long-term implications of proposed settlements, evaluate pension and retirement account divisions, assess tax consequences, and build a post-divorce financial plan. Many people accept settlements that look fair today but are disadvantageous over 10–20 years.
