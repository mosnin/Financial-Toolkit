Premium Estimation Formula

Term and whole life premiums are estimated based on age and coverage amount:

Term Life Premium
Annual Term Premium = (Coverage / 1,000) x Base Rate x Age Factor x Term Factor
Whole Life Premium
Annual Whole Life Premium = (Coverage / 1,000) x Base Rate x Age Factor x Whole Life Multiplier

Whole life premiums are typically 5-15 times higher than comparable term premiums because they include lifetime coverage and a cash value savings component.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your age - younger applicants receive lower rates.
  2. Enter coverage amount - the death benefit you want.
  3. Select term length - how long you need coverage (for the term policy comparison).

Example Scenario

A 35-year-old seeking $500,000 in coverage for 20 years might pay approximately $30 per month for term life versus $350 per month for whole life. Over 20 years, term would cost about $7,200 total while whole life would cost about $84,000 - a difference of $76,800. Investing the monthly savings from term could potentially build significant wealth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Term life provides coverage for a specific period at lower premiums. Whole life provides lifetime coverage with cash value but costs significantly more. Term is pure protection while whole life combines insurance with savings.

Whole life costs more because it provides lifetime coverage, builds cash value, and guarantees a death benefit. Premiums are typically 5-15 times higher than term life for the same coverage amount.

For most people, term life offers the best value for pure protection. Whole life may make sense for estate planning or guaranteed inheritance. Many advisors recommend buying term and investing the difference.

Many term policies include a conversion option allowing you to convert to whole life without a medical exam. This can be valuable if your health changes during the term period.
