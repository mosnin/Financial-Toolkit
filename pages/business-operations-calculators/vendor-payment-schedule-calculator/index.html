Vendor Payment Formulas

These calculations help you weigh the cost-benefit of different payment timing choices.

Early Payment Amount
Early Payment = Invoice Amount x (1 - Early Discount %)
Early Payment Savings
Savings = Invoice Amount x Early Discount %
Annualized Discount Return
Annualized Return = (Discount % / (1 - Discount %)) x (365 / Days Saved) x 100
Late Payment Cost (1 month late)
Late Cost = Invoice Amount x Monthly Late Fee % / 100

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the invoice amount -- the total amount owed to the vendor.
  2. Select payment terms -- the standard due date (net 30, 60, or 90).
  3. Enter early payment discount -- the percentage discount offered for paying early (leave blank or 0 if none).
  4. Enter days early to qualify -- how many days before the due date you must pay to receive the discount.
  5. Enter late fee percentage -- the monthly fee charged on overdue balances (leave blank or 0 if none).
  6. Click "Analyze Payment Schedule" to see your optimal payment strategy.

Example Scenario

Your vendor sends a $10,000 invoice with net 30 terms. They offer a 2% discount if you pay within 10 days and charge a 1.5% monthly late fee.

  • Early Payment Amount: $10,000 x (1 - 2%) = $9,800
  • Early Payment Savings: $200
  • Annualized Return on Discount: (2%/98%) x (365/20 days saved) = ~37.2% annualized -- almost always worth taking
  • Late Payment Cost (1 month late): $10,000 x 1.5% = $150 extra

In this case, paying early saves $200 and delivers a 37% annualized return on the cash deployed. Unless you have a critical use for that $9,800, the early payment is the financially optimal choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Net 30 means the full invoice amount is due within 30 days of the invoice date. Net 60 and Net 90 work similarly with 60 and 90-day payment windows respectively.

2/10 net 30 means you can take a 2% discount if you pay within 10 days, otherwise the full amount is due in 30 days. This is one of the most common early payment discount structures in B2B trade.

Take an early payment discount if the annualized return on the discount exceeds your cost of capital. A 2% discount for paying 20 days early annualizes to about 36% -- almost always worth taking if you have the cash.

Late payments typically incur late fees, often 1-3% per month or a flat fee. Repeated late payments can damage supplier relationships, reduce credit terms offered, and impact your business reputation.
