Frequently Asked Questions

The 50/30/20 rule suggests allocating 50% of after-tax income to needs (housing, food, utilities), 30% to wants (entertainment, dining out, hobbies), and 20% to savings and debt repayment. It is a flexible guideline, not a rigid rule. Our Budget Percentage Calculator lets you apply this framework or customize your own allocation percentages.

The debt snowball pays off debts from smallest balance to largest for psychological wins and motivation. The debt avalanche pays off debts from highest interest rate to lowest to minimize total interest paid. The avalanche saves more money mathematically, but the snowball helps people stay on track behaviorally. Use our calculators to compare both strategies for your specific debts.

Most financial experts recommend saving 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses in a liquid, accessible account. If you are self-employed, have variable income, or work in a volatile industry, 6 to 12 months is more appropriate. Use our Emergency Savings Timeline Calculator to set your target and determine how long it will take to reach it at your current savings pace.

At a 10% savings rate you would typically need around 40 years to reach financial independence. At 50% you could potentially retire in about 17 years. The exact timeline depends on your investment returns and the 4% safe withdrawal rate assumption. Our Financial Independence Calculator shows you the precise timeline based on your income, expenses, and savings rate.

Your take-home pay is your gross salary minus federal and state income taxes, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), health insurance premiums, and any retirement contributions. These deductions typically reduce your paycheck by 25% to 35%. Our Net Income Calculator accounts for all deductions to show your real monthly and annual take-home pay.
