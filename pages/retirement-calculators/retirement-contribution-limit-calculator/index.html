How to Use This Contribution Limit Calculator

  1. Enter your age - determines catch-up contribution eligibility (age 50+, ages 60-63).
  2. Select your employment type - self-employed individuals have access to Solo 401(k) and SEP-IRA options.
  3. Check all account types you have or plan to open - each has its own independent limit.
  4. Select your HSA coverage type if you have an HSA-eligible health plan.
  5. Click "Show My 2026 Limits" to see a breakdown and your total maximum contribution.

Example Scenario

A 52-year-old W-2 employee with a 401(k), Traditional IRA, and family HSA coverage can contribute: $31,000 to their 401(k) ($23,500 + $7,500 catch-up), $8,000 to their IRA ($7,000 + $1,000 catch-up), and $9,550 to their HSA ($8,550 + $1,000 catch-up) for a total maximum of $48,550 in tax-advantaged retirement and health savings in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

For 2026, the 401(k) employee contribution limit is $23,500. If you are age 50 or older, you can make an additional $7,500 catch-up contribution for a total of $31,000. Employees aged 60-63 may be eligible for an enhanced catch-up of $11,250 under SECURE 2.0.

The 2026 IRA contribution limit is $7,000 per year. If you are age 50 or older, you can contribute an extra $1,000 catch-up contribution for a total of $8,000. Income limits apply to Roth IRA contributions and deductible Traditional IRA contributions.

For 2026, the HSA contribution limit is $4,300 for self-only coverage and $8,550 for family coverage. If you are age 55 or older, you can add a $1,000 catch-up contribution. You must be enrolled in a qualifying High Deductible Health Plan to contribute.

Yes, you can contribute to both a 401(k) and an IRA in the same year. These limits are independent of each other. However, Traditional IRA deductibility depends on your income and workplace plan coverage. Roth IRA contributions have their own income phase-out limits.
