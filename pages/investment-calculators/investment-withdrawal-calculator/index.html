Withdrawal Projection Formula

Each year, the portfolio earns returns on its current balance and then pays out the fixed annual withdrawal:

Annual Withdrawal Amount
Withdrawal = Starting Portfolio × (Annual Withdrawal % / 100)
Year-End Balance
Balance(n) = Balance(n-1) × (1 + Annual Return %) − Annual Withdrawal

The portfolio is depleted when the balance reaches zero or goes negative.

How to Use This Withdrawal Calculator

  1. Enter your portfolio value — your total investment balance at the start.
  2. Enter the annual withdrawal % — the percentage of your starting portfolio you plan to withdraw each year. The common guideline is 4%.
  3. Enter the annual return % — your expected average portfolio return after inflation (often 5–7% for a balanced portfolio).
  4. Enter projection years — how many years you want to see projected (e.g., 30 for a typical retirement).
  5. Click "Calculate Longevity" to see the annual withdrawal, end balance, and year-by-year table.

Example Scenario

Suppose you retire with a $500,000 portfolio, plan to withdraw 4% ($20,000) per year, and expect a 6% annual return. After 30 years, your portfolio would still have approximately $678,000 — well above zero. Increasing the withdrawal to 6% ($30,000) with the same 6% return would deplete the portfolio around year 38. Reducing the return assumption to 4% with a 4% withdrawal rate would result in a flat balance over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

The 4% rule is a guideline suggesting that retirees can withdraw 4% of their portfolio in the first year of retirement, then adjust that amount for inflation each year, and the portfolio should last at least 30 years. It was derived from the Trinity Study based on historical stock and bond returns.

A safe withdrawal rate is the percentage of a portfolio you can withdraw annually without running out of money over a long retirement. The traditional figure is 4%, but many financial planners now suggest 3–3.5% given lower expected returns and longer life expectancies.

If the annual withdrawal exceeds the portfolio's annual growth, the portfolio will shrink each year. Eventually the balance reaches zero. The higher the withdrawal rate relative to returns, the faster the portfolio is depleted.

In practice, many retirees adjust withdrawals annually for inflation to maintain purchasing power. More sophisticated strategies include dynamic spending rules where withdrawals are reduced in down years and increased in strong years. This calculator uses a fixed percentage withdrawal for simplicity.
