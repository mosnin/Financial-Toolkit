EV Charging Cost Formula

EV charging costs are calculated based on energy consumption:

Daily Charging Cost
Daily Cost = (Daily Miles / Miles per kWh) x Electricity Rate per kWh
EV Cost Per Mile
Cost Per Mile = Electricity Rate / Miles per kWh

How to Use This EV Charging Cost Calculator

  1. Enter your battery size - the total capacity in kWh (e.g., 60 kWh for a Tesla Model 3).
  2. Enter your electricity rate - what you pay per kWh (check your utility bill).
  3. Enter your EV efficiency - miles per kWh (typically 3-4 for most EVs).
  4. Enter daily miles - how many miles you drive each day.
  5. Optionally enter gas details - for a side-by-side comparison with a gas vehicle.
  6. Click "Calculate Charging Cost" to see your results.

Example Scenario

Suppose you drive an EV with a 60 kWh battery that gets 3.5 miles per kWh, you drive 35 miles daily, and your electricity costs $0.14/kWh. Your daily charging cost would be $1.40, monthly cost about $42.60, and annual cost about $518. Compared to a 25 MPG gas car at $3.50/gallon, the gas cost would be $1,788 per year, saving you about $1,270 annually with the EV.

Miles per kWh is the EV equivalent of MPG. It measures how far an electric vehicle can travel on one kilowatt-hour of electricity. Most modern EVs get between 3 and 4 miles per kWh, though this varies with driving conditions and temperature.

Home charging is typically much cheaper, averaging $0.10-$0.16 per kWh. Public Level 2 chargers usually cost $0.20-$0.35 per kWh, and DC fast chargers can cost $0.30-$0.60 per kWh. Home charging overnight on off-peak rates can be even cheaper.

Using a Level 2 home charger (240V), most EVs charge from empty to full in 6-10 hours, making overnight charging convenient. A standard 120V outlet (Level 1) adds only 3-5 miles of range per hour and is much slower.
