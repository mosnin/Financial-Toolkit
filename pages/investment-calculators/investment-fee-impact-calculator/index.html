Fee Impact Formula

The fee impact is modeled by comparing compound growth at the gross return rate versus the net return rate (gross minus advisor fee):

Portfolio Without Fees
Value = Portfolio × (1 + Annual Return %)^Years
Portfolio With Fees
Value = Portfolio × (1 + Annual Return % − Advisor Fee %)^Years

The total fees paid is the difference, representing the compounded opportunity cost of the advisor fee.

Example Scenario

Suppose you have a $250,000 portfolio earning 7% per year and paying a 1% advisor fee for 20 years. Without fees, your portfolio would grow to approximately $966,850. With the 1% annual fee (net return 6%), it would grow to only $801,784 — a difference of $165,066. That $165,066 represents the compounded cost of the 1% fee over two decades, which is nearly two-thirds of your original investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

An assets under management (AUM) fee is an annual charge by a financial advisor or wealth management firm, typically expressed as a percentage of your total portfolio value. The industry standard ranges from 0.25% to 1.50%, though many robo-advisors charge 0.25% or less.

A good advisor may add value through tax planning, behavioral coaching, estate planning, and portfolio construction. Research by Vanguard suggests an advisor can potentially add about 3% in net returns annually through these services. The key is whether the value added exceeds the cost of the fee.

Because the fee is charged on the total portfolio value — which grows over time — the absolute dollar amount of the fee grows each year. This means you pay more in fees in year 20 than in year 1, and the fee's impact compounds alongside your returns.

Alternatives include robo-advisors (typically 0.25%), fee-only financial planners who charge flat fees or hourly rates, and self-directed investing through low-cost index funds. A flat-fee planner can provide ongoing advice without the compounding cost of a percentage-based fee.
