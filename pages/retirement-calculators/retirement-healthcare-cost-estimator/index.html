Example Scenario

A 55-year-old in good health planning to retire at 65 in an average-cost city would expect annual healthcare costs around $7,500 at retirement. Over a 20-year retirement (to age 85), with healthcare inflation at 5% per year, total projected costs exceed $248,000. Setting aside roughly $500/month now in an HSA or dedicated account could help cover this substantial future expense.

Frequently Asked Questions

A Fidelity study estimates the average couple retiring at 65 will need approximately $315,000 for healthcare costs in retirement, not counting long-term care. Individual costs vary widely based on health status, location, plan choice, and how long you live.

Medicare Part A covers hospital stays (usually premium-free). Part B covers outpatient care with a monthly premium. Part D covers prescription drugs. Medicare does not cover routine dental, vision, hearing, or long-term care. Most retirees purchase a Medigap supplement or Medicare Advantage plan to fill gaps.

Healthcare costs typically inflate faster than general inflation — around 5–6% per year historically versus 2–3% for general goods. This makes healthcare an increasingly large share of retirement spending over time, requiring dedicated savings strategies.

If you retire before age 65, you must bridge the gap until Medicare eligibility. Options include COBRA, marketplace ACA plans, or a spouse's employer plan. Costs are significantly higher — often $500–$1,500+/month per person — making pre-Medicare healthcare a major planning consideration.
