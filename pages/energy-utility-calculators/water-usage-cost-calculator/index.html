Water Bill Formula

Cost Calculations
Water Charge = (Monthly Gallons / 1,000) × Water Rate Sewer Charge = (Monthly Gallons / 1,000) × Sewer Rate Monthly Bill = Water Charge + Sewer Charge Annual Cost = Monthly Bill × 12 Cost per Gallon = Monthly Bill / Monthly Gallons 10% Conservation Savings = Annual Cost × 10% 20% Conservation Savings = Annual Cost × 20%

Water and sewer rates are typically charged per 1,000 gallons or per CCF (100 cubic feet = 748 gallons). This calculator uses per-1,000-gallon rates for simplicity. Divide your bill rate by 0.748 if you need to convert from CCF to 1,000-gallon units.

Most utilities also charge a fixed monthly service fee in addition to usage-based charges. Check your bill for any base charges not captured in this estimate.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly gallons used -- find this on your water bill; average US household is 3,000-8,000 gallons/month.
  2. Enter water rate per 1,000 gallons -- the usage charge from your bill (typically $3-$10/thousand gallons).
  3. Enter sewer rate per 1,000 gallons -- the wastewater charge from your bill (often equals or exceeds water charge).
  4. Click "Calculate Water Bill" to see monthly bill, annual cost, cost per gallon, and conservation savings.

Example Scenario

Family of 4: 6,000 Gallons/Month

Monthly usage: 6,000 gallons | Water rate: $5.50/thousand gal | Sewer rate: $6.00/thousand gal

Water Charge = 6 × $5.50 = $33.00

Sewer Charge = 6 × $6.00 = $36.00

Monthly Bill = $33 + $36 = $69.00

Annual Cost = $69 × 12 = $828/year

Cost per Gallon = $69 / 6,000 = $0.0115/gallon

20% Conservation Savings = $828 × 20% = $165.60/year

Frequently Asked Questions

The average US household spends about $70-$100 per month on combined water and sewer service, or $840-$1,200 per year. Usage averages about 80-100 gallons per person per day. Rates vary widely by municipality and region.

The sewer rate covers wastewater treatment and is typically calculated based on your water usage. Most utilities assume that most of the water you use eventually enters the sewer system. Sewer charges often equal or exceed the water supply charge.

Key conservation steps include fixing leaks (a dripping faucet can waste 3,000+ gallons/year), installing low-flow showerheads and faucet aerators, using WaterSense-certified toilets, running full loads in dishwashers and washing machines, and watering lawns at dawn or dusk to reduce evaporation.

Water utilities typically bill by the hundred cubic feet (CCF) or by the thousand gallons (kGal). One CCF equals approximately 748 gallons. Your bill will show the rate per CCF or per 1,000 gallons, and your meter reading in those units.
