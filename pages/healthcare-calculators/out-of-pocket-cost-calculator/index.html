How Cost Distribution Works

Medical costs flow through three distinct phases under most health insurance plans:

Phase 1: Deductible Zone
You pay 100% of covered costs up to your annual deductible.
Phase 2: Coinsurance Zone
You pay your coinsurance % on costs above the deductible, until your OOP max is reached.
Phase 3: OOP Max Cap
Once your total out-of-pocket hits the OOP max, insurance covers 100% of remaining covered costs.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual deductible - find this on your insurance card or Summary of Benefits.
  2. Enter your coinsurance % - your share after the deductible (commonly 10-30%).
  3. Enter your out-of-pocket maximum - the most you can ever pay in a plan year.
  4. Enter expected annual medical costs - your estimate for the year including all services.
  5. Click Calculate to see exactly how costs distribute and your total out-of-pocket.

Example Scenario

A plan has a $1,500 deductible, 20% coinsurance, and a $7,000 out-of-pocket maximum. The member expects $8,000 in medical costs this year. Phase 1: They pay $1,500 toward the deductible. Phase 2: On the remaining $6,500, they pay 20% = $1,300. Total so far: $2,800, well below the $7,000 OOP max. The insurance company covers the remaining 80% = $5,200. If costs were $40,000 instead, total out-of-pocket would be capped at $7,000 regardless.

Frequently Asked Questions

The out-of-pocket maximum is the most you will pay for covered healthcare in a plan year. Once reached, insurance covers 100% of covered in-network costs for the rest of the year. For 2026, ACA self-only plans cap at approximately $9,450.

Generally, deductibles, coinsurance, and copays for covered in-network services count toward your OOP max. Monthly premiums do NOT count. Out-of-network costs may not count toward your in-network OOP max. Always check your specific plan documents.

A copay is a flat dollar amount you pay for a specific service (e.g., $30 per doctor visit). Coinsurance is a percentage of the total cost (e.g., 20% of a $500 procedure = $100). Coinsurance typically applies after your deductible is met.

In a typical plan: you pay 100% of costs until you meet your deductible, then you pay your coinsurance percentage until you hit your out-of-pocket maximum, and after that insurance pays 100% of covered costs for the rest of the year.
