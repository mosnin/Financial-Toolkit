Turnover Cost Formula

The total turnover cost per employee includes direct and indirect expenses:

Turnover Cost Formula
Total Cost = Recruiting Cost + (Weekly Salary x Training Weeks x Productivity Loss %)

Where Weekly Salary is the annual salary divided by 52, and Productivity Loss % represents the reduced output during the training period.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual salary - the departing employee's yearly compensation.
  2. Enter recruiting cost - job postings, recruiter fees, interview time, and onboarding.
  3. Enter training time - weeks needed for the new hire to reach full productivity.
  4. Enter productivity loss - estimated percentage of reduced output during training.

Example Scenario

An employee earning $75,000/year leaves. Recruiting costs $5,000, training takes 8 weeks, and the new hire operates at 50% productivity during that time. Weekly salary is $1,442. Productivity loss cost = $1,442 x 8 x 0.50 = $5,769. Total turnover cost = $5,000 + $5,769 = $10,769, about 14.4% of annual salary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Employee turnover typically costs between 50% and 200% of the departing employee's annual salary. For senior or specialized roles, costs can be even higher.

Turnover costs include recruiting and hiring expenses, onboarding and training costs, lost productivity during the vacancy and ramp-up period, management time, and potential impact on team morale.

Strategies include competitive compensation, career development opportunities, positive workplace culture, regular feedback and recognition, and flexible work arrangements.

Average annual turnover rates vary by industry, generally ranging from 10% to 20%. Technology and retail sectors often see higher rates.
