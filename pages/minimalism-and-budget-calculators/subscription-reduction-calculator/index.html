How the Subscription Analysis Works

Formulas
Cost per Use (Daily) = Monthly Cost / 30
Cost per Use (Weekly) = Monthly Cost / 4
Cost per Use (Monthly) = Monthly Cost / 1
Cost per Use (Rarely) = Monthly Cost / 0.5 (2× monthly cost per actual use)
5-Year Invested Value = Savings × [((1 + r/12)^60 − 1) / (r/12)]

Subscriptions used "rarely" (less than once per month) are automatically flagged as recommended cuts since their cost-per-use is extremely high. Monthly usage subscriptions are kept. The 5-year projection shows how much the monthly savings grow if invested in a diversified portfolio.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. List all subscriptions: Include streaming, software, news, fitness, food delivery, apps, and any other recurring monthly charge.
  2. Enter the monthly cost: If you pay annually, divide by 12 to get the monthly equivalent.
  3. Choose usage frequency: Be honest — select "rarely" if you haven't used a service in the past month.
  4. Set your investment return: Default is 7% for a diversified index fund portfolio.
  5. Review recommendations: The calculator flags all "rarely" used subscriptions as immediate cut candidates.

Example Scenario

Taylor has 8 active subscriptions totaling $187/month. After auditing usage:

  • Netflix ($17, weekly): Keep — high usage, low cost per use
  • Gym membership ($45, rarely): Cancel — $90 per actual visit
  • Magazine app ($12, rarely): Cancel — barely opened in 6 months
  • Cloud storage backup ($10, daily): Keep — critical utility
  • Meal kit service ($80, monthly): Downgrade — only using 2 of 4 kits

By canceling rarely-used subscriptions, Taylor saves $57/month ($684/year). Invested at 7%, that becomes $4,041 in 5 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cost per use is calculated by dividing the monthly subscription cost by the estimated number of times you use it per month. A $15/month streaming service used 4 times per month costs $3.75 per use. Subscriptions with a high cost per use are prime candidates for cancellation.

Cancel subscriptions you use rarely or never first. Next, look for overlapping services (multiple music or streaming platforms) and consolidate to just one. Finally, evaluate whether annual billing options would save money on services you actively use.

Studies suggest the average American household spends over $200 per month on subscriptions, though many people underestimate their total by 50% or more. Regular audits using a tool like this calculator help keep subscription creep under control.

Subscription creep is the gradual accumulation of recurring monthly charges over time. Each individual subscription seems small and reasonable, but collectively they can drain hundreds of dollars from your budget each month without you noticing. Regular audits are essential.
