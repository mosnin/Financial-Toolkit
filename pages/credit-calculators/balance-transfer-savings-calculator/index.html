Balance Transfer Savings Formula

The savings from a balance transfer are calculated by comparing interest costs:

Savings Calculation
Interest Saved = (Current APR - Transfer APR) / 12 x Balance x Promo Months
Net Savings = Interest Saved - (Balance x Transfer Fee %)

The breakeven point is the month at which the cumulative interest savings exceed the transfer fee. After that point, every month of the promo period is pure savings.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current balance - the amount you plan to transfer.
  2. Enter your current APR - the interest rate on your existing card.
  3. Enter the promotional APR - the rate offered on the new card (often 0%).
  4. Enter the transfer fee percentage - typically 3-5%.
  5. Enter the promotional period - how many months the low rate lasts.
  6. Click "Calculate Savings" to see your net savings and breakeven point.

Example Scenario

You have a $7,000 balance at 24.99% APR. A new card offers 0% APR for 18 months with a 3% transfer fee ($210). During the promo period, you would save approximately $2,624 in interest. After subtracting the $210 fee, your net savings are $2,414. The transfer fee is recouped within the first 2 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

A balance transfer moves existing credit card debt to a new card, typically one offering a promotional 0% or low APR period. This lets you pay down principal faster during the promo period instead of paying high interest charges.

Most balance transfer cards charge a fee of 3-5% of the transferred amount. On a $5,000 transfer, that is $150-$250. Some cards occasionally offer no-fee transfers, though these are less common.

When the promo period expires, the remaining balance will accrue interest at the card's regular APR, which is often 17-27%. It is important to plan to pay off as much as possible during the promotional period.

Opening a new card creates a hard inquiry (small temporary dip). However, the increased available credit can lower your utilization ratio, potentially improving your score. Closing the old card could hurt your score by reducing available credit.

Promotional 0% APR periods typically last 12-21 months. The length depends on the card issuer and your creditworthiness. Longer promotional periods give you more time to pay down the balance interest-free.
