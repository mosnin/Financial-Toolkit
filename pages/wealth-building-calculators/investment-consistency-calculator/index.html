Investment Consistency Calculator

Consistency is one of the most underrated factors in wealth building. This calculator quantifies exactly what it costs — in real dollars — to miss even 10% of your monthly investment contributions. By comparing a fully consistent strategy against an inconsistent one, you'll see your consistency premium and the years of financial head-start that disciplined investing provides.

Investment Consistency Formula

The consistent investor invests every month; the inconsistent investor invests in only 90% of months (missing roughly 1.2 months per year):

Consistent Portfolio
FVconsistent = PMT × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r]
Inconsistent Portfolio (90% of months)
FVinconsistent = PMT × 0.9 × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r]
Consistency Premium
Premium = FVconsistent − FVinconsistent

Where PMT is the monthly investment, r is the monthly return rate (annual / 12), and n is total months invested. The inconsistent scenario assumes contributions are skipped at random intervals, losing both the cash contribution and all future compounding on that contribution.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly investment amount — the amount you plan to invest each month.
  2. Enter the annual market return — expected average annual return (8–10% is historically typical for equity indices).
  3. Enter years invested — your planned investment horizon.
  4. Enter your starting age — used to calculate your final retirement age.
  5. Click "Calculate Consistency Premium" to see the exact cost of inconsistency and the value of staying the course.

Example Scenario

An investor contributing $500/month at an 8% return starting at age 30 for 35 years builds a consistent portfolio of approximately $1,056,000. The inconsistent investor — skipping 10% of months — accumulates about $950,000. The consistency premium is over $106,000 in wealth lost by missing fewer than 5 investment days per year on average. This does not account for the added benefit of purchasing at lower prices during market dips that a consistent DCA investor captures.
