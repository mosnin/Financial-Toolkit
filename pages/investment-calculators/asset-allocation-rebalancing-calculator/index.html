How Rebalancing Is Calculated

For each asset class, the target dollar amount is computed from the total portfolio value, and the difference from the current holding gives the buy or sell amount:

Target Amount Formula
Target Amount = Total Portfolio Value × (Target % / 100)
Rebalance Amount Formula
Rebalance Amount = Target Amount − Current Holding

A positive value means you need to buy more of that asset class; a negative value means you need to sell.

How to Use This Rebalancing Calculator

  1. Enter your total portfolio value — the combined value of all holdings.
  2. Set target allocations — the percentages you want for stocks, bonds, and cash (must total 100%).
  3. Enter current holdings — the current dollar value of each asset class.
  4. Click "Calculate Rebalancing" to see exactly how much to buy or sell in each category.

Example Scenario

Suppose your $100,000 portfolio currently holds $65,000 in stocks (65%), $30,000 in bonds (30%), and $5,000 in cash (5%), but your target is 60% stocks, 35% bonds, and 5% cash. To rebalance, you would sell $5,000 of stocks and buy $5,000 of bonds, leaving cash unchanged. This restores your intended risk profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Portfolio rebalancing is the process of realigning the weights of assets in your portfolio to maintain your original desired level of asset allocation. Over time, gains in one asset class can cause your portfolio to drift from its target mix.

Most financial advisors recommend rebalancing at least once a year or whenever an asset class drifts more than 5% from its target allocation. Annual or semi-annual rebalancing strikes a balance between staying on target and minimizing transaction costs.
