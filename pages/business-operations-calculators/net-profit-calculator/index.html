Net Profit Formulas

The income statement follows a waterfall structure from revenue down to net profit.

Gross Profit
Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS
Operating Profit (EBIT)
Operating Profit = Gross Profit - Operating Expenses
Pre-Tax Profit (EBT)
Pre-Tax Profit = Operating Profit - Interest Expense
Net Profit
Net Profit = Pre-Tax Profit x (1 - Tax Rate)
Net Profit Margin
Net Margin = (Net Profit / Revenue) x 100

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter revenue -- your total sales income for the period.
  2. Enter COGS -- all direct costs tied to producing your goods or delivering your services.
  3. Enter operating expenses -- indirect costs like salaries, rent, marketing, and administrative costs.
  4. Enter interest expense -- any loan or debt interest paid during the period (enter 0 if none).
  5. Enter your effective tax rate -- the percentage of pre-tax income paid as taxes (enter 0 if not applicable).
  6. Click "Calculate Net Profit" to see the full profitability breakdown.

Example Scenario

A mid-size services business has these figures: Revenue $500,000, COGS $200,000, Operating Expenses $150,000, Interest $10,000, Tax Rate 25%.

  • Gross Profit: $500,000 - $200,000 = $300,000 (60% margin)
  • Operating Profit: $300,000 - $150,000 = $150,000 (30% margin)
  • Pre-Tax Profit: $150,000 - $10,000 = $140,000
  • Tax: $140,000 x 25% = $35,000
  • Net Profit: $140,000 - $35,000 = $105,000 (21% net margin)

Frequently Asked Questions

Net profit is the revenue remaining after all expenses have been deducted, including COGS, operating expenses, interest, and taxes. It represents the true bottom-line profitability of a business.

Net profit margin is net profit expressed as a percentage of revenue. It shows how many cents of profit a business generates for every dollar of revenue after all costs are paid.

Operating profit (EBIT) is profit after COGS and operating expenses but before interest and taxes. Net profit deducts interest expense and income taxes, giving the final take-home profit figure.

Net profit margin benchmarks vary by industry. Software companies often achieve 15-30%+, professional services 10-20%, retail 2-5%, and restaurants 3-9%. Compare against direct competitors for meaningful analysis.
