HVAC Sizing Formula

HVAC size is estimated using a base BTU-per-square-foot calculation adjusted for climate, insulation, and building characteristics:

Sizing Formula
BTU = Home Sqft x Base BTU/sqft x Climate Factor x Insulation Factor x Story Factor Tons = BTU / 12,000

Base BTU per square foot is approximately 25. Climate, insulation, and story multipliers adjust this up or down. One ton of cooling capacity equals 12,000 BTU per hour.

How to Use This Calculator

  2. Select your climate zone - based on your geographic region.
  3. Choose insulation quality - how well your home retains heating and cooling.
  4. Select number of stories - multi-story homes need slightly larger systems.

Example Scenario

A 2,000-square-foot two-story home in a hot climate with average insulation would need approximately a 4-ton (48,000 BTU) system. Estimated installation cost would be around $8,500-$15,000 depending on the system type and efficiency rating.

Frequently Asked Questions

The general rule is 20-25 BTU per square foot for mild climates and 30-40 BTU for hot or cold climates. Insulation quality, ceiling height, and window area all affect the actual requirement.

One ton of HVAC capacity equals 12,000 BTU per hour. A 2-ton system provides 24,000 BTU/hr. Most homes need 1.5 to 5 tons depending on size and climate.

An oversized system cycles too frequently, wastes energy, and fails to dehumidify. An undersized system runs constantly and wears out faster. Proper sizing is critical for comfort and efficiency.

A new central HVAC system costs $5,000-$12,000 for basic, $10,000-$18,000 for mid-range, and $15,000-$30,000+ for high-efficiency installations. Costs include the unit, ductwork, and labor.

Central AC units last 15-20 years, furnaces 15-25 years, and heat pumps 12-17 years. Regular maintenance extends lifespan. Plan for replacement when repair costs exceed 50% of a new system.
