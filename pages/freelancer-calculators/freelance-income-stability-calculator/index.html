Income Stability Formula

Average Monthly Income
Average = (Month 1 + Month 2 + Month 3) / 3
Income Variability (Coefficient of Variation)
Variability % = (Standard Deviation / Average) × 100
Stability Score (0–100)
Score = max(0, 100 - Variability %)
Recommended Emergency Fund
Emergency Fund = Monthly Expenses × Adjusted Months Target

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your last 3 months of gross freelance income as accurately as possible.
  2. Enter your total monthly expenses including rent, utilities, food, debt payments, and discretionary spending.
  3. Set your emergency fund target in months (default 6; higher variability warrants more).
  4. Click Analyze Stability to see your variability score and personalized income smoothing strategy.

Example Scenario

A freelance copywriter earned $7,000, $4,500, and $9,000 over three months with $4,000 in monthly expenses.

  • Average Income: ($7,000 + $4,500 + $9,000) / 3 = $6,833
  • Standard Deviation: approx. $1,870
  • Variability: $1,870 / $6,833 = 27.4%
  • Stability Score: 100 - 27.4 = 73 / 100 (Moderate)
  • Emergency Fund (6 months): $4,000 × 6 = $24,000
  • Strategy: Build retainer relationships; smooth income by paying yourself $5,500/month

Frequently Asked Questions

Most financial advisors recommend 3-6 months for employees, but freelancers should aim for 6-12 months due to income variability. The higher your variability percentage, the larger the cushion you need.

Income variability is the coefficient of variation — standard deviation divided by the mean, as a percentage. Under 20% is stable; above 50% is high risk requiring a larger financial buffer.

Income smoothing means paying yourself a fixed monthly salary from a business buffer account. Surplus builds during high months and draws down during slow months, reducing stress and aiding household budgeting.

Diversify your client base, pursue retainer contracts, and build your pipeline ahead of current projects. Recurring revenue is the single most effective stabilizer for freelance income.
