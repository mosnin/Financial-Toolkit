Property Management Cost Formula

Annual Cost Breakdown
Gross Annual Income = Monthly Rent × 12 Annual Management Cost = Gross Income × Management Fee % Annual Leasing Cost = Monthly Rent × Leasing Fee (amortized annually) Vacancy Loss = Gross Income × Vacancy Rate % Maintenance Reserve = Gross Income × Maintenance Reserve % Total Expenses = Management + Leasing + Vacancy Loss + Maintenance Net Rental Income = Gross Annual Income − Total Expenses

The leasing fee is amortized over 12 months to represent its average annual impact, assuming one tenant turnover per year at the specified fee.

Vacancy loss represents income lost while the unit sits empty between tenants, calculated as a percentage of potential gross income.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly rent -- the market rent or current rent charged to tenants.
  2. Enter management fee % -- the ongoing monthly fee charged by the property manager, typically 8-12%.
  3. Enter leasing fee -- the number of months' rent charged per new tenant placement (usually 0.5-1 month).
  4. Enter vacancy rate % -- the estimated percentage of time the unit will be vacant each year.
  5. Enter maintenance reserve % -- the percentage of rent set aside for repairs and upkeep.
  6. Click "Calculate Management Costs" to see a full expense breakdown and net income.

Example Scenario

$2,000/Month Rental with Full-Service Management

You rent a unit at $2,000/month and hire a property manager at 10% with a 1-month leasing fee, 5% vacancy, and 8% maintenance reserve.

Gross Annual Income = $2,000 × 12 = $24,000

Management Cost = $24,000 × 10% = $2,400

Leasing Cost (annualized) = $2,000 × 1 / 1 = $2,000

Vacancy Loss = $24,000 × 5% = $1,200

Maintenance Reserve = $24,000 × 8% = $1,920

Net Rental Income = $24,000 − $7,520 = $16,480/year

Frequently Asked Questions

Property management fees typically range from 8% to 12% of monthly rent collected. Rates vary by market, property type, and level of service. Full-service managers handling everything including maintenance may charge toward the higher end.

A leasing fee is a one-time charge for finding and placing a new tenant. It is typically equal to one month's rent or 50-100% of the first month's rent. This fee covers tenant screening, marketing, and lease execution.

A common rule of thumb is to reserve 1% of the property value per year for maintenance, or 5-15% of monthly rent. Older properties, those with deferred maintenance, or properties with pools or large HVAC systems may require higher reserves.

National average vacancy rates hover around 5-8%, but local market conditions matter most. A tight urban rental market may have 2-3% vacancy, while a slower suburban market may see 8-12%. Check local rental statistics for the most accurate estimate.
