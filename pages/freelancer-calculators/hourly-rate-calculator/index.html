Hourly Rate Formula

Total Annual Billable Hours
Billable Hours = Weeks Worked × Billable Hours Per Week
Required Gross Revenue
Gross Revenue = (Target Income + Business Expenses) ÷ (1 - Tax Rate)
Minimum Hourly Rate
Minimum Rate = Required Gross Revenue ÷ Annual Billable Hours
Recommended Rate (with buffer)
Recommended Rate = Minimum Rate × (1 + Profit Buffer %)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your target take-home income — the after-tax amount you want to keep each year.
  2. List your annual business expenses — software, insurance, equipment, marketing, and professional fees.
  3. Set weeks worked per year by subtracting vacation, sick days, and holidays from 52 weeks.
  4. Enter your realistic billable hours per week — only count actual client-facing work, not admin time.
  5. Set your effective tax rate, including income tax and self-employment tax (~15.3%).
  6. Add a profit buffer (10-25% recommended) to give yourself a cushion above the minimum rate.
  7. Click Calculate My Rate to see your minimum and recommended hourly rates.

Example Scenario

A freelance designer wants $75,000 take-home, has $10,000 in expenses, works 46 weeks/year, bills 25 hours/week, faces a 32% tax rate, and wants a 20% buffer.

  • Annual Billable Hours: 46 × 25 = 1,150 hours
  • Required Gross Revenue: ($75,000 + $10,000) ÷ (1 - 0.32) = $125,000
  • Minimum Hourly Rate: $125,000 ÷ 1,150 = $108.70/hr
  • Recommended Rate (20% buffer): $108.70 × 1.20 = $130.44/hr
  • Taxes to set aside: $125,000 × 0.32 = $40,000/year

Your hourly rate must cover both the employer and employee portions of Social Security and Medicare, which is why freelancers typically charge 25-40% more than equivalent salaried employees earn.

Include software subscriptions, hardware depreciation, office costs, professional insurance (E&O, general liability), accounting and legal fees, marketing spend, professional development, and any subcontractor costs. Total these annually and divide by billable hours to find the per-hour overhead cost that must be covered by your rate.

Unlike employees, freelancers don't receive paid time off. Subtract your planned vacation, sick days, and holidays from your annual working days before calculating billable hours. If you want 15 days off, that's roughly 120 hours of income you must earn in the remaining days — increasing your required hourly rate.
