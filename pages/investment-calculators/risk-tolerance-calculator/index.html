How Risk Tolerance is Assessed

Your risk tolerance score is determined by combining several factors:

Risk Score Calculation
Score = Age Factor + Time Horizon Factor + Comfort Questions Average (weighted)

The score maps to a risk profile (Conservative to Aggressive) that suggests an appropriate asset allocation split between stocks, bonds, and cash.

How to Use This Risk Tolerance Calculator

  1. Enter your age - younger investors generally have higher risk capacity.
  2. Enter your annual income - higher income can support more risk.
  3. Enter your time horizon - how many years until you need the money.
  4. Answer each question honestly - your emotional comfort matters.
  5. Click "Get Risk Profile" to see your assessment and recommended allocation.

Example Scenario

A 35-year-old with a $75,000 income, 20-year time horizon, and moderate comfort with volatility might receive a "Moderate" risk profile with a recommended allocation of 60% stocks, 30% bonds, and 10% cash.

Frequently Asked Questions

Risk tolerance is your ability and willingness to endure fluctuations in the value of your investments. It is influenced by your financial situation, time horizon, investment goals, and emotional comfort with market volatility.

Generally, younger investors can take on more risk because they have more time to recover from market downturns. As you approach retirement, a more conservative allocation helps protect your savings.

Common risk profiles include Conservative (low risk, stable returns), Moderately Conservative, Moderate (balanced approach), Moderately Aggressive, and Aggressive (high risk, higher potential returns).

Risk tolerance is one important factor, but you should also consider your financial goals, time horizon, income stability, and overall financial plan. A financial advisor can help you develop a comprehensive strategy.
