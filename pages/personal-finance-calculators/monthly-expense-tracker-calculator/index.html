How Expense Percentages Are Calculated

Formula
Total Monthly Expenses = Sum of all category amounts
Category % = (Category Amount / Gross Income) × 100
Savings Rate = (Savings / Gross Income) × 100
Annual Expenses = Total Monthly Expenses × 12

Tracking expenses by category reveals spending patterns that are invisible when you only look at your bank balance. Most people are surprised by how much small recurring costs (subscriptions, daily coffees, dining) accumulate over a month — category breakdowns make these visible and actionable.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly gross income: Optional, but enables percentage calculations and savings rate.
  2. Fill in each expense category: Use monthly averages. For irregular expenses, divide annual total by 12.
  3. Click "Analyze Expenses": See your total spending, category breakdown, and savings rate.
  4. Identify top categories: Look for categories above expected benchmarks — those are reduction opportunities.
  5. Set targets: Use this as a baseline and track monthly to measure improvement over time.

Example Scenario

Casey earns $5,500/month gross and tracks these expenses: Housing $1,350, Utilities $175, Food $580, Transportation $420, Insurance $280, Subscriptions $95, Entertainment $250, Personal $180, Savings $600, Other $120. Total: $4,050. That is 73.6% of income spent, with a savings rate of 10.9%. Housing is 24.5% of income (healthy), but food at 10.5% is above the typical 8-10% benchmark — a potential area to reduce.

Frequently Asked Questions

The BLS reports average American household spending of approximately $5,000–$6,000/month, with housing at roughly 33%, transportation at 16%, and food at 12% of total spending.

Savings Rate = (Monthly Savings / Gross Monthly Income) x 100. A rate of 15–20% including retirement contributions is generally recommended. The FIRE community often targets 30–50%+ to accelerate financial independence.

Track all regular categories: housing, utilities, food, transportation, healthcare, insurance, subscriptions, entertainment, personal care, savings, and miscellaneous. For irregular expenses, divide annual totals by 12 to get a monthly average.

Start with your highest categories. Meal prep to cut food costs, audit subscriptions, refinance debt, switch insurance providers, and reduce utility usage. Even 10% reduction in one large category creates significant annual savings.
