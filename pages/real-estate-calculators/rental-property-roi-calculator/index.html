ROI Formulas

Return on Investment
Total Cash Invested = Down Payment + Closing Costs Annual Cash Flow = (Monthly Rent - Monthly Expenses) x 12 Equity Gain = Purchase Price x (Appreciation Rate / 100) Total Annual Return = Annual Cash Flow + Equity Gain Annual ROI = (Total Annual Return / Total Cash Invested) x 100 Cash-on-Cash = (Annual Cash Flow / Total Cash Invested) x 100

The annual ROI combines both cash flow and appreciation to give a complete picture of the investment's return relative to the cash you put in.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the purchase price of the property.
  2. Enter down payment and closing costs -- these form your total cash investment.
  3. Enter monthly rent -- the gross rent you expect to collect.
  4. Enter total monthly expenses -- include mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, management, and vacancy allowance.
  5. Enter the expected annual appreciation rate -- the national average is roughly 3-4% historically.
  6. Click "Calculate ROI" to see your returns.

Example Scenario

$400,000 Rental Property with 20% Down

Purchase price: $400,000. Down payment: $80,000. Closing costs: $8,000. Total cash invested: $88,000.

Monthly rent: $2,800. Monthly expenses: $2,200. Monthly cash flow: $600. Annual cash flow: $7,200.

At 3% annual appreciation, the property gains $12,000 in equity from appreciation in the first year.

Total return = $7,200 + $12,000 = $19,200. Annual ROI = $19,200 / $88,000 = 21.82%. Cash-on-cash return = $7,200 / $88,000 = 8.18%.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most investors consider 8-12% annual ROI as a good return for rental property. This includes both cash flow and appreciation. However, returns vary significantly by market, property type, and management approach.

ROI measures the return relative to your actual cash investment (including leverage effects), while cap rate measures the property's return relative to its total value, ignoring financing. ROI is typically higher when using leverage.

Including appreciation gives a more complete picture of total returns but adds uncertainty since future appreciation is not guaranteed. Many conservative investors focus on cash-on-cash return and treat appreciation as a bonus.

Include mortgage payment, property taxes, insurance, maintenance, property management fees, HOA dues, vacancy allowance, and reserves for capital expenditures. The more comprehensive your expense estimate, the more accurate your ROI calculation.

Leverage (using a mortgage) amplifies returns because you control a large asset with a smaller cash investment. If the property appreciates or generates positive cash flow, your percentage return on invested cash is higher than it would be with an all-cash purchase.
