How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly take-home income: Use your net pay after taxes and deductions.
  2. Add each recurring bill: Include the name, monthly amount, and due date (day of month) for up to 10 bills.
  3. Click "Plan My Bills": See your total bill burden, percentage of income, remaining discretionary income, and a calendar view.
  4. Review the bill calendar: Bills are sorted by due date so you can plan cash flow throughout the month.
  5. Identify opportunities: If bills exceed 50% of income, look for subscriptions to cancel or bills to negotiate.

How Bill Percentages Are Calculated

Formula
Total Monthly Bills = Sum of all bill amounts
Bills % of Income = (Total Bills / Monthly Income) × 100
Discretionary Income = Monthly Income − Total Bills
Annual Total = Total Monthly Bills × 12

Discretionary income is the money remaining after all fixed recurring bills are paid. This amount is available for variable expenses (groceries, gas, dining, entertainment) and savings. The higher your discretionary income, the more financial flexibility you have.

Example Scenario

Alex takes home $4,200/month and has these recurring bills: Rent $1,400 (1st), Car payment $350 (5th), Internet $70 (8th), Phone $85 (10th), Car insurance $120 (15th), Electric $95 (18th), Netflix $18 (20th), Gym $45 (22nd), Student loan $280 (25th). Total bills: $2,463 — 58.6% of income. Discretionary income: $1,737/month. The bill calendar shows a cluster of bills early and mid-month, helping Alex plan when to have funds available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Under the 50/30/20 rule, essential recurring bills should total no more than 50% of after-tax income. If your fixed bills exceed this, look for bills to reduce, negotiate, or eliminate.

Negotiate rates on cable, internet, and insurance; eliminate rarely-used subscriptions; refinance loans; switch to cheaper phone plans. Even small reductions compound into significant annual savings.

Discretionary income is money left after paying all fixed bills and essential expenses. It covers variable spending like groceries, gas, dining, entertainment, and savings contributions.

Due dates help you plan cash flow, avoid late fees, and prevent overdrafts. Knowing when large bills hit lets you ensure sufficient funds are in your checking account at the right times.
