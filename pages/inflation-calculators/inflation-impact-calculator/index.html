Inflation Impact Calculator

Inflation Impact Formula

Formulas Used
Future Cost = Current Amount × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Purchasing Power Loss = Future Cost − Current Amount

Real Value Decline % = (1 − 1/(1 + Inflation Rate)^Years) × 100

Monthly Inflation Cost = Purchasing Power Loss ÷ (Years × 12)

The future cost formula uses compound inflation to project what today's price will be in the future. The purchasing power loss is the raw dollar erosion. The real value decline percentage tells you how much less your money buys. The monthly inflation cost breaks the total erosion down into an average monthly figure, making it easier to budget for.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the current amount: This could be the price of a specific item, your monthly budget, savings balance, or any dollar figure you want to project forward.
  2. Set the inflation rate: Use 3% for the US historical average, 2% for the Fed's target, or a higher rate for categories like healthcare or education.
  3. Enter the number of years: How many years into the future you want to project — try 5, 10, or 20 years to see the long-term effect.
  4. Click "Calculate Impact": Review all four output metrics to understand the full scope of inflation's effect on your money.

Example Scenario

Suppose you currently spend $2,000 per month on living expenses and want to know the inflation impact over 15 years at 3% annual inflation:

  • Current monthly expenses: $2,000 (enter $24,000 as the annual amount)
  • Future cost after 15 years: $24,000 × (1.03)^15 = $37,371
  • Purchasing power loss: $37,371 − $24,000 = $13,371
  • Real value decline: 36.2% — your $24,000 only buys what $15,475 bought before
  • Monthly inflation cost: $13,371 ÷ (15 × 12) = $74.28 per month on average

This means to maintain the same standard of living in 15 years, you would need to earn an extra $74 per month just to keep up with inflation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Inflation reduces purchasing power by making each dollar worth less over time. At 3% annual inflation, $1,000 today will only buy the equivalent of about $744 worth of goods in 10 years — a 25.6% loss in purchasing power.

Real value decline is calculated as: Real Value = Current Amount / (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years. The percentage decline is ((Current Amount − Real Value) / Current Amount) × 100. This tells you how much buying power your money has lost.

Monthly inflation cost is the average extra amount you pay per month due to inflation over a given period. It is calculated by dividing the total extra cost (Future Cost − Current Amount) by the total number of months in the period.

The US long-term average is approximately 3% per year. For conservative planning use 3–4%; for recent conditions or specific categories like healthcare or tuition, you may want to use 5–8%. The Federal Reserve targets 2% as its inflation goal.
