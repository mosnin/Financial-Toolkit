How Strong Is Your Emergency Fund?

An emergency fund is your financial immune system — without it, any unexpected event forces you into debt. This 6-question quiz evaluates not just the size of your fund, but where it's held, how consistently you contribute, and whether it's appropriately sized for your specific risk profile including job stability and dependents.

Frequently Asked Questions

The standard recommendation is 3–6 months of essential living expenses. If you are self-employed, have a single income household, work in a volatile industry, or have dependents, aim for 6–12 months. Two-income households with stable jobs can manage with 3 months.

Your emergency fund should be in a high-yield savings account (HYSA) — separate from your checking account, FDIC-insured, and accessible within 1–3 business days. Avoid investing it in stocks or bonds where its value can drop exactly when you need it most.

Build a $1,000 starter emergency fund first, then aggressively pay off high-interest debt (above 7–8% APR). Once high-interest debt is cleared, fully fund your emergency fund before resuming investing. Without any buffer, one surprise forces you back into debt.

True emergencies are unexpected, necessary, and urgent: job loss, medical expenses, essential car or home repairs, or a family crisis. Planned irregular expenses (car registration, holidays, annual subscriptions) should be budgeted for separately in a sinking fund — not taken from your emergency fund.
