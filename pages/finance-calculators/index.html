Frequently Asked Questions

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original principal and previously accumulated interest. Over time, this creates exponential growth. The more frequently interest compounds and the longer your time horizon, the greater the effect. Use our Compound Interest Calculator to see this in action with your own numbers.

The two most popular strategies are the avalanche method (paying off highest-interest debt first to minimize total interest) and the snowball method (paying off smallest balances first for motivational wins). Our Debt Payoff Calculator lets you compare both approaches side by side.

Most financial advisors recommend saving 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses. If you are self-employed or have variable income, aim for 6 to 12 months. Our Emergency Fund Calculator helps you determine your personal target and create a plan to reach it.

A common benchmark is to have 25 times your annual expenses saved by retirement, based on the 4% withdrawal rule. However, your exact number depends on your lifestyle, expected Social Security benefits, and retirement age. Use our Retirement Savings Calculator for a personalized projection.

Net worth is simply your total assets minus your total liabilities. Assets include cash, investments, retirement accounts, and property. Liabilities include mortgages, student loans, credit card balances, and other debts. Our Net Worth Calculator walks you through each category step by step.
