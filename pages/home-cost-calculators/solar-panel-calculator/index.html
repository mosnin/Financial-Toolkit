Solar Panel Sizing Formula

Solar system size is calculated based on your energy consumption and available sunlight:

Sizing Formula
Monthly kWh = Electric Bill / Electricity Rate ($0.14/kWh avg) System Size (kW) = (Monthly kWh / 30) / Peak Sun Hours / 0.80 efficiency Panels Needed = System Size (W) / Panel Wattage

The 0.80 factor accounts for real-world system losses from wiring, inverter efficiency, temperature, and shading. Cost is estimated at $3.00 per watt before the 30% federal Investment Tax Credit.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly electric bill - your average monthly electricity cost.
  2. Enter available roof space - usable south/west-facing roof area in square feet.
  3. Select peak sun hours - based on your geographic region.
  4. Choose panel wattage - higher wattage means fewer panels needed.
  5. Click "Calculate System" to see system size, panels, cost, and payback period.

Example Scenario

A homeowner with a $150/month electric bill in a region with 5 peak sun hours, using 370W panels, would need approximately a 7.1 kW system (20 panels). The cost after the 30% federal tax credit would be about $14,900, with a payback period of roughly 8.3 years and annual savings of $1,800.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average US home needs a 6-8 kW system, which is 16-22 panels at 370W each. The exact number depends on your electricity usage, sun hours, panel efficiency, and roof orientation.

Solar systems cost $2.50-$3.50 per watt installed before incentives. A typical 7 kW system costs $17,500-$24,500 before the 30% federal tax credit, dropping to $12,250-$17,150 after.

Payback period is typically 6-12 years depending on electricity rates, system cost, incentives, and sun exposure. After payback, solar provides essentially free electricity for 15-20+ more years.

Solar panels typically last 25-30+ years. Most manufacturers guarantee at least 80% output after 25 years. Inverters may need replacement after 10-15 years.
