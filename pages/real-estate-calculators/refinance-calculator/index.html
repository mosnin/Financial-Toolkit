How the Formula Works

The refinance calculator compares two mortgage scenarios: your current loan at its existing rate and remaining term, versus a new loan at the lower rate for the same remaining term.

Refinance Savings Calculation
Current Payment = P[r1(1+r1)^n] / [(1+r1)^n - 1] New Payment = P[r2(1+r2)^n] / [(1+r2)^n - 1] Monthly Savings = Current Payment - New Payment Breakeven Months = Closing Costs / Monthly Savings Net Savings = (Monthly Savings x Months) - Closing Costs

The breakeven point tells you how many months of savings you need to recoup the upfront closing costs. After that point, every month of savings is pure profit. The net savings represent your total benefit over the remaining life of the loan after subtracting closing costs.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the current outstanding balance on your mortgage.
  2. Enter your current interest rate and the new rate you have been offered or are considering.
  3. Set the remaining term on your current loan. The new loan will use this same term for an apples-to-apples comparison.
  4. Enter the estimated closing costs for the refinance.
  5. Click "Calculate Savings" to see your potential savings and breakeven point.

Example Scenario

You have a mortgage with a $280,000 balance at 7.0% with 25 years remaining. You are offered a refinance at 5.5% with $6,000 in closing costs. Your current payment is about $1,979, and the new payment would be approximately $1,718, saving you $261 per month. The breakeven point would be about 23 months. Over the remaining 25 years, your net savings after closing costs would be approximately $72,300.

Frequently Asked Questions

Refinancing generally makes sense when you can lower your interest rate by at least 0.5-1%, plan to stay in the home long enough to recoup closing costs (past the breakeven point), and have good credit and sufficient equity. It can also make sense to refinance to switch from an adjustable rate to a fixed rate for stability.

The breakeven point is the number of months it takes for your monthly savings to equal the closing costs of the refinance. For example, if closing costs are $6,000 and you save $200 per month, your breakeven point is 30 months. You should plan to stay in your home at least this long for refinancing to be worthwhile.

Refinance closing costs typically range from 2-5% of the loan amount. Common fees include application fees, appraisal ($300-600), title search and insurance ($400-900), origination fees (0.5-1% of loan), and recording fees. On a $300,000 loan, expect to pay $6,000-$15,000 in closing costs.

Refinancing to a shorter term (e.g., 30-year to 15-year) usually means a higher monthly payment but significantly less total interest and a faster payoff. This makes sense if you can comfortably afford the higher payments and want to build equity faster and save on interest.

Yes, refinancing replaces your old loan with a new one. If you refinance into a new 30-year loan after 10 years, you are starting a fresh 30-year term, potentially paying more total interest despite a lower rate. Consider refinancing into the remaining term of your original loan to avoid extending your payoff date.
