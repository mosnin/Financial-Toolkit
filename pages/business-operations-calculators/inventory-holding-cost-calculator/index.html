Holding Cost Formula

The annual inventory holding cost is calculated as:

Holding Cost Formula
Annual Holding Cost = (Inventory Value x Storage %) + Insurance + (Inventory Value x Obsolescence %)

The cost per unit is found by dividing the total annual holding cost by the number of units in inventory.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter average inventory value - the average dollar value of goods held in stock.
  2. Enter storage cost rate - warehousing and storage as a percentage of inventory value.
  3. Enter annual insurance cost - yearly insurance premium for your inventory.
  4. Enter obsolescence rate - estimated percentage of inventory that becomes obsolete annually.
  5. Enter total units - the number of units in your inventory for per-unit cost calculation.

Example Scenario

A company holds $500,000 worth of inventory with a 10% storage cost rate, $5,000 in annual insurance, and a 3% obsolescence rate across 10,000 units. Annual holding cost = ($500,000 x 10%) + $5,000 + ($500,000 x 3%) = $50,000 + $5,000 + $15,000 = $70,000. That is $7.00 per unit per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Inventory holding cost (also called carrying cost) is the total cost of storing unsold goods. It includes warehousing, insurance, depreciation, obsolescence, and the opportunity cost of capital tied up in inventory.

Holding costs typically range from 20% to 30% of inventory value per year. This varies by industry, product type, and storage requirements.

You can reduce holding costs by implementing just-in-time inventory, improving demand forecasting, negotiating better storage rates, reducing lead times, and regularly clearing obsolete stock.

Holding costs include storage and warehousing costs, insurance premiums, obsolescence and spoilage, opportunity cost of capital, handling and labor costs, and inventory shrinkage.
