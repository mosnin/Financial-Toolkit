EV Range Formula

Range Calculations
Full Battery Range = Battery Size (kWh) × Efficiency (mi/kWh) Available Energy = Battery Size × (Current Charge % / 100) Current Range = Available Energy × Efficiency (mi/kWh) Range After Trip = Current Range − Destination Distance Charging Needed = MAX(0, (Destination Distance / Efficiency) − Available Energy)

This calculator assumes consistent efficiency throughout the trip. Real-world performance varies with driving conditions, weather, and speed.

As a safety buffer, most EV experts recommend maintaining at least 10-15% charge (not reaching 0%) to preserve battery health and avoid getting stranded.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter battery size in kWh -- find this in your EV's specifications or owner's manual.
  2. Enter efficiency (miles per kWh) -- the EPA-rated efficiency or your recent real-world average.
  3. Enter current charge % -- check your dashboard or EV app.
  4. Enter destination distance -- the one-way distance to your destination in miles.
  5. Click "Calculate EV Range" to see if you can make the trip and how much charging you need.

Example Scenario

Tesla Model 3 Long Range Road Trip

Battery: 82 kWh | Efficiency: 3.5 mi/kWh | Current Charge: 80% | Destination: 200 miles

Available Energy = 82 × 80% = 65.6 kWh

Current Range = 65.6 × 3.5 = 229.6 miles

Range After Trip = 229.6 − 200 = 29.6 miles remaining

Result: You can make the trip with approximately 29.6 miles of range to spare -- but may want to top up to 90% before departing for extra safety margin.

Frequently Asked Questions

EV efficiency, measured in miles per kWh (mi/kWh), indicates how far a vehicle travels on one kilowatt-hour of battery energy. Most modern EVs achieve 3-4 mi/kWh. Efficiency varies with speed, temperature, terrain, and driving style.

EPA-rated range is measured under controlled conditions. Real-world range is reduced by highway speeds, cold weather, using heat or A/C, aggressive acceleration, and hilly terrain. Expect 10-30% less range in cold weather or at highway speeds.

For daily driving, most EV manufacturers recommend charging to 80% to preserve long-term battery health. Reserve 100% charging for road trips when you need maximum range. Lithium-ion batteries experience less degradation when kept between 20% and 80% state of charge.

Charging time depends on the charger level and battery size. Level 1 (120V outlet): 3-5 miles of range per hour. Level 2 (240V home charger): 15-30 miles per hour. DC Fast Charging: 100-300+ miles per hour. A DC fast charger can add 200 miles in about 15-20 minutes.
