Alimony Formula

Most states use a variation of the following general approach:

Common Alimony Formula
Monthly Alimony = (Higher Income - Lower Income) x Support Percentage (30-40%)
Duration = Marriage Years x Duration Factor (typically 0.3 to 0.5)

The support percentage varies by state (commonly 30-40% of the income difference). The duration is often tied to the length of the marriage, with longer marriages resulting in longer support periods. Some states like New York use specific statutory formulas.

Example Scenario

One spouse earns $10,000/month and the other earns $3,000/month after a 12-year marriage. The income difference is $7,000. Using a general formula of 33% of the income difference, the estimated monthly alimony would be approximately $2,310. With a duration factor of 0.4, alimony might last about 5 years, totaling approximately $138,600 over the full period.

Frequently Asked Questions

Alimony calculations vary by state but generally consider the income difference between spouses, the length of the marriage, each spouse's earning capacity, age, health, and standard of living during the marriage. Many states use a formula based on a percentage of the income difference.

Alimony duration depends on the length of the marriage. A common guideline is that alimony lasts for roughly one-third to one-half the length of the marriage. Short marriages (under 10 years) may have temporary alimony, while very long marriages (20+ years) may result in permanent alimony in some states.

For divorce agreements executed after December 31, 2018, alimony is no longer tax-deductible for the payer and is not considered taxable income for the recipient under federal tax law. Agreements from before that date may follow the old rules.

Alimony (spousal support) is paid from one spouse to the other to maintain their standard of living after divorce. Child support is paid specifically for the financial needs of the children. They are separate obligations calculated using different formulas.
