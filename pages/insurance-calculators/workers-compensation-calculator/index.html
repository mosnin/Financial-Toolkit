Workers Compensation Premium Formula

Workers comp premiums follow a standard industry formula:

Workers Comp Premium Formula
Premium = (Payroll / $100) x Class Code Rate x Experience Modification Rate

The payroll is expressed per $100 of wages. The class code rate reflects the risk of the job type. The EMR adjusts the premium based on your claims history relative to your industry average.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual payroll - total wages for all employees.
  2. Select industry class code - the category that matches your employees' work.
  3. Enter your EMR - your experience modification rate (1.0 if new or unknown).
  4. Click "Calculate Premium" to see your estimated workers comp cost.

Example Scenario

A retail business with $500,000 in annual payroll, a class code rate of $1.50 per $100, and an EMR of 1.0 would pay approximately $7,500 per year in workers compensation premiums. If their EMR improved to 0.85 due to a good safety record, the premium would drop to about $6,375.

Frequently Asked Questions

Premiums are calculated by multiplying payroll per $100 by the class code rate, then by the EMR. The class code reflects job risk, and the EMR reflects your claims history.

The EMR compares your claims history to the industry average. 1.0 is average, below 1.0 means fewer claims (lower premium), above 1.0 means more claims (higher premium).

Class codes categorize businesses by work type and risk. Office workers have rates around $0.20-$0.50 per $100 of payroll, while construction may be $5-$15+ per $100.

Almost every state requires businesses with employees to carry workers compensation. Requirements vary by state and business size.

Implement strong safety programs, properly classify employees, maintain accurate payroll records, and establish return-to-work programs to lower your EMR over time.
