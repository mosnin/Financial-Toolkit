Real vs Nominal Return Formula

Fisher Equation
Real Return = ((1 + Nominal Rate) / (1 + Inflation Rate)) − 1

Nominal Future Value = Investment × (1 + Nominal Rate)^Years

Real Future Value = Investment × (1 + Real Rate)^Years

Lost to Inflation = Nominal Future Value − Real Future Value

The Fisher Equation, developed by economist Irving Fisher, gives the precise relationship between nominal returns, real returns, and inflation. Unlike the simple approximation (Nominal − Inflation), the Fisher Equation accounts for the compounding interaction between the return and inflation rate, which becomes significant at higher values.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your initial investment: The dollar amount you invested or plan to invest.
  2. Input the nominal return rate: The annual percentage return your investment is expected to earn, as shown on your brokerage statement or fund prospectus.
  3. Set the inflation rate: Use 3% for the long-term US average, or a recent figure from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).
  4. Enter the holding period: How many years you plan to hold the investment.
  5. Review your results: Compare the nominal vs real future value to understand how much of your apparent gain is real wealth creation versus simply keeping up with rising prices.

Example Scenario

Suppose you invest $10,000 in a fund earning 8% annually, with inflation running at 3%, held for 10 years:

  • Real return (Fisher Equation): ((1.08) / (1.03)) − 1 = 4.85% per year
  • Nominal future value: $10,000 × (1.08)^10 = $21,589
  • Real future value: $10,000 × (1.0485)^10 = $16,006
  • Lost to inflation: $21,589 − $16,006 = $5,583

While your account balance grows to $21,589, only $16,006 represents true purchasing power growth. More than $5,500 of your nominal gain is simply compensating for the rising cost of goods — it is not real wealth creation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Nominal return is the stated percentage gain on an investment before accounting for inflation. Real return is the inflation-adjusted gain — what you actually earned in terms of purchasing power. If your investment returned 8% but inflation was 3%, your real return is approximately 4.85%, not 5%.

Use the Fisher Equation: Real Return = ((1 + Nominal Rate) / (1 + Inflation Rate)) - 1. A simplified approximation is Real Return ≈ Nominal Rate − Inflation Rate, but the Fisher Equation is more accurate, especially at higher rates.

Real return tells you how much your purchasing power actually grew. A 6% nominal return during 6% inflation means you broke even in real terms — you can buy exactly the same amount of goods as before. Nominal returns can be misleading when inflation is high.

Historically, the US stock market has delivered a real return of approximately 7% per year after inflation. Bonds typically provide a real return of 1–3%. Any positive real return means you are growing your purchasing power, while a negative real return means inflation is outpacing your investment gains.
