Frequently Asked Questions

An ARM has a fixed rate for an initial period (typically 5, 7, or 10 years) then adjusts periodically based on a market index. ARMs often start with lower rates than fixed loans, making them attractive if you plan to sell or refinance before the adjustment period. Use our Adjustable Rate Mortgage Calculator to model worst-case payment scenarios before committing.

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is typically required when your LTV exceeds 80%, meaning your down payment is less than 20%. Once your LTV drops to 80% through payments or appreciation, you can request PMI cancellation, and lenders are legally required to remove it at 78% LTV. Our Loan-to-Value Ratio Calculator tracks your progress toward PMI elimination.

Leverage amplifies both gains and losses. If a property appreciates 5% and you put 20% down, your return on equity is roughly 25% (5% appreciation on 5x your investment). However, leverage also magnifies losses and increases your monthly obligation. Our Real Estate Leverage Calculator models different down payment scenarios to show the impact on your overall return.

Gross rental yields above 7–8% are generally considered strong, while net yields of 4–6% after expenses are respectable in most markets. High-appreciation markets like major cities often show lower yields, while smaller markets offer higher income returns. Use our Real Estate Yield Calculator to compute both gross and net yields and our Rental Expense Ratio Calculator to understand your cost structure.

Property managers typically charge 8% to 12% of monthly rent plus leasing fees. Self-management saves that cost but requires your time for tenant screening, repairs, and administration. For out-of-state investors or those with multiple properties, professional management often pays for itself. Our Property Management Cost Calculator shows the exact dollar cost so you can weigh it against your time.
