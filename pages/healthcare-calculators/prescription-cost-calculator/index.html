Prescription Cost Calculator

Prescription drug costs are one of the most significant and often surprising healthcare expenses. This calculator estimates your annual and monthly out-of-pocket prescription costs based on your drug tier, insurance copay amount, and number of annual refills — and shows exactly how much you save by choosing generics over brand-name medications.

Prescription Cost Formula

The calculation is simple but the savings opportunities are significant:

Annual Prescription Cost
Annual Cost = Copay per Fill × Annual Refills
Monthly Cost
Monthly Cost = Annual Cost / 12
Annual Savings vs Brand-Name (Tier 3)
Savings = (Brand Copay - Generic Copay) × Annual Refills

Generic drugs use Tier 1 copays versus Tier 3 brand-name copays to calculate the annual savings from switching.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your drug tier - find this on your plan's drug formulary or insurance card. Common prescriptions are listed by tier.
  2. Confirm or adjust the copay - use the pre-filled amount or enter your actual copay from your insurance card.
  3. Enter annual refills - 12 for a monthly prescription; 4 for quarterly; 52 for weekly.
  4. Click Calculate to see your annual cost, monthly cost, and brand-name savings.

Example Scenario

A patient takes a blood pressure medication monthly. The brand-name version (Tier 3) has an $80 copay — costing $960 per year. Their doctor confirms a generic equivalent is available at a Tier 1 $10 copay. Switching saves $840 per year ($70/month) with no difference in medical effectiveness. Multiplied across two prescriptions, the savings could exceed $1,680 annually — simply by having a conversation with your doctor about generics.

Frequently Asked Questions

Drug tiers are categories used by insurance plans to set different copay levels. Tier 1 (generic) has the lowest copays. Tier 2 (preferred brand) is mid-range. Tier 3 (non-preferred brand) costs more. Tier 4 specialty drugs are the most expensive, often with coinsurance rather than flat copays.

Generic drugs are chemically equivalent to brand-name drugs but typically cost 80-85% less at list price. A brand-name medication with a $50 copay might have a generic equivalent with a $10 copay, saving $480 per year. Ask your doctor if a generic is available.

Specialty drugs are complex biologics or high-cost medications for serious conditions. They require special handling and administration. List prices can exceed $10,000/month. Patient assistance programs from manufacturers can significantly reduce these costs for qualifying patients.

Under the Inflation Reduction Act, Medicare Part D out-of-pocket prescription costs are capped at $2,000 per year starting in 2025, eliminating catastrophic drug cost exposure. This applies to Medicare beneficiaries, not marketplace or employer insurance plans.
