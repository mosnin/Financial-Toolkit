Working Capital Formulas

These liquidity measures are standard balance sheet ratios used by lenders, investors, and management teams.

Working Capital
Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities
Current Ratio
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio (Acid-Test)
Quick Ratio = (Cash + Receivables) / Current Liabilities
Current Assets
Current Assets = Cash + Accounts Receivable + Inventory
Current Liabilities
Current Liabilities = Accounts Payable + Short-Term Debt

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter cash and equivalents -- all bank account balances and highly liquid funds.
  2. Enter accounts receivable -- the total amount customers owe you that you expect to collect within 12 months.
  3. Enter inventory -- the value of all goods held for sale (leave blank or 0 for service businesses).
  4. Enter accounts payable -- amounts you owe to suppliers and vendors due within 12 months.
  5. Enter short-term debt and other liabilities -- any loans or obligations due within 12 months.
  6. Click "Calculate Working Capital" to see your working capital, current ratio, quick ratio, and adequacy assessment.

Example Scenario

A wholesale distributor has: Cash $50,000, Receivables $80,000, Inventory $40,000, Payables $30,000, Short-Term Debt $20,000.

  • Current Assets: $50,000 + $80,000 + $40,000 = $170,000
  • Current Liabilities: $30,000 + $20,000 = $50,000
  • Working Capital: $170,000 - $50,000 = $120,000
  • Current Ratio: $170,000 / $50,000 = 3.4x
  • Quick Ratio: ($50,000 + $80,000) / $50,000 = 2.6x

Both ratios are well above 1.0, indicating strong liquidity. The business can comfortably meet short-term obligations and has a healthy buffer for growth investments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Working capital is the difference between a company's current assets and current liabilities. It measures the short-term liquidity available to fund daily operations and is calculated as Current Assets - Current Liabilities.

A current ratio between 1.5 and 3.0 is generally considered healthy. A ratio below 1.0 means current liabilities exceed current assets, which may indicate liquidity risk. A ratio above 3.0 could suggest inefficient use of assets.

The quick ratio (acid-test ratio) measures immediate liquidity by excluding inventory from current assets. It is calculated as (Cash + Receivables) / Current Liabilities. A quick ratio above 1.0 is generally considered safe.

To improve working capital: speed up receivables collection, negotiate extended supplier payment terms, reduce excess inventory, secure a revolving credit line, or retain more earnings rather than distributing profits.
