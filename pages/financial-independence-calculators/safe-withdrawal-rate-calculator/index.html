Safe Withdrawal Rate Calculator

Your safe withdrawal rate (SWR) determines whether your retirement portfolio can fund your lifestyle without running out of money. This calculator computes your actual withdrawal rate from your portfolio, delivers a color-coded safety assessment against the 4% rule, estimates how long your portfolio will last, and shows the maximum sustainable withdrawal for your situation.

How the Safe Withdrawal Rate Is Assessed

Formula
Withdrawal Rate = Desired Annual Income ÷ Portfolio Value × 100%
Sustainable Withdrawal = Portfolio Value × 0.04

Safety Assessment:
• Green: ≤ 3.5% — Very safe, conservative
• Yellow: 3.5%–4.5% — Safe for most horizons
• Red: > 4.5% — Elevated risk of depletion

Longevity: estimated years until portfolio depletes at real return minus withdrawal rate

Portfolio longevity is estimated by simulating annual withdrawals at your desired income level against a real return derived from your stock/bond allocation. A 60/40 portfolio historically earned about 5% real; a 100% stock portfolio about 6.5% real. Higher stock allocations extend longevity but increase year-to-year volatility.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your portfolio value: Total of all retirement investment accounts.
  2. Set your desired annual income: The gross amount you plan to withdraw each year.
  3. Choose your retirement horizon: How many years you need the portfolio to last. 30 years is standard; use 40–50 for early retirees.
  4. Enter stock allocation: Your percentage in equities (bonds make up the remainder).
  5. Review the assessment: Green means your rate is conservative, yellow is moderate, red signals elevated risk.

Example Scenario

Morgan has a $1,200,000 portfolio and wants to withdraw $52,000/year. That's a 4.33% withdrawal rate — in the yellow zone. With a 60/40 stock-bond allocation and a 35-year horizon, the calculator estimates the portfolio lasts approximately 36 years. The 4% rule sustainable amount is $48,000/year — $4,000 less than Morgan's desired income. To stay safely in the green, Morgan could reduce spending by $4,000/year or work toward a slightly larger portfolio before retiring.
