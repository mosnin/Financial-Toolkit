Frequently Asked Questions

The average cost of a four-year degree varies widely by school type. Public in-state universities average around $25,000–$30,000 per year all-in, while private universities can exceed $60,000 per year. Use our College Tuition Cost Calculator to model total costs including annual tuition increases over your full enrollment period.

Student loans can be worthwhile when the degree leads to higher lifetime earnings that exceed the total loan cost with interest. A general guideline is to borrow no more than your expected first-year salary. Our Education ROI Calculator helps you evaluate whether the projected earnings premium justifies the debt load for your chosen field.

The most effective strategies include making extra principal payments, refinancing to a lower interest rate after graduation, and avoiding income-driven repayment plans if you can afford standard payments. Our Student Loan Interest Savings Calculator shows exactly how much you save with each approach.

It depends heavily on your college's location and housing market. In high-cost cities, off-campus apartments are often more expensive than dorms. In college towns, sharing a house off-campus can be significantly cheaper. Use our Student Housing Cost Calculator to compare both options side by side with real numbers.

Education ROI compares the total cost of a degree (tuition, fees, forgone income while in school) against the lifetime earnings premium it provides over a high school diploma. Our Education ROI Calculator automates this comparison so you can evaluate different schools, majors, and degree levels side by side.
