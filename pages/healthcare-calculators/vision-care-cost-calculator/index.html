How Vision Cost Is Calculated

Annual vision costs have two components: the eye exam (annualized based on frequency) and eyewear costs. Insurance is evaluated by comparing total spending with and without the plan:

Annual Total (Without Insurance)
Total = (Exam Cost × Exam Frequency) + Annual Eyewear Cost
Annual Total (With Insurance)
Total = Insurance Premium + max(0, Annual Care Cost - Coverage Amount)
Annual Savings from Insurance
Savings = Without Insurance Total - With Insurance Total

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select exam frequency - how often you typically get an eye exam.
  2. Enter exam cost - what you'd pay out-of-pocket without any insurance.
  3. Select eyewear type - your corrective eyewear situation (pre-filled estimates adjust automatically).
  4. Enter annual eyewear cost - your realistic spend on glasses or contacts each year.
  5. Enter vision insurance coverage - the total annual benefit your plan provides (check your Summary of Benefits).
  6. Enter vision insurance premium - your annual premium cost for the vision plan.
  7. Click Calculate to see with vs without comparison and whether insurance is worth it.

Example Scenario

A contact lens wearer gets an annual exam ($150) and buys daily disposable contacts ($500/year) for a total of $650 without insurance. Their vision plan costs $150/year in premiums and covers $300 of benefits. With insurance: $150 premium + ($650 - $300 covered) = $150 + $350 = $500. Insurance saves $150 annually. If they also need new glasses every other year ($300 amortized to $150/year), total without insurance rises to $800. Insurance becomes an even clearer winner at $500 vs $800.

Frequently Asked Questions

A comprehensive eye exam without insurance typically costs $100-$250 depending on location and provider type. Retail vision chains often offer exams for $75-$100. Contact lens fittings add $20-$60.

Vision insurance is worth it if your annual covered care costs exceed the premium. For someone who gets an annual exam and new glasses, vision insurance typically saves $100-$300 annually. Use this calculator to find your specific break-even point.

Basic single-vision glasses cost $100-$200. Progressive lenses add $150-$300. Premium coatings and designer frames can push totals to $400-$800+. Online retailers like Zenni or Warby Parker offer complete pairs starting around $20-$95.

Glasses are generally cheaper long-term since a pair lasts 2-3 years. Daily disposable contacts cost $400-$700+ per year. Biweekly or monthly contacts run $200-$400 annually plus solution. Many people combine both for different activities.
