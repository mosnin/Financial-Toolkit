Total Cost of Ownership Formula

The total cost of ownership sums all vehicle-related expenses over the ownership period:

Total Cost of Ownership
Total = Purchase Price + (Insurance + Fuel + Maintenance) x Years Owned
Cost Per Mile
Cost Per Mile = Total Cost / (Annual Miles x Years Owned)

How to Use This Vehicle Total Cost Calculator

  1. Enter the purchase price - the total price you paid or will pay for the vehicle.
  2. Enter annual insurance - your yearly auto insurance premium.
  3. Enter annual fuel cost - your estimated yearly fuel expense (use our Fuel Cost Calculator).
  4. Enter annual maintenance - yearly maintenance, repairs, tires, and service costs.
  5. Enter years of ownership - how long you plan to keep the vehicle.
  6. Enter annual miles - your typical yearly driving distance.

Example Scenario

Suppose you buy a $35,000 car, pay $1,800/year for insurance, $2,100/year for fuel, and $1,200/year for maintenance, and keep it for 5 years driving 12,000 miles annually. Your total cost of ownership would be $60,500 ($35,000 + $25,500 in operating costs). That works out to $12,100 per year, $1,008 per month, or about $1.01 per mile.

Frequently Asked Questions

The total cost of owning a car includes the purchase price (or loan payments), insurance, fuel, maintenance and repairs, registration, taxes, and depreciation. AAA estimates the average cost of owning a new car is over $10,000 per year.

According to AAA, the average cost per mile to own and operate a new vehicle is roughly $0.60-$0.80 per mile, depending on the vehicle type. This includes all costs: depreciation, insurance, fuel, maintenance, fees, and financing.

You can reduce costs by buying a reliable used vehicle, shopping for competitive insurance rates, performing regular maintenance, driving fuel-efficiently, and keeping the vehicle longer to spread the purchase cost over more years.

Used cars are typically cheaper to own overall because they have already undergone the steepest depreciation. However, older vehicles may have higher maintenance costs and lower fuel efficiency. A 2-3 year old certified pre-owned vehicle often offers the best balance.

Budget for oil changes ($30-$100 each), tire replacement ($400-$800 per set), brake service ($200-$600), battery replacement ($100-$250), and general repairs. A good rule is to budget $75-$150 per month for maintenance and repairs on an average vehicle.
