DTI Ratio Formula

The debt-to-income ratio is calculated by dividing your total monthly debt payments by your gross monthly income:

Debt-to-Income Ratio
DTI = (Total Monthly Debt Payments / Gross Monthly Income) x 100

For example, if your monthly debts total $1,500 and your gross monthly income is $5,000, your DTI ratio is 30%.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Add up all monthly debt payments - include mortgage or rent, auto loans, student loans, credit card minimum payments, personal loans, and child support.
  2. Enter your gross monthly income - your total earnings before taxes and deductions.
  3. Click "Calculate DTI Ratio" to see your ratio, rating, and how lenders would view your profile.

Example Scenario

Sarah earns $6,000 per month before taxes. Her monthly obligations include a $1,200 mortgage payment, $350 car loan, $200 student loan, and $150 in credit card minimums, totaling $1,900 in debt payments. Her DTI ratio is $1,900 / $6,000 = 31.7%, which falls in the "good" range and would be viewed favorably by most lenders.

Frequently Asked Questions

A DTI ratio of 36% or less is generally considered good by most lenders. A ratio below 20% is excellent, while anything above 43% may make it difficult to qualify for a mortgage.

Lenders use DTI to assess your ability to manage monthly payments and repay borrowed money. It is one of the key factors in mortgage and loan approval decisions, alongside credit score and employment history.

Monthly debt payments include mortgage or rent, car loans, student loans, minimum credit card payments, personal loans, child support, and any other recurring debt obligations. It does not include utilities, groceries, or insurance.

You can lower your DTI by paying down existing debts, avoiding new debt, increasing your income through raises or side work, or refinancing loans to lower monthly payments.

Front-end DTI only includes housing-related costs (mortgage, taxes, insurance). Back-end DTI includes all monthly debt obligations. Lenders typically look at both, with ideal front-end DTI below 28% and back-end below 36%.
