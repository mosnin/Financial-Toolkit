Frequently Asked Questions

Financial health means having a stable income, manageable debt, an adequate emergency fund, consistent savings, and a growing net worth. Key benchmarks include a debt-to-income ratio below 36%, emergency savings of 3–6 months of expenses, and a savings rate of at least 15–20%.

Lenders generally want your total DTI (all debt payments divided by gross monthly income) below 43%. A DTI under 28% for housing costs alone is considered excellent. Above 43% total indicates financial strain and limits your borrowing options.

Do a full financial review at least once a year — or whenever a major life change occurs (new job, marriage, child, home purchase). Monthly check-ins on your budget and savings keep you on track between annual reviews.

At minimum, you should have health insurance, auto insurance (if you own a car), renters or homeowners insurance, and ideally a term life insurance policy if others depend on your income. Disability insurance is often overlooked but critically important.
