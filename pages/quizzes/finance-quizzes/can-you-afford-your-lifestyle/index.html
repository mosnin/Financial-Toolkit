Can You Afford Your Lifestyle?

Lifestyle affordability isn't just about whether you can pay your bills this month — it's about whether your spending patterns are sustainable long-term. This 8-question quiz examines the key spending ratios and habits that determine if your lifestyle is within your means, on the edge, or quietly accumulating financial pressure you'll feel later.

Frequently Asked Questions

The traditional rule is to spend no more than 30% of gross income on housing. In high-cost cities this often isn't realistic, but exceeding 40% leaves very little room for savings, debt repayment, or unexpected expenses.

Lifestyle inflation — or lifestyle creep — occurs when spending increases at the same rate as income, leaving savings unchanged. It's dangerous because it normalises a higher cost of living that becomes hard to reverse and leaves you dependent on maintaining your current income indefinitely.

A common guideline is to keep all vehicle costs (payment, insurance, fuel, maintenance) under 15–20% of take-home pay. A car payment exceeding 10% of monthly income alone is a financial strain signal.

There's no magic number, but the issue is subscription creep — small charges that individually feel trivial but collectively add up to $100–$500/month. Do a quarterly audit and cancel anything you haven't used in the past 30 days.
