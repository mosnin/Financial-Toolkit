Frequently Asked Questions

Start with your target annual income, add business expenses and a profit margin, then divide by the number of billable hours you realistically expect in a year. Most freelancers find they bill only 60–70% of their working hours, so a 40-hour week translates to roughly 1,200–1,400 billable hours annually. Use our Project Pricing Calculator and Hourly to Annual Income Calculator to model different scenarios.

A common guideline is to set aside 25–30% of net freelance income for federal and state taxes. Self-employment tax alone is 15.3% on your net earnings (though you can deduct half of it). The exact amount depends on your income level, filing status, deductions, and state tax rate. Our Freelance Tax Estimator gives a personalized estimate based on your actual numbers.

Utilization rate is the percentage of your total available work hours that are billable to clients. A rate of 60–70% is considered healthy for independent freelancers because the remaining time goes to marketing, admin, and business development. Tracking your utilization rate helps you identify whether low earnings stem from underpricing or simply insufficient billable work.

Yes, contractor rates are typically 20–40% higher than equivalent employee salaries to compensate for self-employment taxes, no employer benefits, and income variability. When comparing an offer, you need to add back the value of health insurance, retirement matching, paid leave, and other perks to make a true apples-to-apples comparison. Use our Contractor vs Employee Income Calculator for a side-by-side breakdown.

Retainer agreements provide predictable monthly income, reduce the time spent on sales and proposal writing, and deepen client relationships. In exchange for guaranteed availability, freelancers often offer a modest discount on their standard rate. The key is pricing retainers to reflect both the hours reserved and the value of priority access. Our Retainer Pricing Calculator helps you find the right number.
