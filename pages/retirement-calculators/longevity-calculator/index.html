Longevity Estimation Method

The calculator starts from actuarial baselines and applies lifestyle adjustments based on published longevity research:

Base Life Expectancy (US Actuarial Average)
Male base: 76 years | Female base: 81 years
Lifestyle Adjustments
Exercise: -3 (sedentary) to +4 (very active) years Diet: -2 (poor) to +3 (excellent) years Smoking: -10 (current) to 0 (never) years Family history: -4 (significant) to 0 (none) years

The planning horizon adds a 5-year safety buffer to the estimated longevity age to guard against outliving your savings.

How to Use This Longevity Calculator

  1. Enter your current age and select your gender for the actuarial baseline.
  2. Select your exercise level - be honest about your typical weekly activity.
  3. Select your diet quality - consider your average eating habits over the past year.
  4. Select your smoking status - this is one of the largest single factors.
  5. Select family history - major diseases in close relatives before age 75 matter most.
  6. Enter your expected retirement age to calculate your years in retirement.
  7. Click "Estimate Longevity" to see your estimated life expectancy and planning horizon.

Example Scenario

A 45-year-old woman who exercises moderately, eats a good diet, never smoked, and has no significant family history starts with a base of 81 years. Adjustments for moderate exercise (+2) and good diet (+1) bring her estimate to 84. Her conservative planning horizon is 89. If she retires at 65, she should plan for 24 years of retirement expenses and build her savings accordingly.
