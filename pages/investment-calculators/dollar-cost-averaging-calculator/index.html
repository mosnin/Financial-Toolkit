Dollar-Cost Averaging Formula

The future value of a series of equal monthly contributions with compound interest is calculated as:

Future Value of Contributions
FV = PMT × [(1 + r)^n − 1] / r

Where PMT is the monthly payment, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12), and n is the total number of months. The initial investment compounds separately:

Future Value of Initial Investment
FV_initial = P × (1 + r)^n

Example Scenario

Suppose you invest $500 per month for 20 years with an 8% annual return and no initial investment. You would invest a total of $120,000, but your portfolio would grow to approximately $294,510. The total gain of roughly $174,510 comes entirely from compounding returns. The effective average cost per dollar invested is significantly less than the final portfolio value per dollar invested, demonstrating the power of consistent investing over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed amount of money at regular intervals regardless of market conditions. This reduces the impact of volatility by buying more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

DCA reduces timing risk — the risk of investing a large lump sum at a market peak. However, it does not eliminate market risk. In a steadily rising market, a lump-sum investment often outperforms DCA, but DCA reduces emotional and psychological barriers to investing.

A common guideline is to invest 15–20% of your gross income. Start with whatever amount you can consistently commit to without straining your budget. Even small amounts invested regularly can grow significantly over long periods due to compounding.

The effective average cost is the total amount invested divided by the total portfolio value relative to the initial share price. In practice, it represents your average cost basis per unit purchased, which tends to be lower than the average price over the period.
