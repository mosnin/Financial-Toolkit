HSA Growth Formula

The HSA balance is calculated using the future value of an annuity formula, exactly like a retirement account:

HSA Balance at Retirement (Future Value of Annuity)
FV = PMT × [(1 + r)^n - 1] / r

Where PMT is annual contribution, r is the annual return rate, and n is years until retirement. Annual tax savings equals your contribution multiplied by your marginal tax rate — the immediate benefit from the deduction.

Annual Tax Savings
Tax Savings = Annual Contribution × (Marginal Tax Rate / 100)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual HSA contribution - up to the IRS limit ($4,300 for self-only, $8,550 for family in 2026).
  2. Enter expected annual return - use 5-7% for a diversified investment portfolio; use 2-3% if keeping funds in cash.
  3. Enter years until retirement - years remaining in your career.
  4. Enter your marginal tax rate - your federal income tax bracket (22%, 24%, 32%, etc.).
  5. Click Calculate to see projected balance, tax savings, and growth.

Example Scenario

A 40-year-old contributes the maximum self-only amount of $4,300/year to their HSA, invests it at 6% annually, and has 25 years until retirement. They are in the 24% tax bracket. At retirement, their HSA balance would grow to approximately $237,000. Their annual tax savings are $1,032. Over 25 years, they will have contributed $107,500 total, with the remaining $129,500 coming from investment returns — all of which can be withdrawn tax-free for medical expenses in retirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

For 2026, the IRS HSA contribution limits are approximately $4,300 for self-only HDHP coverage and $8,550 for family coverage. Individuals age 55 or older can contribute an additional $1,000 catch-up amount. Limits are adjusted annually for inflation.

HSAs offer three tax benefits: contributions are tax-deductible or pre-tax; investment earnings grow tax-free; and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. This makes the HSA the most tax-advantaged savings account available.

Yes. Most HSA providers allow you to invest funds in mutual funds, ETFs, and other securities once your balance exceeds a threshold (often $1,000-$2,000). Investing your HSA for long-term growth rather than spending it annually maximizes its retirement value.

After age 65, you can withdraw HSA funds for any purpose without penalty (though non-medical withdrawals are taxed like a Traditional IRA). Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses remain tax-free at any age, making the HSA an outstanding retirement medical expense fund.
