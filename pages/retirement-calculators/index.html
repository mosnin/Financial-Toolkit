Frequently Asked Questions

A common rule of thumb is to save 25 times your annual retirement expenses, which corresponds to a 4% withdrawal rate. However, your exact target depends on your lifestyle, Social Security income, pension benefits, healthcare costs, and life expectancy. Use our Retirement Nest Egg Calculator to get a personalized estimate.

You can claim as early as age 62 at a reduced benefit or delay until age 70 to receive the maximum amount. Delaying increases your benefit by roughly 8% per year between full retirement age and 70. Our Social Security Estimator helps you compare the lifetime value of claiming at different ages so you can choose the optimal strategy.

An RMD is the minimum amount the IRS requires you to withdraw annually from tax-deferred retirement accounts like traditional IRAs and 401(k)s, starting at age 73 under current law. Failing to take your RMD results in a steep penalty. Use our RMD Calculator to determine exactly how much you must withdraw each year.

The 4% rule was developed from historical market data and suggests you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio in year one, then adjust for inflation, with a high probability of not running out of money over 30 years. In today's lower-yield environment, many planners suggest 3% to 3.5% for longer retirements. Our Retirement Withdrawal Rate Calculator lets you model different scenarios.

At minimum, contribute enough to capture your full employer match — that is an immediate 50% to 100% return on your contribution. Beyond that, aim to save 15% of gross income including employer contributions. Our 401(k) Match Calculator shows exactly how much your employer match is worth over time.
