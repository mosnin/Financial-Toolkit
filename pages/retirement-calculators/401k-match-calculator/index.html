401(k) Match Formula

The calculator uses the following logic to determine your match and any uncaptured free money:

Your Annual Contribution
Your Contribution = Annual Salary x (Employee Contribution % / 100)
Employer Match
Eligible Contribution = min(Your Contribution %, Match Cap %) Employer Match = Annual Salary x (Eligible Contribution % / 100) x (Employer Match Rate / 100)
Free Money Left on Table
Max Match = Annual Salary x (Match Cap % / 100) x (Employer Match Rate / 100) Left on Table = Max Match - Actual Employer Match

If your contribution rate is at or above the match cap, you are capturing the full match and there is no money left on the table.

How to Use This 401(k) Match Calculator

  1. Enter your annual salary - your gross pre-tax salary before any deductions.
  2. Enter your contribution rate - the percentage of your salary you currently contribute to your 401(k).
  3. Enter the employer match rate - the percentage your employer contributes per dollar you put in (e.g., 50 for 50 cents per dollar).
  4. Enter the match cap - the maximum percentage of your salary that qualifies for the match (check your plan documents).
  5. Click "Calculate Match" to see your totals and whether you are maximizing free money.

Example Scenario

Sarah earns $80,000 per year. Her employer offers a 100% match up to 4% of salary, meaning the employer matches dollar-for-dollar on the first 4% she contributes. Sarah currently contributes only 2% ($1,600/year). Her employer matches $1,600, but the maximum possible employer match is $3,200 (4% of $80,000). Sarah is leaving $1,600 in free money on the table each year — simply by raising her contribution from 2% to 4% she doubles her employer's annual gift to her retirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

An employer match is when your employer contributes money to your 401(k) based on how much you contribute. For example, a 50% match up to 6% means if you contribute 6% of salary, your employer adds another 3%. This is essentially free money added to your retirement savings.

The match cap is the maximum percentage of your salary that your employer will match against. If the employer matches 100% up to 4%, and you earn $60,000, the employer will contribute at most $2,400 (4% of $60,000) regardless of how much more you contribute.

You should always contribute at least enough to capture the full employer match — it is an instant 50–100% return on your money. If your employer matches 100% up to 5% of your salary, contribute at least 5% to avoid leaving free money on the table.

Yes, many employers apply a vesting schedule to matching contributions. Common schedules are cliff vesting (e.g., 100% after 3 years) or graded vesting (e.g., 20% per year over 5 years). Your own contributions are always 100% vested immediately.
