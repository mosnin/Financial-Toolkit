Portfolio Volatility Formulas

For each asset, the standard deviation of the entered period returns is calculated using the population standard deviation formula:

Standard Deviation (per asset)
σ = √[ Σ(rᵢ − r̄)² / n ]

Where rᵢ are the individual period returns and r̄ is their mean. The weighted average portfolio volatility is then:

Weighted Portfolio Volatility
Portfolio Volatility = Σ (Weight_i × σ_i) / Total Weight

Note: This is a simplified linear weighted model. A full covariance-matrix approach would require pairwise correlation data but is beyond the scope of this calculator.

How to Use This Volatility Calculator

  1. Enter asset returns — type the periodic returns for each asset as comma-separated percentages (e.g. annual or quarterly returns over several periods).
  2. Enter weights — the percentage of your portfolio each asset represents. Weights do not need to sum to exactly 100% but the total is shown for reference.
  3. Leave unused rows blank — you can use as few as 1 or as many as 5 assets.
  4. Click "Calculate Volatility" to see each asset's standard deviation, the weighted portfolio volatility, and a risk level label.

Example Scenario

Suppose your portfolio holds three assets: a stock fund with annual returns of 10%, -5%, 8%, 12%, -3% (40% weight); a bond fund with returns of 5%, 2%, 6%, 4%, 3% (30% weight); and a growth stock with returns of 15%, -10%, 20%, -5%, 10% (30% weight). The stock fund's standard deviation is about 7.3%, the bond fund's about 1.4%, and the growth stock's about 10.8%. The weighted portfolio volatility would be approximately 6.2%, falling into the "Moderate" risk category.

Frequently Asked Questions

Portfolio volatility measures how much the value of a portfolio fluctuates over time. It is typically expressed as the standard deviation of returns. Higher volatility means larger swings in value — both upward and downward.

It depends on your risk tolerance and time horizon. The S&P 500 has historically had annualized volatility of around 15–20%. Conservative portfolios might target 5–10% volatility through diversification and bond allocation. Aggressive growth portfolios may accept 20–30% or higher.

Standard deviation in investing measures how much an asset's returns vary from its average return. A higher standard deviation means more unpredictable returns. For example, an asset with a 10% average return and a 20% standard deviation might realistically return anywhere from -10% to +30% in a given year.

Diversification is the primary way to reduce volatility. Holding assets that are not highly correlated — such as stocks, bonds, real estate, and cash — means their returns partially offset each other, smoothing the overall portfolio's performance.
