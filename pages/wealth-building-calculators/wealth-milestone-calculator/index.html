Wealth Milestone Calculator

Knowing exactly when you'll reach your next financial milestone — $50k, $100k, $250k, $500k, $1M, or $2M — transforms abstract savings goals into concrete dates on a calendar. This calculator takes your current savings balance, monthly contributions, expected return rate, and starting age to produce a precise milestone timeline with target dates for each major wealth landmark.

Wealth Milestone Formula

Milestones are calculated by projecting portfolio growth month by month until each target is reached:

Portfolio at Month m
P(m) = P0 × (1 + r/12)m + PMT × [((1 + r/12)m − 1) / (r/12)]
Age at Milestone
Agemilestone = Starting Age + (months to milestone / 12)
Target Date
Date = Today + months to milestone

Where P0 is current savings, r is annual return rate, and PMT is monthly contribution. The calculator iterates month by month until each milestone balance is reached.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current savings — your total invested savings balance today.
  2. Enter your monthly contribution — how much you invest each month.
  3. Enter the annual return rate — your expected annual investment return.
  4. Enter your starting age — your current age for age-based milestone display.
  5. Click "Calculate Milestones" to see target years, ages, and calendar dates for each milestone.

Example Scenario

A 28-year-old with $25,000 in savings contributing $500/month at a 7% annual return reaches $50,000 in approximately 3.2 years (age 31), $100,000 in about 7 years (age 35), $250,000 in roughly 16 years (age 44), and $500,000 around age 51. The $1 million milestone arrives at approximately age 57, and $2 million by age 65. Notice how the years between milestones shrink as the portfolio grows — from 4 years to reach $100k after $50k, to less than 3 years to go from $500k to $1M.

Frequently Asked Questions

The first $100,000 is hardest because investment returns are still small relative to contributions. At $10,000, a 7% return only adds $700/year. But once you hit $100,000, that same 7% adds $7,000/year — nearly matching many people's monthly savings contribution. This is why Charlie Munger famously called the first $100,000 difficult to accumulate.

Milestones accelerate dramatically after the first $100k because compounding gains become substantial. It might take 7 years to reach $100k, but only 5 more years to reach $200k, and then fewer years to double again. Each successive milestone is typically reached in less time than the previous one.

Starting age is critical. Someone who begins saving $500/month at 22 with 7% returns can reach $1 million by age 57. Starting the same plan at age 32 pushes that milestone to age 64. A 10-year head start reduces the timeline to $1 million by approximately 7 years due to compounding.
