Frequently Asked Questions

The average US household spends roughly $200–$300 per month on all utilities combined, including electricity, gas, water, sewer, and trash. Electricity alone averages around $115–$140 per month nationally, but costs vary significantly by region, climate, home size, and efficiency. Use our Electricity Cost and Gas Bill Calculators to get an estimate specific to your usage.

Solar is typically worth it if your electricity bill is above $100/month, your roof has good sun exposure, and you plan to stay in your home for 5+ years. The federal solar tax credit and net metering policies significantly improve the economics. Our Solar Savings Calculator models the full 25-year return including installation costs, incentives, and projected electricity savings.

On average, EVs cost about 2 to 4 times less per mile to fuel than gasoline vehicles. The exact savings depend on your local electricity rate, gasoline price, and the efficiency of both vehicles. At average US rates, an EV costs roughly $0.03–$0.05 per mile to charge versus $0.10–$0.15 per mile for a gasoline car. Use our EV Range Calculator for a personalized comparison.

The highest-impact upgrades are typically air sealing and insulation, upgrading to a heat pump or efficient HVAC system, installing a programmable thermostat, replacing old appliances with Energy Star models, and switching to LED lighting. Our Home Energy Efficiency Calculator ranks improvements by annual savings and payback period so you can prioritize the highest-ROI upgrades first.

A household carbon footprint is the total greenhouse gas emissions produced by your home's energy use, transportation, diet, and purchases. In the US, the average household emits roughly 16 tons of CO2 equivalent per year. Our Carbon Footprint Calculator breaks down emissions by category so you can identify the highest-impact areas to reduce.
