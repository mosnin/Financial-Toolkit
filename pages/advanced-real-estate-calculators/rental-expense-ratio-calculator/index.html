Expense Ratio Formula

Expense Ratio Calculations
Total Expenses = Mortgage + Tax + Insurance + Maintenance + Management + Vacancy Operating Expenses = Total Expenses − Mortgage (excludes debt service) Expense Ratio = Total Expenses / Gross Income × 100 NOI = Gross Income − Operating Expenses (excludes mortgage) Net Income = Gross Income − Total Expenses Net Income Margin = Net Income / Gross Income × 100

NOI is a financing-neutral measure of property performance -- it excludes mortgage payments to show how the property performs regardless of how it is funded. This is the standard metric used for cap rate and property valuation.

Net income (after mortgage) represents the actual cash flow the investor takes home each year after all obligations are met.

Example Scenario

$24,000 Gross Income Rental Property

A rental property generates $24,000/year. Annual expenses: mortgage $15,000, property tax $4,000, insurance $1,200, maintenance $1,500, management $2,400, vacancy $1,200.

Total Expenses = $15,000 + $4,000 + $1,200 + $1,500 + $2,400 + $1,200 = $25,300

Operating Expenses (ex-mortgage) = $10,300

NOI = $24,000 − $10,300 = $13,700

Net Income = $24,000 − $25,300 = −$1,300 (negative cash flow)

This shows the property is slightly cash-flow negative, signaling potential need for rent increase or expense reduction.

Frequently Asked Questions

The rental expense ratio is the percentage of gross rental income consumed by operating expenses. It is calculated by dividing total operating expenses by gross rental income. A lower ratio means more income flows through to the owner.

A well-managed residential rental typically has an expense ratio between 35% and 45%. Ratios above 50% suggest the property may be over-leveraged, poorly managed, or in need of significant maintenance. Commercial properties often have different norms depending on lease structure.
