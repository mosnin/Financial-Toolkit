Churn Rate Formulas

These formulas quantify the rate and impact of customer attrition on your business.

Churn Rate
Churn Rate = (Customers Lost / Customers at Start) x 100
Retention Rate
Retention Rate = 100% - Churn Rate
MRR Impact
MRR Lost = Customers Lost x Average Revenue per Customer
LTV Impact (Simplified)
LTV Lost = MRR Lost / Monthly Churn Rate

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter customers at start of period -- the total number of paying customers at the beginning of the month or year.
  2. Enter customers lost -- the number of customers who cancelled, churned, or did not renew during the period.

Example Scenario

A SaaS company starts the month with 500 customers, loses 25, and charges an average of $99/month.

  • Churn Rate: (25 / 500) x 100 = 5%
  • Retention Rate: 100% - 5% = 95%
  • MRR Lost: 25 x $99 = $2,475/month
  • LTV Lost: $2,475 / 0.05 = $49,500 in potential lifetime value

A 5% monthly churn rate is quite high for SaaS. Reducing churn to 2% would recover $1,485/month in MRR and dramatically extend average customer lifespan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Customer churn rate is the percentage of customers who stop doing business with a company during a given period. It is calculated as (Customers Lost / Customers at Start of Period) x 100.

For SaaS, a monthly churn rate below 2% is considered good, while under 0.5% is excellent. Enterprise SaaS often achieves under 1% monthly. Annual churn below 5-7% is generally considered healthy.

Churn directly reduces LTV. Higher churn shortens the average customer lifespan, reducing how long they pay for your product. LTV is often calculated as Average Revenue per Customer / Churn Rate.

Monthly churn measures customers lost per month; annual churn measures customers lost per year. A 2% monthly churn compounds to roughly 22% annual churn, which is why even small monthly rates matter significantly.
