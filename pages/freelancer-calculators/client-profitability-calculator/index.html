Client Profitability Formula

Total Hours
Total = Billable Hours + PM/Admin Hours + Revision Hours
Effective Hourly Rate
Effective Rate = (Monthly Revenue - Late Payment Impact) / Total Hours
Net Monthly Profit
Net Profit = Revenue - (Total Hours × Target Rate) - Late Payment Impact
Profit Margin
Margin = Net Monthly Profit / Monthly Revenue × 100
Opportunity Cost
Opportunity Cost = (Target Rate - Effective Rate) × Total Hours

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the client's monthly revenue (what they pay you each month on average).
  2. Set your target hourly rate — the rate you want to earn in a perfect scenario.
  3. Enter actual billable hours, plus project management/admin hours and revision hours for that client.
  4. Estimate the monthly late payment impact in dollars (time spent chasing invoices, interest cost).
  5. Click Analyze Client to see the verdict and complete profitability breakdown.

Example Scenario

A client pays $5,000/month. The freelancer spends 30 billable hours, 5 PM hours, and 3 revision hours, with $100 in late payment costs. Target rate is $100/hr.

  • Total Hours: 30 + 5 + 3 = 38 hours
  • Effective Rate: ($5,000 - $100) / 38 = $128.95/hr
  • Net Monthly Profit: $5,000 - (38 × $100) - $100 = $1,100
  • Profit Margin: $1,100 / $5,000 = 22%
  • Annual Value: $1,100 × 12 = $13,200
  • Verdict: Worth Keeping — effective rate exceeds target rate

Frequently Asked Questions

Divide the client's monthly revenue by total hours including billable, PM, revisions, and communication. This reveals the true hourly value of the relationship versus what you think you're charging.

Late payments create cash flow stress and require collection effort. A client paying 60 days late is materially less profitable than one on NET-15. Quantify this as collection time plus opportunity cost of delayed cash.

Consider ending a client relationship when their effective hourly rate is well below your target, they request frequent out-of-scope revisions, pay late consistently, or the overhead time outweighs their revenue contribution.

Multiply the client's net monthly profit contribution by 12. This helps prioritize retention and compare clients against each other to allocate your time and marketing energy effectively.
