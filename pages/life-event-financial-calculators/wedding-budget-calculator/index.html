Methodology & Cost Ranges

This calculator sums all entered wedding cost categories and divides by guest count to produce a per-guest figure — one of the most useful benchmarking metrics in wedding planning.

Total Wedding Cost
Total = Venue + Catering + Photography + Music + Flowers + Attire + Cake + Stationery + Rings + Honeymoon + Misc
Cost Per Guest
Per Guest = Total Wedding Cost / Number of Guests
Savings Timeline
Months = (Total − Current Savings) / Monthly Savings

National averages by category: Catering $75–$175/person; Venue $3,000–$12,000; Photography $2,500–$5,000; DJ $1,200–$2,500; Band $4,000–$12,000; Flowers $1,500–$5,000; Wedding dress $1,000–$4,000.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Start with your guest count — this is the single biggest lever on total cost.
  2. Enter vendor quotes — use real quotes from venues and vendors rather than estimates whenever possible.
  3. Check the per-guest cost — compare against your target budget per person to quickly spot overruns.
  4. Add honeymoon and rings — these are often forgotten in initial wedding budgets but add significantly to the total.
  5. Enter savings and monthly contribution — see your savings readiness date.

Example Scenario

Sarah and Tom are planning a 120-guest wedding in suburban Chicago. Their venue costs $6,000, catering and bar $14,400 ($120/person), photography $3,800, DJ $2,000, flowers $2,800, attire $3,200, cake $700, stationery $350, rings $2,000, honeymoon $5,000, and miscellaneous $1,500 — a total of $41,750. Per-guest cost comes to $348. They have $8,000 saved and save $1,200/month. With $33,750 still needed, they can reach their budget in about 28 months, targeting a summer wedding in just over two years.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average wedding in the United States costs between $25,000 and $35,000, with the median closer to $30,000. However, costs range widely — from under $10,000 for an intimate ceremony to $100,000+ for large formal receptions. Location is the biggest variable; weddings in New York or California average significantly more than in the Midwest or South.

Per-guest cost is calculated by dividing the total wedding budget by the number of guests. The national average is roughly $250–$350 per guest for a traditional wedding. Catering alone typically costs $75–$175 per person. When budgeting, add all fixed costs (venue, photography, DJ) and all variable per-guest costs (catering, favors, invitations) to get your total, then divide by guest count.

Industry guidelines suggest: Catering & bar 33–35%, Venue 15–20%, Photography & videography 10–12%, Music/entertainment 5–8%, Flowers & decor 8–10%, Attire 8–10%, Stationery 2–3%, Officiant 1–2%, Wedding rings 2–3%, Miscellaneous/contingency 5–8%. These are starting points — adjust based on your priorities.

Key strategies include: choosing an off-peak date (Friday, Sunday, or January–March), limiting the guest list (the single biggest cost driver), serving a buffet instead of plated dinner, hiring a DJ instead of a live band, using a university photography student for engagement photos, choosing in-season flowers, and skipping extras like wedding favors that guests rarely take home.
