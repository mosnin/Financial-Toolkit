Tax Comparison Formula

The calculator grows both account types at the same return, then applies taxes at withdrawal to determine after-tax wealth:

Traditional Account (Pre-Tax)
Future Balance = (Current Balance + Annual Contributions) grows at return% for N years After-Tax Value = Future Balance x (1 - Retirement Tax Rate) Tax paid is deferred — you get a deduction now but pay taxes on all withdrawals
Roth Account (After-Tax)
After-Tax Contribution = Annual Contribution x (1 - Current Tax Rate) Future Balance = after-tax contributions + balance grows at return% for N years After-Tax Value = Full future balance (withdrawals are tax-free)
Key Insight
If retirement tax rate = current tax rate, outcomes are mathematically identical. Roth wins if retirement rate > current rate. Traditional wins if retirement rate < current rate.

How to Use This Tax Impact Calculator

  1. Enter your annual contribution amount - the same amount will be modeled in both Traditional and Roth for a fair comparison.
  2. Enter your current account balance - used to project the starting point for both scenarios.
  3. Enter years until retirement and your expected annual return.
  4. Enter your current marginal tax rate - this is the rate on your last dollar of income (check your tax bracket).
  5. Enter your estimated retirement tax rate - consider your expected income sources in retirement.
  6. Click "Compare" to see which strategy gives you more after-tax wealth and by how much.

Example Scenario

An investor contributes $10,000/year on a $100,000 balance with 20 years to retirement at 7% return. At a current 24% tax rate and expected 20% retirement tax rate, the Traditional account grows to approximately $788,000 pre-tax, or $630,000 after taxes at withdrawal. The Roth account, funded with after-tax dollars ($7,600/year effective), grows to roughly $600,000 — tax-free. In this case, the Traditional wins by about $30,000, because the current tax rate is higher than the retirement rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Roth accounts win when your tax rate in retirement is higher than or equal to your current tax rate. This is common for young workers early in their careers, people who expect tax rates to rise, or those with substantial savings who will be in higher brackets from RMDs.

Traditional accounts win when your current tax rate is significantly higher than your expected retirement tax rate. This is common for peak-earning years in the 32-37% bracket expecting to drop to 22-24% in retirement. The upfront deduction is worth more than the tax-free growth.

A common approach is to diversify: contribute to both pre-tax and Roth accounts. This gives you tax flexibility in retirement. Most advisors recommend having both types available if eligible, allowing you to optimize withdrawals each year based on your actual income.

Roth IRAs do not have required minimum distributions (RMDs) during the owner's lifetime. Roth 401(k)s are also now RMD-exempt under SECURE 2.0. Traditional accounts require RMDs starting at age 73, which can push you into higher tax brackets unexpectedly.
