Inflation-Adjusted Salary Formula

Formulas Used
Nominal Raise % = ((Salary Year 2 − Salary Year 1) / Salary Year 1) × 100

Inflation-Adjusted Salary Year 1 = Salary Year 1 × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Real Salary Change % = ((Salary Year 2 − Adjusted Salary Year 1) / Adjusted Salary Year 1) × 100

Purchasing Power Change = Salary Year 2 − Inflation-Adjusted Salary Year 1

To compare salaries across years, the starting salary must be adjusted to today's dollars using the cumulative inflation over the period. The real salary change reveals the true change in buying power, while the purchasing power change shows the dollar difference in today's money.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Salary Year 1: Your starting salary in the earlier year.
  2. Enter Salary Year 2: Your current or more recent salary.
  3. Set the start and end years: The calculator uses these to determine the number of years for inflation compounding.
  4. Enter the average inflation rate: Use approximately 3% for a typical period, or look up the actual CPI average for your specific years at BLS.gov.
  5. Click "Analyze My Raise": The calculator will instantly tell you whether your raise was a real increase, kept pace, or fell behind inflation.

Example Scenario

You earned $55,000 in 2019 and now earn $65,000 in 2026 — a 7-year period with an average inflation rate of 4.5% (reflecting the higher inflation of the early 2020s):

  • Nominal raise: ($65,000 − $55,000) / $55,000 = +18.2%
  • Inflation-adjusted 2019 salary in 2026 dollars: $55,000 × (1.045)^7 = $75,468
  • Real salary change: ($65,000 − $75,468) / $75,468 = −13.9%
  • Verdict: Despite a nominal 18.2% raise, your purchasing power actually fell by 13.9% — a real pay cut.

This illustrates how high inflation periods can mask real wage stagnation behind seemingly positive nominal raises.

Frequently Asked Questions

A nominal salary change is the raw percentage difference between two salaries. A real salary change adjusts for inflation — it tells you whether your purchasing power actually increased. A nominal raise of 5% in a year with 6% inflation is actually a real pay cut of about 1%.

You got a true raise only if your salary grew faster than inflation over the same period. If your nominal raise percentage exceeds the cumulative inflation rate between the two years, your real purchasing power increased. If not, you effectively took a pay cut in real terms.

Real Salary Change = ((Salary Year 2 / Inflation Adjustment Factor) − Salary Year 1) / Salary Year 1 × 100. The inflation adjustment factor is (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years between the two salaries.

For historical salary comparisons, use the actual average CPI inflation for the period. The US historical long-term average is about 3% per year, but specific years may vary significantly. You can look up official CPI data at the Bureau of Labor Statistics website for exact figures.
