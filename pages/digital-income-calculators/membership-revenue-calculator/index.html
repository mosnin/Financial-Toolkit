Membership Revenue Formulas

Paying Members
Paying Members = Free Members x (Conversion Rate / 100)
MRR (blended)
MRR = (Monthly Members x Monthly Fee) + (Annual Members x Annual Price / 12)
Annual Plan Price
Annual Price = Monthly Fee x 12 x (1 - Annual Discount / 100)
Churn Impact
Monthly Churn Loss = MRR x (Churn Rate / 100)

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your total free members — the full size of your free community or subscriber list.
  2. Set your conversion rate to paid — the percentage who upgrade. Start with historical data or use 3-5% as an estimate.
  3. Enter your monthly membership fee for your standard monthly plan.
  4. Set the annual plan discount you offer for upfront annual payments (0% if no annual plan).
  5. Enter the annual plan adoption rate — what percentage of paying members choose the annual option.
  6. Set your monthly churn rate to see the monthly revenue loss from cancellations.

Example Scenario

An online community platform has 5,000 free members, 6% conversion to paid at $25/month, with a 20% annual discount and 35% of members on annual plans. Monthly churn is 3.5%.

  • Paying Members: 5,000 x 6% = 300 paying members
  • Monthly Members (65%): 195 x $25 = $4,875/month
  • Annual Price: $25 x 12 x (1 - 20%) = $240/year
  • Annual Members (35%): 105 x $240 / 12 = $2,100 MRR
  • Total MRR: $4,875 + $2,100 = $6,975
  • ARR: $6,975 x 12 = $83,700
  • Monthly Churn Cost: $6,975 x 3.5% = $244

Frequently Asked Questions

Typical free-to-paid conversion rates for online communities range from 2% to 10%. High-value niche communities with strong free content can achieve 5-15%. Consumer apps often see 2-5% conversion to paid tiers.

Annual plans provide upfront cash flow, reduce monthly churn, and improve retention. Members who pay annually churn at much lower rates than monthly members. Even with a 20-30% discount, annual plans are typically more profitable long-term due to better retention.

Churn compounds over time — a 5% monthly churn rate means losing roughly 46% of members per year. Improving churn from 5% to 3% can double the lifetime value of each member, making retention one of the highest-leverage improvements for membership businesses.

MRR is the normalized monthly income from all paying members. For annual plan members, their annual payment is divided by 12 to calculate their MRR contribution. ARR is MRR multiplied by 12. These are accounting metrics — actual cash received may differ from annual plans.
