Inflation-Adjusted Tuition Calculator

College tuition inflation has historically outpaced general inflation at roughly 5–7% per year, meaning today's tuition will look very different by the time your child enrolls. This calculator projects the per-year tuition cost at enrollment, the complete 4-year college cost estimate, and the monthly amount you need to save right now — invested at a reasonable return — to cover the full projected cost.

Tuition Projection Formula

Formulas Used
Years Until Enrollment = Enrollment Age − Child's Current Age

Tuition at Enrollment = Current Tuition × (1 + Tuition Inflation Rate)^Years Until Enrollment

Total 4-Year Cost = Σ Tuition at Enrollment × (1 + Rate)^(0, 1, 2, 3)

Monthly Savings = Total 4-Year Cost × (r/12) / ((1 + r/12)^(months) − 1)
where r = annual investment return rate

The monthly savings formula is the present value of an annuity — it calculates the level monthly contribution needed to reach a future savings target given a specified investment return. The earlier you start, the lower the monthly contribution due to more compound growth years.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter current annual tuition: Use the tuition for your target school type. National averages: public 4-year ~$11,000/yr, private 4-year ~$42,000/yr (tuition only — add room and board for total cost).
  2. Set tuition inflation rate: Historically 5–7% for US colleges. Use 6% as a conservative baseline.
  3. Enter your investment return: A 529 plan invested in index funds historically earns 6–8%. Use a lower rate for conservative planning.
  4. Enter child's current age and enrollment age: Most students start college at 18, but you can adjust for early or late enrollment.
  5. Review the monthly savings target: This is the amount to invest each month, starting today, in a 529 or education savings account.

Example Scenario

Your child is 5 years old. A private university currently costs $42,000/year. Tuition inflation is 6%/year. You invest savings at 7% return. Enrollment at age 18 means 13 years to save:

  • Tuition at year 1 of college: $42,000 × (1.06)^13 = $89,644/yr
  • Total 4-year projected cost: ~$402,000
  • Monthly savings needed (starting now): approximately $1,320/month in a 7% return account
  • If you wait until child is 10: Monthly savings jumps to about $2,500/month — nearly double!

This example shows the powerful effect of starting early: every year you delay college savings significantly increases the monthly contribution required.

Frequently Asked Questions

College tuition in the US has historically increased at 5–7% per year on average, significantly outpacing general inflation. Over the past 30 years, college costs have increased by over 200% in inflation-adjusted terms. Private universities tend to increase faster than public ones.

Projected Tuition = Current Annual Tuition × (1 + Tuition Inflation Rate)^Years Until Enrollment. The 4-year total accounts for continued tuition inflation during each of the 4 years of college, making the total cost higher than simply multiplying year-1 cost by 4.

The required monthly savings depends on your investment return rate and years until enrollment. If you invest in a 529 plan earning 6–7%, you need significantly less per month than if savings sit in cash. The earlier you start, the lower the monthly contribution required due to compound growth.

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged college savings account that allows your money to grow tax-free when used for qualified education expenses. Starting a 529 plan early and investing in growth assets allows compound returns to offset the impact of tuition inflation, potentially covering a large portion of future costs.
