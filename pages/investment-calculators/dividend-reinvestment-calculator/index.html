Dividend Reinvestment Formula

With reinvestment, the future value is calculated by compounding both the investment appreciation and reinvested dividends:

With Reinvestment
FV = P x (1 + Yield + Growth)^n

Without reinvestment, the portfolio grows only by the capital appreciation (dividend growth rate), and dividends are received as cash:

Without Reinvestment
FV = P x (1 + Growth)^n + cumulative dividends received

How to Use This Dividend Reinvestment Calculator

  1. Enter the initial investment - the amount you are investing today.
  2. Enter the annual dividend yield - the current dividend yield percentage.
  3. Enter the dividend growth rate - the expected annual increase in dividend payments.
  4. Enter the investment period - how many years you plan to hold the investment.
  5. Choose reinvestment option - compare both scenarios or select one.
  6. Click "Calculate Growth" to see projected future values.

Example Scenario

An investor puts $10,000 into a stock with a 3.5% dividend yield and 5% annual dividend growth over 20 years. With reinvestment, the portfolio could grow to approximately $46,610, versus $33,066 without reinvestment (assuming stock price grows with dividends). The reinvestment benefit would be approximately $13,544.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dividend reinvestment, or DRIP, is the automatic use of dividend payments to purchase additional shares of the same stock or fund, compounding your returns over time.

Reinvesting dividends can significantly increase long-term returns. Over 20-30 years, reinvested dividends can account for 40% or more of total portfolio growth due to compounding.

Yes, in taxable accounts, dividends are taxed in the year they are received regardless of whether they are reinvested. In tax-advantaged accounts like IRAs, dividends grow tax-deferred.

The dividend growth rate is the annualized percentage increase in a company's dividend payment over time. Companies with consistent dividend growth are often considered more stable investments.
