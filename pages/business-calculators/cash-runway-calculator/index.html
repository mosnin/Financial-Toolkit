How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current cash balance (total available cash and equivalents).
  2. Enter your monthly burn rate (total monthly operating expenses).
  3. Enter your current monthly revenue (set to 0 for pre-revenue startups).
  4. Optionally set a monthly revenue growth rate to model increasing revenue.
  5. Click Calculate Runway to see months of runway, projected zero-cash date, and break-even estimate.

Example Scenario

A startup has $500,000 in the bank, spends $50,000/month, earns $20,000/month in revenue, and is growing revenue at 10% month-over-month.

  • Current Net Burn: $50,000 - $20,000 = $30,000/month
  • Simple Runway (no growth): $500,000 / $30,000 = 16.7 months
  • With 10% revenue growth, revenue will exceed expenses around month 10 (break-even)
  • Actual runway with growth: significantly longer as net burn decreases monthly

Frequently Asked Questions

Cash runway is the number of months a company can continue operating before running out of cash, given its current cash balance and net monthly burn rate (expenses minus revenue).

Gross burn rate is total monthly operating expenses. Net burn rate is gross burn minus monthly revenue. Net burn = Monthly Expenses - Monthly Revenue.

Most investors and advisors recommend maintaining 12-18 months of runway. Less than 6 months is considered critical and should trigger immediate action to extend runway or raise funds.

Growing revenue reduces net burn rate over time, extending your runway. If revenue grows fast enough to exceed expenses, you reach break-even and theoretically have infinite runway.

You can extend runway by reducing expenses (cutting costs, renegotiating contracts), increasing revenue (raising prices, acquiring customers faster), or raising additional capital through investment or debt.
