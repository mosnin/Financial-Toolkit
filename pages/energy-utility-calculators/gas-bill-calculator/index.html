Gas Bill Formula

Cost Calculations
Monthly Therms = Monthly Usage (if CCF: × 1.02 to convert to therms) Monthly Bill = Monthly Therms × Rate per Therm Annual Bill = Monthly Bill × 12 Daily Cost = Monthly Bill / 30.44 Electric Equivalent (kWh) = Monthly Therms × 29.3 kWh/therm Electric Equivalent Cost = Electric kWh × $0.16/kWh (US avg rate) Gas Savings vs. Electric = Electric Equivalent Cost − Monthly Gas Bill

1 therm = 100,000 BTUs = approximately 29.3 kWh of equivalent electricity energy content. Note that electric resistance heaters convert electricity to heat at ~100% efficiency, while gas furnaces vary from 60% to 98% AFUE.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly gas usage -- find therms or CCF on your gas bill.
  2. Select usage unit -- choose therms or CCF based on what your utility bills.
  3. Enter gas rate per therm -- found on your bill, typically $0.80 to $2.00/therm.
  4. Select billing period -- how often you receive a bill.
  5. Click "Calculate Gas Bill" to see your costs and comparison to electric equivalent.

Example Scenario

Midwestern Home: 60 Therms/Month at $1.20/Therm

Usage: 60 therms/month | Rate: $1.20/therm | Billing: Monthly

Monthly Bill = 60 × $1.20 = $72.00

Annual Bill = $72 × 12 = $864/year

Electric Equivalent = 60 × 29.3 kWh = 1,758 kWh × $0.16 = $281.28/month

Gas Savings vs. Electric = $281.28 − $72 = $209/month ($2,508/year)

This shows natural gas heating is significantly cheaper than electric resistance heating in this scenario.

Frequently Asked Questions

A therm is a unit of heat energy equal to 100,000 BTUs. A CCF (hundred cubic feet) is a volume measurement of natural gas. One CCF contains approximately 1.02 therms of energy, so they are nearly interchangeable for billing purposes.

The average US household spends about $800-$1,000 per year on natural gas, roughly $65-$85 per month. This varies significantly by climate, home size, and heating system efficiency. Cold-weather states tend to have higher gas bills.

In most US markets, natural gas is cheaper per BTU of heat produced than resistance electric heating. However, modern heat pumps (electric) can be 2-3x more efficient than gas furnaces in mild climates, making them cost-competitive or cheaper.

The biggest opportunities are improving home insulation and sealing air leaks, upgrading to a high-efficiency furnace (95%+ AFUE), installing a programmable thermostat, lowering water heater temperature to 120°F, and using low-flow showerheads to reduce hot water demand.
