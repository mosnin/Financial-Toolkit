Markup Formulas

These are the core formulas used to compute markup and related metrics.

Markup %
Markup % = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Cost) x 100
Selling Price from Markup %
Selling Price = Cost x (1 + Markup % / 100)
Profit Margin %
Profit Margin = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Selling Price) x 100

Example Scenario

You purchase a product for $50 and sell it for $80.

  • Markup Amount: $80 - $50 = $30
  • Markup %: ($30 / $50) x 100 = 60%
  • Profit Margin: ($30 / $80) x 100 = 37.5%

Alternatively, if you want a 60% markup on a $50 item, the selling price would be $50 x 1.60 = $80.

Frequently Asked Questions

Markup is the amount added to the cost of a product to determine the selling price. It is expressed as a percentage of cost: Markup % = ((Selling Price - Cost) / Cost) x 100.

Selling Price = Cost x (1 + Markup % / 100). For example, if an item costs $50 and you want a 60% markup, the selling price is $50 x 1.60 = $80.

Markup varies widely by industry. Retail clothing often uses 50-100% markup, restaurants 200-300%, and electronics 5-20%. The right markup depends on your costs, competition, and target market.

Markup is based on cost, while margin is based on selling price. A 100% markup equals a 50% margin. Markup is always a larger number than margin for the same transaction.
