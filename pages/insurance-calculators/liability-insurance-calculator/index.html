Liability Insurance Premium Formula

Liability insurance premiums are based on revenue, industry risk, and desired coverage level:

Premium Estimation Formula
Annual Premium = Base Rate x (Revenue / $100K) x Industry Factor x Risk Multiplier

The base rate varies by industry. Revenue is used as a proxy for exposure. The industry factor reflects historical claim rates, and the risk multiplier adjusts for your specific risk profile.

How to Use This Calculator

Example Scenario

A professional services firm with $500,000 in annual revenue and moderate risk would need approximately $1,000,000 in liability coverage, costing around $750 per year. A construction company with the same revenue at high risk might need $2,000,000 in coverage at approximately $3,500 per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

General liability insurance covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, personal and advertising injury, and legal defense costs.

Most small businesses need at least $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate. Higher-risk businesses may need $5 million or more.

Small business general liability typically costs $400-$1,500 per year for low-risk businesses. Higher-risk industries may pay $2,000-$5,000 or more annually.

While not always legally required, many clients, landlords, and contracts require proof of coverage. Operating without it exposes your assets to lawsuits.
