How Approval Probability Is Estimated

This calculator uses a weighted scoring model based on the primary factors lenders evaluate:

Key Approval Factors
Credit Score (40%) + DTI Ratio (30%) + Income-to-Loan Ratio (20%) + Overall Profile (10%)

Each factor is scored and combined to produce an overall approval likelihood estimate. This is a simplified model; actual lender decisions involve additional criteria and proprietary underwriting algorithms.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your credit score - your current FICO or VantageScore.
  2. Enter your annual income - your total yearly earnings before taxes.
  3. Enter your DTI ratio - your debt-to-income percentage (use our DTI calculator if unsure).
  4. Enter the loan amount - how much you plan to borrow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most conventional lenders require a minimum score of 620-640. Scores above 740 qualify for the best rates. Some lenders offer loans for scores as low as 580, but with higher interest rates and stricter terms.

Most lenders prefer a DTI ratio below 36%, with the maximum typically being 43% for qualified mortgages. Some lenders may accept higher DTIs for borrowers with excellent credit scores or significant assets.

Each loan application results in a hard inquiry, which can lower your score by 5-10 points temporarily. However, multiple inquiries for the same type of loan within 14-45 days are typically counted as a single inquiry.

Improve your credit score by paying bills on time and reducing balances. Lower your DTI by paying off debts or increasing income. Consider a co-signer, provide collateral, or apply for a smaller loan amount.

Beyond credit score and DTI, lenders evaluate employment stability, income consistency, collateral value, existing relationship with the lender, loan purpose, and your overall financial reserves.
