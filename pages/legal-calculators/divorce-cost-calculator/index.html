Divorce Cost Formula

The total cost of divorce is the sum of several components:

Total Divorce Cost
Total = Attorney Fees + Filing Fees + Mediation + Other Costs

Attorney fees are calculated as hourly rate multiplied by estimated hours. Filing fees vary by state (typically $200-$400). Mediation adds $3,000-$8,000. Other costs include service fees, document preparation, and potential expert witnesses, estimated based on complexity.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select the complexity level - consider children, assets, businesses, and potential disputes.
  2. Choose contested or uncontested - uncontested divorces where both parties agree are much less expensive.
  3. Indicate whether mediation is involved - mediation can reduce costs compared to litigation.
  4. Estimate attorney hours - simple uncontested cases may need 5-15 hours, complex contested cases 50-200+ hours.
  5. Enter the attorney hourly rate - rates typically range from $150 to $500+ per hour depending on location and experience.
  6. Click "Calculate Cost" to see the estimated total divorce cost breakdown.

Example Scenario

A couple with moderate complexity (children, some assets) pursues a contested divorce with mediation. With an attorney charging $300/hour for an estimated 40 hours, the attorney fees would be $12,000. Filing fees are approximately $350, mediation costs around $5,000, and other costs around $2,000. The estimated total divorce cost would be approximately $19,350.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average cost of a divorce in the United States ranges from $7,000 to $15,000 for an uncontested divorce and $15,000 to $50,000 or more for a contested divorce. Costs vary significantly based on complexity, attorney fees, and whether the case goes to trial.

An uncontested divorce is one where both spouses agree on all major issues (property division, custody, support). A contested divorce involves disagreements that require negotiation, mediation, or trial to resolve. Uncontested divorces are significantly less expensive and faster.

Yes, mediation typically costs $3,000 to $8,000 total and can significantly reduce overall divorce costs by helping couples reach agreements without expensive litigation. Mediation is often faster and less adversarial than going to court.

Key cost drivers include: contested custody battles, complex asset division (businesses, investments, real estate), discovery and depositions, expert witnesses (forensic accountants, child psychologists), and going to trial rather than settling.

You can reduce costs by using mediation instead of litigation, agreeing on as many issues as possible before hiring attorneys, using a collaborative divorce process, being organized with financial documents, and avoiding using the legal process to settle emotional scores.
