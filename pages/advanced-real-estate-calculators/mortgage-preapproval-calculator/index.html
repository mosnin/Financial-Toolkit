Mortgage Preapproval Formula

Preapproval estimates are based on the debt-to-income (DTI) ratio and the standard mortgage payment formula:

Maximum Monthly Payment
Max Payment = (Gross Monthly Income x 0.43) - Monthly Debts

The maximum home price is then derived by calculating the loan amount that produces this payment at the estimated interest rate for a 30-year term, plus your down payment.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual gross income - your total yearly income before taxes.
  2. Enter your monthly debt payments - include car loans, student loans, credit card minimums, and other recurring debts.
  3. Select your credit score range - this determines your estimated interest rate.
  4. Enter your down payment - the cash you plan to put toward the purchase.
  5. Click "Calculate Preapproval" to see your estimated home buying budget.

Example Scenario

With an annual income of $85,000, monthly debts of $500, a credit score of 740 (very good), and a $50,000 down payment, you could be preapproved for approximately $340,000. Your estimated monthly payment would be around $1,550 at a 6.5% rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

A mortgage preapproval is an estimate from a lender of how much you can borrow based on your income, debts, credit score, and down payment. It gives you a realistic budget for home shopping.

Your credit score influences the interest rate you qualify for. Higher scores typically mean lower rates and higher preapproval amounts, while lower scores may result in higher rates and smaller loan amounts.

The debt-to-income (DTI) ratio compares your monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income. Most lenders prefer a DTI of 43% or less for mortgage qualification.
