Frequently Asked Questions

The S&P 500 has historically returned roughly 10% per year on average before inflation, or about 7% after inflation. A "good" return depends on your asset class, risk tolerance, and time horizon. Use our Annualized Return Calculator and CAGR Calculator to benchmark your own portfolio against market averages.

Even a seemingly small difference in expense ratios compounds dramatically over decades. A 1% higher annual fee on a $100,000 portfolio could cost you over $100,000 in lost growth over 30 years. Our Expense Ratio Impact Calculator shows the exact dollar cost of fund fees over your chosen time horizon.

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of market conditions. It reduces the risk of investing a lump sum at a market peak and removes the emotion from timing decisions. Our Dollar-Cost Averaging Calculator lets you model how this strategy performs across different market scenarios.

Most financial advisors recommend rebalancing annually or whenever your portfolio drifts more than 5% from its target allocation. Rebalancing too frequently can trigger unnecessary taxes and trading costs. Use our Asset Allocation Rebalancing Calculator to identify exactly what trades are needed to restore your target mix.

CAGR stands for Compound Annual Growth Rate. It is calculated as (Ending Value / Beginning Value) raised to the power of (1 / Number of Years) minus 1. CAGR smooths out year-to-year fluctuations to give a single consistent growth rate, making it ideal for comparing investments over different time periods.
