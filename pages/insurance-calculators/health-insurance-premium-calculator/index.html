Health Insurance Premium Formula

Health insurance premiums are estimated using a base rate adjusted by age, plan tier, and family size:

Premium Estimation Formula
Monthly Premium = Base Rate x Age Factor x Tier Multiplier x Family Size Adjustment

The base rate represents the average individual premium. The age factor reflects the ACA's 3:1 age rating ratio. The tier multiplier adjusts for the level of coverage, and the family adjustment scales for household size.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your age - the primary policyholder's age.
  2. Select a plan tier - Bronze, Silver, Gold, or Platinum.
  3. Enter family size - the total number of people on the plan.

Example Scenario

A 35-year-old individual choosing a Silver plan would pay approximately $450 per month. A family of four with the same primary member on a Silver plan might pay around $1,350 per month. Upgrading to Gold would increase costs by roughly 20%, while Bronze would save about 20%.

Frequently Asked Questions

ACA marketplace plans come in four tiers: Bronze (lowest premium, ~60% coverage), Silver (moderate, ~70%), Gold (higher premium, ~80%), and Platinum (highest premium, ~90% coverage).

Under the ACA, insurers can charge older adults up to 3 times more than younger adults. A 64-year-old may pay roughly 3x what a 21-year-old pays for the same plan.

Bronze plans suit healthy individuals who rarely need care. Silver offers a good balance and qualifies for cost-sharing reductions. Gold and Platinum are better for frequent medical needs.

Each family member adds to the total premium. Under the ACA, families pay for up to three children under 21; additional children are not charged extra.
