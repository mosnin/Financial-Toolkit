Education ROI Formula

This calculator uses standard investment return logic applied to the earnings premium from a degree:

Lifetime Earnings Gain
Gain = (Salary With Degree - Salary Without Degree) × Years in Career
Net Financial Benefit
Net Benefit = Lifetime Earnings Gain - Degree Cost
ROI %
ROI = (Net Financial Benefit / Degree Cost) × 100
Breakeven Years
Breakeven = Degree Cost / Annual Salary Premium

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter total degree cost - include all tuition, fees, books, and net costs after aid.
  2. Enter salary without degree - your current or expected salary with only a high school diploma or associate degree.
  3. Enter expected salary with degree - use Bureau of Labor Statistics data or job postings for your field.
  4. Enter career length - typically 30-40 years for most careers.
  5. Click Calculate to see lifetime gain, ROI, and breakeven point.

Example Scenario

A prospective nursing student is considering a 4-year BSN program costing $60,000 total. Without the degree, they could earn $38,000 per year as a medical assistant. With the BSN, they expect to earn $72,000. Over a 35-year nursing career, the lifetime earnings gain is $1,190,000. After subtracting the $60,000 degree cost, the net benefit is $1,130,000 — an ROI of 1,883%. The breakeven point is less than 2 years into the career.

Frequently Asked Questions

Education ROI is calculated by dividing the net financial benefit (lifetime earnings gain minus degree cost) by the degree cost, expressed as a percentage. A positive ROI means the degree pays for itself and more over your career.

Any positive ROI means the degree provides a financial benefit over your career. An ROI above 100% is generally considered strong. STEM, business, and healthcare degrees typically show the highest ROIs due to large salary premiums relative to degree costs.

Breakeven typically ranges from 5 to 15 years depending on the salary gap and degree cost. High-earning fields with lower tuition costs break even fastest. The breakeven point is where cumulative salary gains equal the total cost of the degree.

This calculator uses nominal figures for simplicity. For a more conservative estimate, you may want to use real salary figures and account for the opportunity cost of time spent in school vs. working full-time.
