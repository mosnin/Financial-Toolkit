Purchasing Power Formula

Formulas Used
Purchasing Power (Future) = Today's Amount ÷ (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Future Value Needed = Today's Amount × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Years = Target Year − Current Year (2026)

The purchasing power formula discounts today's amount by inflation to show you its real value in the future. The future value needed is the inverse — how many future dollars you will need to match today's buying power. These two formulas together give you the complete picture of inflation's impact on your money.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter today's dollar amount: This is the amount in today's dollars you want to evaluate — could be savings, a salary target, or a planned expense.
  2. Set the target year: Choose the year you want to project to. The calculator computes the number of years automatically.
  3. Choose an inflation assumption: Select 2% (Fed target), 3% (historical average), or enter a custom rate if you have a specific scenario in mind.
  4. Review the table: The year-by-year table shows how purchasing power erodes each year, helping you spot key milestones in your financial timeline.

Example Scenario

You have $50,000 in savings today (2026) and want to know its real value when you plan to retire in 2046 — 20 years from now — at 3% inflation:

  • Future value needed to match today's $50,000: $50,000 × (1.03)^20 = $90,306
  • Purchasing power of $50,000 in 2046: $50,000 ÷ (1.03)^20 = $27,684
  • What this means: Your $50,000 today will only feel like $27,684 in 2046. To have the same buying power, you will need over $90,000.

This dramatic difference underscores why investing savings to outpace inflation is critical for retirement planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Purchasing power is the quantity of goods or services that one unit of currency can buy. Inflation reduces purchasing power over time — at 3% annual inflation, $1,000 today will only have the purchasing power of about $744 in 10 years.

Future Purchasing Power = Today's Amount / (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years. The equivalent future amount you need is Today's Amount × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years. Both formulas are used in this calculator to give you the complete picture.

Use 2% for the Federal Reserve's target scenario (optimistic), 3% for the US historical long-run average (moderate), or a custom rate if you expect higher inflation based on current economic conditions or specific spending categories.

The year-by-year table shows the compounding effect of inflation clearly — small annual losses accumulate significantly over decades. This helps you plan ahead for retirement, education savings, and long-term investment goals by understanding the real value of money at each future point.
