Calculation Methodology

Annual Subscription Cost
Annual = Cost per User/mo × 12 × Seats × (1 − Annual Discount if applicable) + Support Cost
Perpetual License Annual Cost (Year 1)
Year 1 = Cost per User × Seats + Support Cost
3-Year TCO (Subscription)
3-Year TCO = Annual Cost × 3
3-Year TCO (Perpetual)
3-Year TCO = License Cost + (Support Cost × 3)
Cost per User per Day
Daily Cost = (Annual Cost / Seats) / 365

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your software type and enter the number of seats or users.
  2. Choose subscription or perpetual licensing and enter the appropriate cost.
  3. For subscription, select billing cycle and the annual discount offered by the vendor.
  4. Choose a support tier to add ongoing maintenance costs.
  5. Click Calculate Licensing Cost to see annual total, 3-year TCO, and per-user daily cost.

Example Scenario

A company evaluating Microsoft 365 Business Standard for 50 employees:

  • License type: Subscription, $12.50/user/month
  • Seats: 50
  • Annual billing (20% discount): $12.50 × 50 × 12 × 0.80 = $6,000/year
  • Standard support (20%): $6,000 × 0.20 = $1,200/year
  • Total annual cost: $7,200
  • 3-Year TCO: $21,600
  • Cost per user per day: $7,200 / 50 / 365 = $0.39/user/day

Frequently Asked Questions

A perpetual license is a one-time purchase granting permanent use of a specific version. Subscription licensing is paid annually or monthly and includes ongoing updates. Perpetual licenses require separate maintenance contracts (15-25% of license cost) to receive updates. Subscriptions are typically cheaper initially but more expensive long-term.

TCO includes all costs over a defined period: license fees, implementation, training, support and maintenance, integration, and staff time. A 3-year TCO comparison between perpetual and subscription models often shows perpetual is cheaper long-term despite a higher upfront cost.

Basic support (email-only) typically costs 10-15% of license value annually. Standard support (phone + email) runs 18-22%. Premium enterprise support (24/7, dedicated account) costs 25-30% of license value per year. Always include support in your TCO calculation.

Divide the annual per-user cost by 365. For example, $200/user/year equals $0.55/user/day. This metric helps frame software costs in approachable terms for stakeholder buy-in — often less than a cup of coffee per day per employee.
