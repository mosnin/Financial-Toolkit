Heating Efficiency Formula

Efficiency Calculations
Annual Heating Cost = Monthly Bill × 6 (avg heating months) Cost per Sq Ft = Annual Heating Cost / Home Size Thermostat Savings (2°F) = Monthly Bill × 2% (DOE: ~1% per 1°F) Insulation Upgrade Savings varies by current quality: Poor to Average: ~20% reduction Average to Good: ~15% reduction Good to Excellent: ~8% reduction Efficiency Grade benchmarks (cost/sq ft/year): A: under $0.80 | B: $0.80-1.20 C: $1.20-1.80 | D: $1.80-2.50 | F: over $2.50

Heating months vary by climate. The calculator uses 6 months as an average US assumption. Adjust by multiplying your actual heating season months / 6 to refine annual estimates for your climate.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your home size in square feet -- heated living area only.
  2. Select your heating system type -- each has different efficiency characteristics.
  3. Enter your average winter monthly bill -- use bills from December-February for best accuracy.
  4. Enter your current thermostat setting and insulation quality.
  5. Click "Analyze Heating Efficiency" to get your grade and savings opportunities.

Example Scenario

2,000 Sq Ft Home with Gas Heat, Average Insulation

Home: 2,000 sq ft | System: Gas furnace | Monthly bill: $180 | Thermostat: 70°F | Insulation: Average

Annual heating cost: $180 × 6 = $1,080/year

Cost per sq ft: $1,080 / 2,000 = $0.54/sq ft -- Grade A

2°F thermostat reduction: $180 × 2% = $3.60/month savings

Insulation upgrade (average to good): $1,080 × 15% = $162/year savings

Total annual improvement potential: ~$205/year

Frequently Asked Questions

The DOE estimates savings of about 1% per 1°F reduction over 8 hours. Lowering by 7-10°F while sleeping or away can save up to 10% annually. A continuous 2°F reduction saves approximately 2-3% on heating costs.

In moderate climates, an efficient home should spend $0.50-1.00 per square foot per year on heating. Cold climate homes typically spend $1.00-2.00/sq ft. Costs above $2.00/sq ft suggest poor insulation or an inefficient system needing attention.

Modern heat pumps are 200-400% efficient (moving 2-4 units of heat per unit of electricity). Gas furnaces are 80-98% efficient. Heat pumps are generally more cost-effective in mild climates. In very cold climates, high-efficiency gas may still be more economical depending on local fuel prices.

Poor insulation can increase heating costs 20-40% versus a well-insulated home. Air leaks and inadequate attic insulation are the biggest culprits. Upgrading attic insulation from R-19 to R-49 can reduce heating energy use by 15-25% in cold climates.
