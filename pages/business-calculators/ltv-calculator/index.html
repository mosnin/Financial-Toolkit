LTV Formulas

Customer Lifetime Value
LTV = Avg Purchase Value x Frequency/Year x Lifespan (Years) x Profit Margin %
Annual Customer Revenue
Annual Revenue = Avg Purchase Value x Purchase Frequency Per Year
LTV:CAC Ratio
LTV:CAC Ratio = Customer Lifetime Value / Customer Acquisition Cost

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the average purchase value -- how much a customer spends per transaction on average.
  2. Enter the purchase frequency -- how many times a customer buys from you per year.
  3. Enter the customer lifespan -- the average number of years a customer remains active.
  4. Enter your profit margin -- the percentage of revenue that is profit (gross or net, depending on your preference).
  5. Optionally enter your CAC -- to calculate the LTV:CAC ratio and evaluate acquisition efficiency.
  6. Click "Calculate LTV" to see the full breakdown.

Example Scenario

An e-commerce business has these averages: customers spend $50 per order, purchase 12 times per year, stay active for 3 years, and the profit margin is 30%. CAC is $400.

  • Annual Revenue Per Customer: $50 x 12 = $600
  • Lifetime Revenue: $600 x 3 = $1,800
  • LTV (Profit): $1,800 x 0.30 = $540
  • LTV:CAC Ratio: $540 / $400 = 1.35:1

A ratio of 1.35:1 is below the 3:1 benchmark, suggesting this business needs to either reduce CAC, increase purchase frequency, extend customer lifespan, or improve margins to be sustainable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV or CLV) is the total revenue or profit a business can expect from a single customer account over the entire duration of the relationship. It helps businesses understand how much they can afford to spend to acquire customers.

A common formula is: LTV = Average Purchase Value x Purchase Frequency per Year x Customer Lifespan in Years x Profit Margin %. This gives the total profit expected from one customer over their lifetime.

A ratio of 3:1 is generally considered healthy, meaning you earn $3 for every $1 spent on acquisition. A ratio below 1:1 means you are losing money on each customer. A ratio above 5:1 may indicate you are under-investing in growth.

LTV helps determine how much to invest in customer acquisition, informs marketing budget allocation, guides customer retention strategies, and is a key metric for investors evaluating a business.

Increase purchase frequency through engagement and loyalty programs, raise average order value through upselling and cross-selling, extend customer lifespan through excellent support and retention efforts, and improve profit margins.
