Emergency Fund Formula

Formulas
Target Amount = Monthly Expenses x Months of Coverage
Gap = Target Amount - Current Savings
Months to Goal = Gap / Monthly Contribution

The emergency fund target is calculated by multiplying your essential monthly expenses by the number of months you want coverage for. The gap tells you how much more you need to save, and the timeline shows when you will reach your goal at your current contribution rate.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter monthly expenses: Include only essential expenses such as housing, food, utilities, insurance, and transportation.
  2. Choose months of coverage: Most experts recommend 3-6 months. If you are self-employed or have variable income, consider 6-12 months.
  3. Enter current savings: How much you already have saved specifically for emergencies.
  4. Enter monthly contribution: How much you can realistically set aside each month toward your emergency fund.
  5. Click "Calculate": See your target, the gap, and how many months it will take to reach your goal.

Example Scenario

Jordan has monthly essential expenses of $3,500 and wants 6 months of coverage. Currently, Jordan has $5,000 saved and can contribute $600 per month.

  • Target: $3,500 x 6 = $21,000
  • Gap: $21,000 - $5,000 = $16,000
  • Months to reach goal: $16,000 / $600 = approximately 27 months (about 2 years and 3 months)

Frequently Asked Questions

Most financial experts recommend saving 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses. If you have variable income or are self-employed, aim for 6 to 12 months.

Keep your emergency fund in a high-yield savings account or money market account. These provide easy access while earning some interest. Avoid investing emergency funds in volatile assets.

Emergencies include unexpected job loss, major medical expenses, urgent car or home repairs, and other unforeseen essential expenses. Regular bills and planned purchases are not emergencies.

A common approach is to save a starter emergency fund (such as $1,000), then focus on paying off high-interest debt, and finally build your full emergency fund.

Automate your savings with recurring transfers. Redirect windfalls like tax refunds or bonuses. Cut discretionary spending temporarily and consider a side income stream.
