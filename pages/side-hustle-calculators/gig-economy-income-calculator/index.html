Gig Income Formulas

Several layers of costs reduce your gross platform earnings to your true take-home pay:

Mileage Deduction
Miles Driven x IRS Standard Mileage Rate (e.g., $0.70/mile)
Total Expenses
Mileage Deduction + Other Business Expenses
Net Self-Employment Income
Gross Earnings - Total Expenses
Self-Employment Tax
Net SE Income x 92.35% x 15.3% (both employer and employee share)
Net Take-Home Income
Net SE Income - SE Tax - Income Tax Estimate
Effective Hourly Rate
Net Take-Home Income / Hours Worked

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter gross earnings — the total payout from your platform dashboard, including tips and bonuses.
  2. Enter miles driven — track every business mile, including deadhead miles driving to a pickup or delivery zone.
  3. Set the mileage rate — the IRS standard rate for 2026 is $0.70/mile; you can also use your actual cost-per-mile if you track it.
  4. Add other expenses — phone plan allocation, insulated bags, parking fees, tolls, and any other direct costs.
  5. Enter hours worked — include all time on the app, including waiting for orders.
  6. Enter your tax rate — use your marginal federal rate (10%, 12%, 22%, etc.). State taxes are not included.
  7. Click Calculate to see your true take-home and effective hourly rate.

Example Scenario

Marcus drives for DoorDash and Uber Eats on weekends. In one month he earned $1,200 gross, drove 900 miles, had $25 in other expenses, worked 45 hours, and is in the 12% federal bracket.

  • Mileage deduction: 900 x $0.70 = $630.00
  • Total expenses: $630 + $25 = $655.00
  • Net SE income: $1,200 - $655 = $545.00
  • SE tax: $545 x 92.35% x 15.3% = $77.02
  • Income tax estimate: ($545 - $38.51 SE deduction) x 12% = $60.78
  • Net take-home: $545 - $77.02 - $60.78 = $407.20
  • Effective hourly rate: $407.20 / 45 = $9.05/hr

This reveals that Marcus is effectively earning $9.05/hr — well below the gross earnings imply, making the case for tracking all costs carefully.
