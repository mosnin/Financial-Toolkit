Avalanche Payoff Order

How the Debt Avalanche Works

Strategy
1. Make minimum payments on ALL debts every month.
2. Apply all extra money to the debt with the HIGHEST interest rate.
3. When that debt is paid off, roll its payment into the next highest-rate debt.
4. Repeat until all debts are gone.

This method is optimal because the highest-rate debt grows the fastest. By eliminating it first you stop the most expensive interest compounding as early as possible, reducing total interest paid across all debts.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter each debt: Provide a name, current balance, annual interest rate, and minimum monthly payment for up to five debts.
  2. Add your extra payment: Enter any additional amount you can pay above the total of all minimum payments.
  3. Click "Calculate Payoff Plan": The calculator sorts debts by interest rate and simulates the month-by-month payoff.
  4. Review the payoff order: See each debt's payoff date and total interest paid under the avalanche strategy.
  5. Compare savings: The interest saved and months saved figures show the benefit over making minimums only.

Example Scenario

Taylor has three debts: a credit card ($4,500 at 22.99%), a personal loan ($8,000 at 11.5%), and a car loan ($12,000 at 6.9%). Total minimums are $460/month, and Taylor can add an extra $300/month. Under the avalanche method, the credit card is targeted first. By eliminating the 22.99% card first, Taylor saves approximately $1,800 in interest and becomes debt-free 14 months sooner compared to paying minimums only.

Frequently Asked Questions

The debt avalanche method prioritizes paying off debts from highest to lowest interest rate while making minimum payments on all others. It is the mathematically optimal strategy to minimize total interest paid.

The avalanche saves the most money; the snowball (smallest balance first) gives faster wins. Research shows both work — the best method is whichever keeps you motivated to stay on track.

Most experts focus the avalanche on high-interest consumer debt like credit cards and personal loans. Mortgages usually carry lower rates and may offer tax benefits, so they are often handled separately.

When a debt is eliminated, its full payment is rolled into the next target debt. This ever-growing payment accelerates the payoff timeline with each debt you clear.
