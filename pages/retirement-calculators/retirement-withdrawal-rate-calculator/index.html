Withdrawal Rate Analysis Formula

The calculator simulates year-by-year portfolio drawdown with inflation-adjusted spending:

Your Withdrawal Rate
Withdrawal Rate = Annual Spending / Portfolio Value x 100%
Annual Portfolio Simulation
Balance(n+1) = Balance(n) x (1 + return) - Annual Spending x (1 + inflation)^n Simulated until balance reaches zero or 50 years, whichever comes first
Safe Withdrawal Benchmarks
4% Rule: Portfolio x 0.04 = maximum annual withdrawal for ~30-year retirement 3.5% Rule: Portfolio x 0.035 = more conservative for 35-40 year retirements

How to Use This Withdrawal Rate Calculator

  1. Enter your portfolio value - the total investable assets you are drawing from in retirement.
  2. Enter annual spending from portfolio - your planned yearly withdrawals (exclude Social Security or pension income).
  3. Enter expected portfolio return - a conservative 6-7% is typical for a balanced retirement portfolio.
  4. Enter expected inflation - 3% is a standard assumption; your spending will increase each year to keep pace.
  5. Click "Analyze Withdrawal Rate" to see your rate, longevity estimate, and benchmark comparisons.

Example Scenario

A retiree with a $1.2 million portfolio plans to spend $54,000/year (4.5% withdrawal rate). With a 7% expected return and 3% inflation, the simulation shows the portfolio lasting approximately 34 years. The 4% rule recommends no more than $48,000/year for indefinite sustainability. At $54,000/year the withdrawal is slightly above the 4% benchmark, but the projection still shows strong longevity thanks to favorable return assumptions. Spending $6,000 less per year (or $500/month) would bring this within the safe threshold.

Frequently Asked Questions

The 4% rule comes from the Trinity Study (1994) which found that a balanced portfolio could sustain a 4% initial withdrawal rate (adjusted for inflation annually) for at least 30 years in nearly all historical market scenarios. It is a useful starting point but not a guarantee — actual outcomes depend on market returns and timing.

The 4% rule remains a widely used benchmark. Some researchers suggest a more conservative 3-3.5% given current conditions. For longer retirements (35-40 years), 3.5% is considered more cautious. It is best used as a starting point for planning, not a precise rule.

For a 30-year retirement, research suggests 3.5–4% is historically safe for a diversified portfolio. For a 40-year retirement, 3–3.5% is more appropriate. A variable withdrawal strategy (adjusting spending in down markets) can also extend portfolio longevity.

The 4% rule assumes annual inflation adjustments to maintain purchasing power. Without adjustments, a fixed withdrawal is easier on the portfolio but erodes real spending power. At 3% inflation, $5,000/month in year 1 needs to be $9,031/month by year 20 to maintain the same purchasing power.
