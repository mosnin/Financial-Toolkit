How the College Affordability Formula Works

This calculator determines the gap between what college costs and what your family can cover each year:

Annual Funding Gap
Gap = (Tuition + Room & Board) - EFC - (Savings / 4)

The total 4-year cost is simply the annual total cost multiplied by four. The loan amount needed equals the annual gap multiplied by four minus any remaining savings. The estimated monthly payment is calculated using the standard 10-year loan amortization formula at a 6.5% federal loan rate:

Monthly Payment (Standard 10-Year Repayment)
M = P * [r(1+r)^120] / [(1+r)^120 - 1]

Where P is the total loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (6.5% / 12), and 120 is the number of payments over 10 years.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual tuition and fees - check the college's published Cost of Attendance (COA).
  2. Enter room and board - use on-campus costs or estimate off-campus housing + food.
  3. Enter annual household income - combined gross income before taxes.
  4. Enter your EFC/SAI - found on your FAFSA Student Aid Report or estimated via studentaid.gov.
  5. Enter college savings - total 529 or other savings earmarked for college.
  6. Click Calculate to see your funding gap and projected loan burden.

Example Scenario

A family is considering a private university with $35,000 in annual tuition and $13,000 in room and board. Their EFC is $9,000 and they have $20,000 in a 529 account. The annual gap works out to $34,000. Over four years, total costs reach $192,000, and with savings applied, the loan amount is roughly $116,000. At 6.5% over 10 years, the estimated monthly payment after graduation is about $1,310 — a powerful figure for evaluating whether this school is affordable.

Frequently Asked Questions

The EFC (now called the Student Aid Index or SAI) is a number calculated from your FAFSA that colleges use to determine your financial aid eligibility. It represents how much your family is expected to contribute toward education costs each year. A lower EFC means more aid eligibility.

The funding gap is the difference between total annual college costs and what your family can cover through savings and your Expected Family Contribution. This gap typically must be filled with student loans or additional scholarships.

Federal undergraduate Direct Loans carry a fixed rate set annually by Congress, around 6.5% in recent years. Private loan rates vary widely based on credit. This calculator uses 6.5% as a reasonable baseline estimate for planning purposes.

The standard federal repayment plan spans 10 years (120 monthly payments). Income-driven repayment plans can extend this to 20-25 years with lower monthly payments but more total interest paid over the life of the loan.
