Can Your Business Afford to Hire?

Hiring too early is one of the most common ways small businesses damage their cash flow. Hiring too late costs you growth. This 8-question quiz evaluates your revenue base, profit margins, cash reserves, and growth trajectory to give you a clear hiring readiness verdict.

Frequently Asked Questions

A common rule of thumb is that your annual revenue should be at least 3–5x the cost of the hire (including salary, benefits, payroll taxes, and equipment). So for a $60,000 salary, you'd want at least $180,000–$300,000 in annual revenue, and the role should either generate or free up significant additional revenue.

Revenue per employee varies significantly by industry. Service businesses typically target $100,000–$200,000 per employee. Software companies often exceed $400,000 per employee. Retail and restaurants run lower, around $80,000–$120,000. Before hiring, model what your revenue per employee will be after the addition.

Ideally, you should have at least 3–6 months of the new hire's total compensation cost in cash reserves above your normal operating buffer. New hires often take 3–6 months to reach full productivity, so you need runway to cover their cost during the ramp-up period.
