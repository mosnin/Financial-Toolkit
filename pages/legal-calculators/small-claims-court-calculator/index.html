Small Claims Cost Formula

The total cost and potential recovery for a small claims case is straightforward:

Cost & Recovery Formula
Total Cost = Filing Fee + Service Costs
Net Recovery = Claim Amount - Total Cost
Recovery Ratio = Net Recovery / Total Cost

The total cost is what you pay out of pocket to file the case. Net recovery is what you would take home if you win the full claim amount (minus your filing costs, though these can often be included in the judgment). The recovery ratio helps you determine whether filing is worthwhile.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your claim amount - the total dollar amount you are seeking from the defendant.
  2. Enter the filing fee - check your county clerk's office for the exact amount, or use our estimated default.
  3. Enter service of process costs - the cost to have the defendant officially notified of the lawsuit.
  4. Select your state - to check that your claim is within the small claims limit.
  5. Click "Calculate Costs" to see the total cost to file and your potential net recovery.

Example Scenario

You are owed $3,000 from a contractor who did not complete work. The filing fee is $75 and service costs are $50, making your total cost to file $125. If you win the full amount plus costs, your net recovery would be $3,000 (since costs are typically recoverable). The recovery ratio of 24:1 makes this a strong candidate for small claims court.

Frequently Asked Questions

Filing fees for small claims court typically range from $30 to $200 depending on the state and the amount of the claim. Higher claim amounts generally have higher filing fees. Additional costs include service of process ($20-$100).

Small claims court limits vary by state, ranging from $2,500 to $25,000. Common limits include $5,000 (many states), $10,000 (California for most claims, New York), and $20,000 (Texas). Check your state's specific limit.

In most states, you do not need a lawyer for small claims court and some states actually prohibit attorneys from appearing. Small claims court is designed for individuals to represent themselves.

From filing to hearing, small claims cases typically take 30 to 70 days. The hearing itself usually lasts 15 minutes to an hour. Collecting the judgment after winning can take additional time.

Yes, in most jurisdictions, the winner can include filing fees and service costs as part of the judgment amount. However, winning a judgment and actually collecting the money are two different things.
