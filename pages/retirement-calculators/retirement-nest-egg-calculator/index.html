Nest Egg Calculation Formula

The required nest egg is calculated using the present value of an inflation-adjusted annuity:

Real (Inflation-Adjusted) Return
Real Rate = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) - 1
Required Nest Egg (Present Value of Annuity)
Nest Egg = Annual Income x [1 - (1 + real rate)^(-years)] / real rate
Monthly Savings to Reach Goal
PMT = (Nest Egg - Current Savings x FV factor) x (r/12) / ((1 + r/12)^n - 1)

Years to reach goal is solved iteratively by projecting portfolio growth month by month until the target is reached.

How to Use This Nest Egg Calculator

  1. Enter your desired monthly retirement income - the amount you want to spend each month.
  2. Enter years in retirement - use your longevity estimate or a conservative 25-30 years.
  3. Enter expected portfolio return - 5-7% is a common conservative estimate.
  4. Enter expected inflation - 3% is a standard planning assumption.
  5. Enter your current savings and monthly contribution to see years to goal.
  6. Click "Calculate Nest Egg" to see your savings target and path.

Example Scenario

A 40-year-old wants $5,000/month in retirement for 25 years, with a 7% expected return and 3% inflation. The real return is approximately 3.9%. The required nest egg is about $980,000. With $75,000 already saved and contributing $1,200/month at 7% return, they will reach their goal in approximately 22 years — right on schedule for a retirement at 62.

Frequently Asked Questions

The most common method is the 4% rule: multiply your desired annual retirement income by 25. If you want $60,000/year, you need $1.5 million. This calculator uses a more precise present value formula based on your specific return and inflation assumptions.

Historical average returns: S&P 500 ~10% before inflation, ~7% after. A conservative balanced portfolio (60/40) has returned around 6–7%/year. Most retirement planners suggest using 5–7% as a conservative estimate to avoid over-optimism.

The long-term average US inflation rate is about 3%. For retirement planning, 2.5–3% is a reasonable assumption for general expenses. Healthcare costs inflate faster at 5–6%/year. Using 3% builds a conservative safety buffer into your plan.

Subtract your expected annual Social Security benefit from your desired annual income before entering the monthly income figure. Your portfolio only needs to generate income above and beyond Social Security. Check ssa.gov for your personalized benefit estimate.
