Debt Payoff Formula

The number of months to pay off a debt is calculated using the following formula:

Debt Payoff Formula
n = -ln(1 - (B x r / P)) / ln(1 + r)

Where n is the number of months, B is the current balance, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12), and P is the monthly payment. This formula requires that P is greater than B x r (the monthly interest charge).

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your total debt balance - the current amount you owe.

  3. Enter your monthly payment - the amount you pay each month.

Example Scenario

Suppose you have $15,000 in debt at 18% APR and pay $500 per month. You would be debt-free in approximately 39 months (about 3 years and 3 months), paying $4,311 in total interest for a total outlay of $19,311.

Frequently Asked Questions

The payoff time uses the formula n = -ln(1 - (B x r / P)) / ln(1 + r), where B is the balance, r is the monthly interest rate, and P is the monthly payment. Your payment must exceed the monthly interest charge for the debt to be payable.

If your monthly payment equals or is less than the monthly interest charge, you will never pay off the debt. The balance will stay the same or grow. You need to pay more than the interest portion to reduce the principal.

The avalanche method (paying highest-interest debt first) saves the most money on interest. The snowball method (paying smallest balance first) provides psychological wins. Both work - choose the approach that keeps you motivated.

Even an extra $50-100 per month can dramatically reduce your payoff time and total interest. Use this calculator to compare different payment amounts and see how much time and money you save with higher payments.

This calculator works for any fixed-rate debt with regular monthly payments, including personal loans, auto loans, student loans, and credit cards. It assumes a constant interest rate and consistent monthly payments.
