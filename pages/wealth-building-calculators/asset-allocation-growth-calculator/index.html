Asset Allocation Growth Calculator

Your asset allocation — the mix of stocks, bonds, and cash in your portfolio — is the single most important driver of long-term investment outcomes. This calculator projects how your portfolio grows under your chosen allocation, compares it against three benchmark allocations (aggressive, balanced, and conservative), and provides a risk-adjusted return analysis to help you find the right balance for your goals and timeline.

Asset Allocation Formula

The blended portfolio return is a weighted average of each asset class return, then applied using the compound growth formula:

Blended Return
rblend = (wstocks × rstocks) + (wbonds × rbonds) + (wcash × rcash)
Projected Portfolio Value
FV = P × (1 + rblend)t
Approximate Portfolio Volatility (Risk Score)
σportfolio ≈ wstocks × σstocks + wbonds × σbonds

Where w represents the allocation weight (0–1), r is the assumed return for each asset class, and P is the starting portfolio value. Volatility (risk score) uses approximate standard deviations of 18% for stocks, 7% for bonds, and 1% for cash.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your portfolio size — your current total investable portfolio value.
  2. Enter your allocation percentages — stocks, bonds, and cash must sum to 100%.
  3. Adjust return assumptions — or leave the defaults (10% stocks, 4% bonds, 2% cash) based on historical averages.
  4. Enter the number of years — your investment horizon.
  5. Click "Compare Allocations" to see your projected value alongside aggressive, balanced, and conservative benchmarks.

Example Scenario

A $100,000 portfolio with a 60/30/10 stock/bond/cash allocation and default return assumptions (10%/4%/2%) has a blended annual return of 7.4%. Over 20 years, it grows to approximately $424,000. An aggressive 100% stock portfolio would grow to $673,000, while a conservative 20/60/20 allocation would reach only $209,000. The gap between aggressive and conservative grows dramatically over time, underscoring why asset allocation decisions matter more as your time horizon lengthens.

Frequently Asked Questions

For long-term growth (20+ year horizons), higher stock allocations (80–100%) historically produce better returns. Common guidance like the "110 minus age" rule suggests holding that percentage in stocks. Younger investors with more time can afford to take on more equity risk for higher expected returns.

Historically, US large-cap stocks (S&P 500) have returned approximately 10% annually before inflation and 7% after inflation. US bonds have returned approximately 4–5% annually. Cash equivalents typically return 1–3%. These are the default assumptions in this calculator, which you can adjust.

Most financial planners recommend rebalancing annually or when any asset class drifts more than 5% from its target allocation. Rebalancing forces you to sell high and buy low, maintaining your intended risk profile and preventing any single asset class from dominating your portfolio.

A risk-adjusted return measures how much return an investment generates per unit of risk. A portfolio with a 10% return and high volatility may be less attractive than one with an 8% return and low volatility. The Sharpe ratio and similar metrics quantify this trade-off, helping investors choose allocations appropriate for their risk tolerance.
