Lease vs Buy Comparison Formula

This calculator compares costs over the same time period (the lease term):

Total Lease Cost
Total Lease Cost = Monthly Lease Payment x Lease Term (months)
Net Buy Cost
Net Buy Cost = Total Loan Payments (over lease term) - Vehicle Equity at End

The equity difference shows how much better off you would be financially by choosing one option over the other after the lease term ends.

How to Use This Lease vs Buy Calculator

  1. Enter the vehicle purchase price - the price if you were to buy the vehicle.
  2. Enter the monthly lease payment - the quoted lease payment from the dealer.
  3. Enter the lease term - the length of the lease in months.
  4. Enter the residual value - the vehicle's estimated value at the end of the lease term.
  5. Enter the buy loan interest rate - the rate you would get for a purchase loan.
  6. Click "Compare Costs" to see the total costs side by side.

Example Scenario

Consider a $40,000 vehicle with a $450/month lease for 36 months and a residual value of $22,000. If you bought the same car with a 6% loan for 36 months, your monthly payment would be about $1,217, totaling $43,812. However, you would own a vehicle worth $22,000, making the net buy cost $21,812. The total lease cost would be $16,200, but you would have zero equity at the end.

Frequently Asked Questions

Leasing typically has lower monthly payments, but buying builds equity. Over the long term, buying is usually cheaper because you own the vehicle outright after paying off the loan. Leasing is better if you prefer a new car every few years and want lower upfront costs.

Residual value is the estimated value of the vehicle at the end of the lease term. It determines your lease payments - the higher the residual, the lower your monthly payments. It also represents the price you would pay to buy the vehicle at lease end.

The lease total includes all monthly lease payments over the lease term. The buy total includes all loan payments minus the vehicle's residual value (equity). The equity difference shows which option leaves you in a better financial position.

Yes, most leases include a purchase option at the end of the term for the residual value. If the car's market value exceeds the residual, buying it can be a good deal. If not, you can simply return it and walk away.

Leasing disadvantages include mileage limits (typically 10,000-15,000 miles/year), no equity at the end, potential fees for excess wear and tear, and early termination penalties. You also never stop making payments if you keep leasing.
