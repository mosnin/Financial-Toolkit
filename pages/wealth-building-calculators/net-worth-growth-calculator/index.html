Net Worth Growth Formula

This calculator applies a combined growth model that accounts for both investment returns and asset appreciation:

Net Worth Projection Formula
NW(t) = NW₀ × (1 + rtotal)t + S × [((1 + rtotal)t − 1) / rtotal]

Where NW₀ is your current net worth, rtotal is the blended annual growth rate (investment return + asset appreciation), S is annual savings, and t is the number of years. The combined rate is calculated as rtotal = rinvest + rappreciation (simplified additive model for illustrative projections).

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current net worth — total assets minus total liabilities.
  2. Enter your annual savings added — new money you contribute each year.
  3. Enter your annual investment return — expected return on your investment portfolio (historically ~7% for diversified stock portfolios).
  4. Enter your asset appreciation rate — the expected annual growth of non-investment assets like real estate (typically 2–4%).
  5. Enter the number of years — your projection horizon.
  6. Click "Calculate Growth" to see projections at 5, 10, 20, and 30 years, along with wealth milestones.

Example Scenario

Consider a 35-year-old with a net worth of $75,000 who saves $15,000 per year, earns a 7% investment return, and has assets appreciating at 3%. Their projected net worth reaches approximately $166,000 at 5 years, $374,000 at 10 years, $1.25 million at 20 years, and nearly $3.8 million at 30 years — demonstrating how consistent saving combined with compounding growth can build substantial wealth over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Net worth is the total value of all your assets (savings, investments, real estate, vehicles) minus all your liabilities (mortgages, loans, credit card debt). It is the clearest single-number snapshot of your overall financial health.

Asset appreciation — such as real estate or business equity growing in value — compounds alongside investment returns to accelerate net worth growth. Even a modest 3% annual appreciation on a $300,000 home adds nearly $9,000 to net worth in year one alone.

Most financial planners recommend saving 15–20% of gross income. However, those pursuing financial independence or early retirement often aim for 30–50%. The higher your savings rate, the faster your net worth compounds.

Compounding causes exponential rather than linear growth. As your balance grows, each percentage-point return generates a larger dollar gain. A 7% return on $50,000 adds $3,500, but the same return on $500,000 adds $35,000 — ten times as much from the same rate.
