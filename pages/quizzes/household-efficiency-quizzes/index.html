Frequently Asked Questions

According to the U.S. Department of Energy, about 30% of the energy used in homes is wasted through air leaks, outdated appliances, phantom loads, and poor insulation. For the average household spending $2,000/year on energy, that's roughly $600 wasted annually — or $50 per month that could be recovered with targeted upgrades.

Phantom loads (also called standby power) are the electricity drawn by devices that are plugged in but switched off — TVs, game consoles, phone chargers, microwaves, and more. They account for 5–10% of residential electricity use. Use smart power strips, unplug rarely-used devices, and look for Energy Star appliances with low standby ratings to eliminate them.

Many utility companies offer free or subsidised audits, so check your provider's website before paying out of pocket. The payback period is typically under one year.
