Financial Independence Calculator

Financial independence (FI) means having enough invested assets that your portfolio can sustain your lifestyle indefinitely — making work optional. This calculator uses the proven 25x rule (based on the 4% safe withdrawal rate) to compute your FI number, project how many years until you reach it, estimate your FI age, and show the monthly savings required to get there on your timeline.

How Financial Independence Is Calculated

Formula
FI Number = Annual Expenses ÷ Safe Withdrawal Rate
                  (e.g. $60,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,500,000)

Future Value = PV × (1+r)^n + PMT × [((1+r)^n − 1) / r]
Years to FI = solved for n when FV ≥ FI Number

The FI number is derived from the Trinity Study's finding that a 4% initial withdrawal rate, adjusted annually for inflation, has historically sustained a 30-year retirement across virtually all market scenarios. Dividing annual expenses by 0.04 gives you the portfolio size required. The years-to-FI calculation compounds your current savings and monthly contributions at your expected return rate.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter annual retirement expenses: What does your ideal lifestyle cost per year? Include housing, food, travel, healthcare, and leisure.
  2. Add current savings: The total value of all investment accounts (401k, IRA, taxable brokerage).
  3. Set return and withdrawal rate: 7% return and 4% withdrawal are common starting assumptions.
  4. Enter your current age: The calculator will project your FI age and the year you reach independence.
  5. Review results: Adjust expenses, contributions, or return rate to model different scenarios.

Example Scenario

Sam is 30 years old with $45,000 saved, planning for $55,000/year in retirement expenses. Using the 4% SWR, Sam's FI number is $1,375,000. With a 7% annual return and $2,000/month in savings, Sam's portfolio grows to reach the FI number in approximately 22 years — achieving financial independence at age 52. Increasing monthly savings to $2,500 would cut the timeline to about 19 years, reaching FI at age 49.

Frequently Asked Questions

Your FI number is 25 times your annual expenses — the portfolio size that can sustain a 4% annual withdrawal indefinitely. This comes from the Trinity Study showing a 4% initial withdrawal rate historically survived 30-year retirements.

The safe withdrawal rate (SWR) is the annual percentage of your portfolio you can withdraw without running out of money over a 30-year retirement. The 4% rule is most widely cited. Early retirees with longer horizons often use 3% to 3.5% to be more conservative.

Many FI planners use 7% nominal (about 5% real after 2% inflation) based on historical US stock market averages. Using 6% is more conservative. Run multiple scenarios — small differences in return assumptions have large effects on your FI timeline.
