Passive Income Growth Formula

Each year, the portfolio grows by the reinvested portion of passive income, and the following year's income is based on the larger portfolio:

Year-over-Year Portfolio Growth
Portfolio(t) = Portfolio(t−1) × (1 + yield × reinvestRate)
Annual Passive Income at Year t
Income(t) = Portfolio(t) × yield
Effective Growth Rate
reff = yield × reinvestmentRate

Where yield is the annual passive income yield (as a decimal) and reinvestmentRate is the fraction of income that is reinvested. The portfolio compounds at the effective rate each year.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your initial investment — the amount invested in income-generating assets.
  2. Enter the annual passive income yield — the percentage of your portfolio paid out as income each year (e.g., 5% for a 5% dividend yield).
  3. Enter the reinvestment rate — the percentage of income you reinvest (100% for full reinvestment, 0% if you spend all income).
  4. Enter the number of years — your investment horizon.
  5. Click "Calculate Passive Income" to see income at key milestones, total income generated, and the snowball multiplier.

Example Scenario

A $100,000 investment in dividend stocks with a 5% annual yield and 100% reinvestment grows the portfolio to $265,330 after 20 years and generates $13,266 in annual passive income — versus just $5,000 in year one. Total cumulative passive income over those 20 years exceeds $165,000, and the snowball effect produces a 2.65x increase in annual income. Reducing reinvestment to 50% cuts the final passive income to about $7,700 per year — demonstrating the compounding power of reinvestment.

Frequently Asked Questions

The snowball effect occurs when you reinvest your passive income to buy more income-generating assets. The new assets produce more income, which buys even more assets — creating exponential growth over time. A $100,000 investment yielding 5% with full reinvestment can grow to generate $13,000+ per year in passive income by year 20.

This calculator applies to any yield-generating investment: dividend stocks, REITs, bonds, rental property cash flow, peer-to-peer lending, or high-yield savings. The key inputs are the initial investment, the annual yield percentage, and the reinvestment rate.

Use 100% if you plan to reinvest all passive income (e.g., a DRIP program). Use a lower rate (50–75%) if you plan to spend part of the income while reinvesting the rest. The higher the reinvestment rate, the more powerful the snowball effect.

Passive income growth and compound interest are related concepts. In compound interest, returns are automatically added to principal. In passive income reinvestment, your periodic income distribution is used to acquire additional assets, which then generate their own income — the mechanism is the same but framed as a real-world income-building strategy.
