How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the original purchase price (cost basis) of the asset.
  2. Enter the sale price you received.
  3. Select whether the holding period is short-term or long-term.
  4. Enter your annual ordinary income from wages and other sources.
  5. Select your filing status and click Calculate Tax.

Example Scenario

An investor purchased stock for $10,000 and sold it for $35,000 after holding for 2 years. Annual income (single filer): $95,000.

  • Capital Gain: $35,000 − $10,000 = $25,000
  • Total Income: $95,000 + $25,000 = $120,000 — in 15% LTCG bracket
  • Long-Term Capital Gains Tax: $25,000 × 15% = $3,750
  • NIIT: Income below $200,000 threshold — $0
  • Net Proceeds: $35,000 − $10,000 (cost basis) − $3,750 = $21,250

Frequently Asked Questions

Short-term capital gains apply to assets held one year or less and are taxed at ordinary income rates (10%–37%). Long-term capital gains apply to assets held more than one year and are taxed at preferential rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income.

For 2026, long-term capital gains rates are: 0% for income up to approximately $47,025 (single), 15% for income up to $518,900 (single), and 20% above that threshold. Additional 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax may apply at higher incomes.

The NIIT is an additional 3.8% tax on net investment income (including capital gains) for individuals with modified AGI above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).
