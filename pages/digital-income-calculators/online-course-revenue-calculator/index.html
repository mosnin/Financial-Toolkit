How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your audience size — the total number of email subscribers or followers you promote the course to during a launch.
  2. Set your enrollment conversion rate — the percentage of your audience who purchase. Use 1-3% for cold or disengaged lists, 3-8% for warm, engaged audiences.
  3. Enter your course price — the full price or average order value if you offer payment plans.
  4. Set launches per year — how many times you open enrollment. Evergreen funnels can use 12 as a monthly approximation.
  5. Optionally enter your platform fee to see net revenue after transaction costs.
  6. Click Calculate Revenue to see per-launch figures, annual projections, and revenue per subscriber.

Example Scenario

A productivity coach has an email list of 8,000 subscribers and launches a $497 course three times per year with a 2.5% conversion rate and 5% platform fee.

  • Students per launch: 8,000 x 2.5% = 200 students
  • Gross revenue per launch: 200 x $497 = $99,400
  • Annual gross revenue: $99,400 x 3 = $298,200
  • Annual net revenue (5% fee): $298,200 x 0.95 = $283,290
  • Average monthly income: $283,290 / 12 = $23,608/month

Frequently Asked Questions

Typical email list conversion rates for course launches range from 1% to 5%. Warm, engaged audiences with strong pre-launch sequences can achieve 3-8%. Cold traffic from paid ads typically converts at 0.5-2%. Webinar-based launches often achieve higher rates of 5-15% from attendees.

Course pricing depends on the depth of content, your authority in the niche, and the transformation promised. Introductory courses often sell for $47-$197, mid-tier courses $197-$997, and premium programs $1,000-$5,000+. Higher-priced courses with strong outcomes tend to convert just as well as lower-priced ones when marketed correctly.

Most course creators launch 2-4 times per year using cohort or open/close cart models. More frequent launches (6-12x) work well for lower-priced self-paced courses. Evergreen funnels allow continuous enrollment and are popular for automating income. Each approach has different audience fatigue and conversion implications.

Key course business expenses include platform fees (Teachable, Kajabi, Thinkific take 0-10%), payment processing (2-3%), email marketing software, course production costs, advertising spend for each launch, and any affiliate commissions (typically 30-50% of sale price for partner promotions).
