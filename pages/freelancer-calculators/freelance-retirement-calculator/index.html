Retirement Savings Formula

Nest Egg Target (4% Rule)
Nest Egg = Portfolio-Funded Annual Income ÷ Withdrawal Rate
Portfolio-Funded Income
Portfolio Income = Desired Income − Social Security Benefit
Future Value of Current Savings
FV = Current Savings × (1 + r)^n  where r = annual return, n = years
Additional Gap to Fund via Monthly Contributions
Gap = Nest Egg Target − Future Value of Current Savings
Required Monthly Contribution
Monthly = Gap × (r/12) ÷ [(1 + r/12)^(12n) − 1]

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current age and target retirement age to calculate your savings runway.
  2. Set your desired annual retirement income in today's dollars — what you want to live on each year.
  3. Enter your current retirement savings across all accounts (IRAs, Solo 401(k), brokerage, etc.).
  4. Set your expected annual investment return — 7% is a common inflation-adjusted estimate for a diversified portfolio.
  5. Enter your safe withdrawal rate — the 4% rule is standard; use 3-3.5% for a more conservative plan.
  6. Add your estimated Social Security benefit to reduce the portfolio-funded income requirement.
  7. Click Calculate Retirement Plan to see your target nest egg and required monthly savings.

Example Scenario

A 38-year-old freelancer wants $65,000/year in retirement at age 65, has $30,000 saved, expects 7% returns, uses a 4% withdrawal rate, and estimates $15,000/year in Social Security.

  • Portfolio-Funded Income: $65,000 − $15,000 = $50,000/year
  • Nest Egg Target: $50,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,250,000
  • Years to Save: 65 − 38 = 27 years
  • Future Value of Current $30,000 at 7%: $197,300
  • Remaining Gap: $1,250,000 − $197,300 = $1,052,700
  • Required Monthly Savings: approximately $1,118/month

Frequently Asked Questions

Freelancers have access to several retirement accounts: a Solo 401(k) allows contributions up to $69,000 in 2024; a SEP-IRA allows up to 25% of net self-employment income; and a traditional or Roth IRA allows up to $7,000 per year ($8,000 if 50+). The Solo 401(k) and SEP-IRA are typically most advantageous for self-employed individuals.

A common rule of thumb is to save 15-20% of gross income for retirement, but freelancers should save more to compensate for the lack of employer match. Saving 20-25% of gross income is a more appropriate target. Use this calculator to find the exact monthly amount needed to reach your retirement goal.

The 4% rule suggests retirees can withdraw 4% of their portfolio in the first year of retirement and adjust for inflation thereafter with a high probability of not running out of money over a 30-year retirement. To find your target nest egg, divide your desired annual income by 0.04. For example, $60,000/year requires a $1,500,000 portfolio.

Freelancers pay into Social Security via self-employment tax and are eligible for benefits. However, Social Security alone is rarely sufficient for retirement. Factor your estimated benefit (available at ssa.gov) as income that reduces your portfolio-funded requirement, lowering your required nest egg.
