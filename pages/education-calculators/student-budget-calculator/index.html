How the Student Budget Formula Works

This calculator uses straightforward income-versus-expense arithmetic:

Total Monthly Income
Income = Job Income + Financial Aid + Family Support
Total Monthly Expenses
Expenses = Tuition + Housing + Food + Transportation + Books + Entertainment
Monthly Surplus / Deficit
Balance = Total Income - Total Expenses

A positive balance is a surplus (you have money left over). A negative balance is a deficit (you are spending more than you earn and need to cut costs or find additional income).

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly income from all sources: part-time job, financial aid disbursements divided by months, and family contributions.
  2. Enter each expense category as a monthly amount. For tuition, divide your semester bill by the number of months covered.
  3. Click Calculate to see your monthly balance and annual projection.
  4. If the result shows a deficit, identify which expense categories have the most room to cut.

Example Scenario

A sophomore earns $800/month from a part-time job and receives $600/month from financial aid. Monthly expenses include $700 for tuition, $650 for off-campus housing, $300 for food, $150 for transportation, $100 for books, and $150 for entertainment — totaling $2,050. With $1,400 in income, they have a $650 monthly deficit. This exercise reveals they need to either increase income, reduce housing costs by finding a roommate, or apply for additional aid.
