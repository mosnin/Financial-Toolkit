Freelance Rate Calculation Formula

Monthly Revenue Needed
Monthly Revenue = Desired Income + Monthly Expenses
Effective Billable Hours per Month
Billable Hours = (Billable Hours/Week x 4.33 weeks) x (1 - Non-Billable Ratio %)
Minimum Hourly Rate
Minimum Rate = Monthly Revenue Needed / Effective Billable Hours per Month
Recommended Rate (20% buffer)
Recommended = Minimum Rate x 1.20
Annual Revenue Projection
Annual Revenue = Recommended Rate x Effective Billable Hours/Month x 12

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your desired monthly income — the take-home amount you want from your freelance work after expenses.
  2. Enter billable hours per week — be honest about how many hours you can realistically spend doing client work around your main job.
  3. Enter non-billable time ratio — 20–30% is typical; increase this if you are new and spend more time on proposals and learning.
  4. Enter monthly hustle expenses — software, tools, subscriptions, and marketing costs.
  5. Click Calculate to see your minimum rate, recommended rate with buffer, effective billable hours, and annual revenue potential.

Example Scenario

Mia is a graphic designer who wants to earn $1,500/month from her side hustle. She has 10 billable hours/week available, spends 30% on non-billable tasks, and has $100/month in software costs.

  • Monthly revenue needed: $1,500 + $100 = $1,600
  • Available hours: 10 x 4.33 = 43.3 total hours/month
  • Effective billable hours: 43.3 x (1 - 30%) = 30.3 hrs/month
  • Minimum rate: $1,600 / 30.3 = $52.81/hr
  • Recommended rate (20% buffer): $52.81 x 1.20 = $63.37/hr
  • Annual revenue at recommended rate: $63.37 x 30.3 x 12 = $23,034

Mia should charge at least $63/hour to comfortably hit her goals with room for slow months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Non-billable time includes all hours you work that are not directly billed to a client — prospecting, writing proposals, admin, invoicing, learning, and marketing. For side hustle freelancers, 20–40% of working time is typically non-billable.

A 20% buffer above your minimum rate accounts for slow periods, scope creep, late payments, and the self-employment tax burden (approximately 15.3% in the US). Without this cushion, any variation in workload can push you below your income target.

Include software subscriptions, equipment costs amortized monthly, marketing expenses, professional development, insurance if applicable, and any platform or payment processing fees. These are all legitimate business expenses that must be covered by your rate.

Most side hustle freelancers realistically have 5–15 billable hours per week available after a full-time job. When accounting for non-billable time, the actual available hours for client work are often 60–80% of total hours dedicated to the side hustle.
