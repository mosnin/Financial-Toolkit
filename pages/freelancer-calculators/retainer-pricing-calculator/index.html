Retainer Pricing Formula

Full Rate Value
Full Value = Hours / Month × Hourly Rate
Retainer Price (with discount)
Retainer = Full Value × (1 - Discount %)
Annual Retainer Value
Annual = Monthly Retainer × 12
Client ROI
ROI = (Value Delivered - Retainer Cost) / Retainer Cost × 100

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your estimated hours per month for the client engagement and your standard hourly rate.
  2. Set a retainer discount percentage — the loyalty discount you offer for recurring guaranteed work (typically 10-20%).
  3. Select whether this is a strategic (advisory) or tactical (execution) engagement.
  4. Optionally enter the monthly value delivered to show the client their return on investment.
  5. Click Calculate Retainer to see your pricing and the client's ROI.

Example Scenario

A freelance marketing strategist estimates 20 hours/month for a client at their $120/hr rate, offers a 15% retainer discount, and delivers an estimated $15,000/month in marketing value.

  • Full Rate Value: 20 × $120 = $2,400
  • Retainer Price: $2,400 × (1 - 0.15) = $2,040/month
  • Discount Amount: $360/month
  • Annual Retainer Value: $2,040 × 12 = $24,480
  • Client ROI: ($15,000 - $2,040) / $2,040 = 635%

Frequently Asked Questions

A retainer discount of 10-20% is typical. Offering above 25% erodes the income stability benefit. The discount reflects reduced admin overhead, priority scheduling, and guaranteed cash flow rather than just reduced hourly revenue.

Strategic retainers involve advisory work, consulting, or ongoing strategy direction. Tactical retainers cover recurring execution like content, code maintenance, or design. Strategic retainers typically command premium rates due to their higher business impact.

Divide the value delivered minus retainer cost by retainer cost. If your work drives $20,000 in client revenue for a $2,000 retainer, ROI is 900%. This framing makes retainers easier to justify and renew at increasing rates.

Most freelancers use a 'use it or lose it' policy to protect time and income predictability. If you do offer rollover, cap it at 25% of monthly hours with a one-month expiry. Avoid unlimited rollover which creates unpredictable workload spikes.
