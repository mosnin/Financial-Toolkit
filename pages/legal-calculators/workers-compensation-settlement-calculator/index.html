Workers Comp Settlement Formula

Workers compensation settlements are generally calculated using the following approach:

Settlement Formula
Settlement = (2/3 x Weekly Wage x Disability Rating x Weeks) + Medical Costs

The weekly benefit rate is typically two-thirds of your average weekly wage. This is multiplied by the disability rating (as a percentage) and the number of weeks of disability. Outstanding medical costs are then added to arrive at the total estimated settlement.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your average weekly wage - your gross weekly earnings before the injury.
  2. Enter your disability rating - the percentage assigned by your treating physician.
  3. Enter weeks of disability - how many weeks you have been or expect to be disabled.
  4. Enter total medical costs - all medical expenses related to the workplace injury.
  5. Click "Calculate Settlement" to see your estimated workers comp settlement value.

Example Scenario

A worker earning $1,000 per week suffers a back injury with a 25% disability rating and is disabled for 26 weeks. Their medical bills total $10,000. The weekly benefit rate is $667 (2/3 of $1,000). Disability benefits are $667 x 0.25 x 26 = $4,333. Adding $10,000 in medical costs gives an estimated settlement of approximately $14,333.

Frequently Asked Questions

Workers comp settlements are typically based on your average weekly wage, the disability rating assigned by a doctor, the number of weeks of disability benefits, and any outstanding medical costs. The formula generally calculates two-thirds of your weekly wage multiplied by the disability rating and weeks of disability, plus medical expenses.

A disability rating is a percentage assigned by a medical professional that represents the degree of permanent impairment you have suffered from a workplace injury. A higher rating means greater impairment and typically a larger settlement.

Workers compensation settlements can take anywhere from a few weeks to several months, depending on the complexity of the case, whether the employer or insurer disputes the claim, and whether you have reached maximum medical improvement.

In most cases, workers compensation benefits and settlements are not subject to federal or state income taxes. However, if you also receive Social Security disability benefits, a portion may become taxable.

While not always required, hiring a workers compensation attorney is advisable if your claim is denied, your employer disputes your injury, you have a permanent disability, or the settlement offer seems too low. Attorneys typically work on contingency for workers comp cases.
