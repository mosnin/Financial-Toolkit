How Federal Income Tax Is Calculated

Federal income tax uses a progressive bracket system. Your income is not taxed at a single rate; instead, different portions of your income are taxed at increasing rates as you move through each bracket.

Tax Calculation
Taxable Income = Gross Income - Deductions
Tax = Sum of (Income in each bracket x Bracket rate)

For example, a single filer with $100,000 in taxable income pays 10% on the first $11,600, 12% on income from $11,601 to $47,150, 22% on income from $47,151 to $100,525, and so on through each applicable bracket.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your filing status - Single, Married Filing Jointly, or Head of Household.
  2. Enter your gross annual income - your total income before any deductions.
  3. Enter your deductions - enter your itemized deductions, or leave at 0 to use the standard deduction for your filing status.
  4. Click "Calculate Tax" to see your taxable income, estimated tax, effective rate, and marginal rate.

Example Scenario

A single filer earning $85,000 per year who takes the standard deduction of $14,600 has a taxable income of $70,400. Their federal tax would be approximately $11,028, giving them an effective tax rate of about 12.97% and a marginal rate of 22%.

Frequently Asked Questions

For 2024, the federal tax brackets for single filers are: 10% up to $11,600, 12% up to $47,150, 22% up to $100,525, 24% up to $191,950, 32% up to $243,725, 35% up to $609,350, and 37% above $609,350. Married filing jointly brackets are roughly double these amounts.

Your marginal tax rate is the rate applied to your last dollar of income (your highest bracket). Your effective tax rate is the total tax divided by your total taxable income, representing the average rate you actually pay across all brackets.

For 2024, the standard deduction is $14,600 for single filers, $29,200 for married filing jointly, and $21,900 for head of household.

You should itemize if your total itemized deductions (mortgage interest, state and local taxes, charitable contributions, etc.) exceed the standard deduction for your filing status. Otherwise, the standard deduction will lower your tax bill more.
