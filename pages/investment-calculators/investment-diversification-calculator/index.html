How the Diversification Score Is Calculated

The diversification score (0–100) is computed from three factors: the number of holdings, sector concentration, and correlation level:

Holdings Score
Holdings Score = min(number of holdings / 30, 1) × 40 points
Sector Score
Sector Score = max(1 − largest sector weight / 100, 0) × 40 points
Correlation Score
Low = 20 pts  |  Medium = 10 pts  |  High = 0 pts

The total diversification score is the sum of all three components out of 100.

How to Use This Diversification Calculator

  1. Enter number of holdings — the total number of individual stocks, funds, or assets in your portfolio.
  2. Enter largest sector weight % — the percentage of your portfolio in your most concentrated sector.
  3. Select correlation estimate — how similarly your holdings tend to move together.
  4. Click "Calculate Diversification" to get your score, concentration risk level, and a personalized recommendation.

Example Scenario

Suppose you hold 15 stocks with 40% of your portfolio in technology and medium correlation between holdings. The holdings score would be 20 points (15/30 × 40), the sector score would be 24 points (60% × 40), and the correlation score would be 10 points, giving a total diversification score of 54 out of 100 — a moderate level. The recommendation would be to reduce your technology concentration and consider adding uncorrelated assets like bonds or international equities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Investment diversification is the practice of spreading investments across different assets, sectors, and geographies to reduce risk. The goal is to limit the impact of any single investment's poor performance on your overall portfolio.

Research suggests that 20–30 uncorrelated stocks can eliminate most unsystematic (company-specific) risk. However, diversification also depends on sector spread and correlation. Holding 30 technology stocks is not as diversified as holding 15 stocks across different sectors.

Concentration risk is the potential for large losses resulting from having too much of your portfolio in a single stock, sector, or asset class. A common guideline is to keep no single position above 5–10% of your total portfolio.

Correlation measures how closely two assets move together. A correlation of +1 means they move in perfect lockstep; -1 means they move in opposite directions. True diversification requires holding assets with low or negative correlations so that losses in one area are offset by stability or gains in another.
